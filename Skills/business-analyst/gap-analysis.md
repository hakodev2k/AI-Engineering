# Gap Analysis

## Purpose
Compare current and target states to identify missing capabilities, process changes, data changes, controls, and transition work.

## When to use
Use for transformation, system replacement, operating-model change, compliance remediation, and migration planning.

## Inputs
As-is state, target outcomes, target process or capability model, constraints, known pain points, and strategic goals.

## Preconditions
Both current and desired states are defined well enough for comparison.

## Context to inspect
Processes, roles, systems, data, policies, integrations, controls, metrics, skills, and operating procedures.

## Core knowledge
A gap is not automatically a requirement. Senior BAs distinguish capability gaps from intentional differences and prioritize gaps by business impact and dependency.

## Procedure
1. Define comparison dimensions.
2. Establish evidence for the current state.
3. Define target-state capabilities and outcomes.
4. Compare each dimension systematically.
5. Classify gaps by process, people, data, technology, policy, or control.
6. Identify dependencies and root causes.
7. Assess business impact and urgency.
8. Convert actionable gaps into traceable requirements or change actions.
9. Identify gaps intentionally accepted.
10. Validate with accountable owners.

## Decision points
Prioritize gaps that block target outcomes or create material risk; defer cosmetic or low-value differences unless required by policy.

## Common failure patterns
Treating every difference as a defect, relying on undocumented assumptions about current state, and omitting non-technology gaps.

## Verification
Confirm each gap has evidence, target-state rationale, impact, owner, and disposition.

## Expected output
A prioritized gap register linked to requirements, risks, and transition activities.

## Stop conditions
Stop when current-state evidence is unreliable or the target state is not sufficiently defined.
