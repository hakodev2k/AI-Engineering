# FinOps Operating Model

## Purpose
Establish recurring ownership, decision rights, workflows, and collaboration between engineering, finance, product, procurement, and leadership for cloud economics.

## When to use
Use when FinOps is being introduced, responsibilities are unclear, optimization stalls between teams, or reporting has no accountable action loop.

## Inputs
Organization structure, cloud ownership, finance processes, engineering teams, procurement responsibilities, existing governance, maturity constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect who controls architecture, budgets, contracts, resource provisioning, allocation metadata, forecasting, and risk acceptance.

## Core knowledge
FinOps is a cross-functional operating practice. Central teams can provide data, standards, and expertise, but workload owners must participate in economic decisions. Decision latency matters.

## Procedure
1. Identify key cloud-economic decisions and current owners.
2. Map responsibilities across engineering, finance, product, procurement, and platform teams.
3. Define decision rights for budgets, commitments, optimization, allocation, and exceptions.
4. Establish recurring review cadences appropriate to volatility.
5. Define common data and metric definitions.
6. Create workflows from insight to owned action to verification.
7. Set escalation paths for unresolved cost/reliability trade-offs.
8. Automate repetitive reporting and controls.
9. Measure participation, action completion, forecast accuracy, and realized value.
10. Evolve responsibilities as maturity increases.

## Decision points
Centralize standards and shared expertise; federate workload decisions to teams with context. Use chargeback only when allocation quality and organizational incentives are ready.

## Common failure patterns
A FinOps team owning all savings, finance-only cost targets, dashboards without actions, unclear commitment authority, and optimization incentives that punish healthy growth.

## Verification
Every material workflow has an accountable owner; recurring reviews produce tracked actions; metric definitions are shared; decisions occur within expected cadence.

## Expected output
A RACI/decision-rights model, operating cadence, workflows, escalation paths, and maturity measures.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when executive sponsorship or financial decision authority is required but undefined.
