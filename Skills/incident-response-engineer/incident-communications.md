# Incident Communications

## Purpose
Provide accurate, audience-appropriate incident updates that support decisions without creating noise, speculation, or false confidence.

## When to use
Use throughout material incidents for responders, leadership, support teams, customers, vendors, and other stakeholders as required.

## Inputs
Verified impact, severity, timeline, mitigation status, uncertainty, next actions, update cadence, and audience needs.

## Context to inspect
Inspect communication obligations, status-page rules, customer commitments, confidentiality constraints, and escalation channels.

## Core knowledge
Good incident communication separates facts, hypotheses, actions, and unknowns. Consistency matters more than excessive detail. Update cadence should reflect severity and stakeholder decision needs.

## Procedure
1. Identify audiences and what decisions each must make.
2. State current impact in plain language.
3. Include start time or detection time when known.
4. Distinguish verified facts from investigation hypotheses.
5. State mitigation actions and observed results.
6. Describe relevant uncertainty without speculation.
7. Provide the next update time or trigger.
8. Keep one authoritative internal status source.
9. Correct prior information explicitly when evidence changes.
10. Close with recovery status and follow-up expectations.

## Decision points
Use technical detail for responders and outcome-focused language for business/customer audiences. Communicate early when impact is material even if root cause is unknown.

## Common failure patterns
Speculating about root cause, saying resolved before verification, inconsistent messages across channels, excessive jargon, and long silence while investigation continues.

## Verification
Check every factual statement against the incident record and ensure promised update cadence is being met.

## Expected output
Concise, timestamped status updates appropriate to each audience.

## Stop conditions
Escalate communications involving legal admissions, regulated disclosures, sensitive security details, or contractual notifications requiring authorized review.
