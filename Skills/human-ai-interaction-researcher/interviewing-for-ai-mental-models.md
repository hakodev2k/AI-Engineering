# Interviewing for AI Mental Models

## Purpose
Elicit how users understand an AI system's capabilities, limits, causes, and appropriate use without teaching them the researcher's preferred model during the interview.

## When to use
Use when studying expectations, trust, adoption, explanation needs, error interpretation, learning, or differences between intended and actual system understanding.

## Inputs
Research questions, interaction experience to discuss, target participants, system behavior, known misconceptions, and interview duration.

## Context to inspect
Inspect product language, onboarding, model disclosures, error states, prior interactions, and claims made by marketing or documentation that may shape expectations.

## Core knowledge
Mental models are partial, task-specific explanations users employ to predict behavior. Stated beliefs may differ from operational beliefs revealed by predictions and actions. AI anthropomorphism, automation bias, and opaque system boundaries can distort expectations.

## Procedure
1. Define which predictions or decisions the mental model should support.
2. Prepare neutral prompts about what participants think the system does and why.
3. Ask participants to predict behavior before revealing outcomes when feasible.
4. Probe perceived capability boundaries, data sources, memory, agency, and failure causes.
5. Ask for concrete examples rather than abstract opinions.
6. Use counterexamples to test model stability without correcting participants prematurely.
7. Explore how confidence changes after success and failure.
8. Distinguish beliefs about the interface, underlying model, connected tools, and organization.
9. Capture language participants naturally use for system concepts.
10. Debrief only after relevant observations are complete.
11. Code recurring mental-model patterns and connect them to behavior or risk.

## Decision points
Use prediction tasks when verbal explanations are unreliable. Use interviews alone for exploratory understanding, but combine with behavioral observation when decisions depend on actual reliance or verification behavior.

## Common failure patterns
Explaining the system before eliciting beliefs, asking 'do you trust it?', accepting anthropomorphic language as literal belief, overgeneralizing from one task, and treating misconceptions as user deficits rather than design signals.

## Verification
Findings should include behavioral or predictive evidence where possible and distinguish participant statements from researcher interpretation.

## Expected output
A set of mental-model patterns, associated behaviors, misconceptions, design implications, and evidence excerpts or coded observations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when participants lack enough exposure to form relevant beliefs, the interview would reveal confidential system internals, or the research question actually requires performance evidence rather than perceptions.
