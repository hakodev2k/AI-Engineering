# Longitudinal Adoption and Learning

## Purpose
Study how AI use changes over time as users learn capabilities, develop routines, encounter failures, adapt work practices, or abandon the system.

## When to use
Use when novelty effects, learning curves, workflow integration, trust evolution, habit formation, or sustained value cannot be understood in a single session.

## Inputs
Target users, deployment period, usage telemetry, research questions, product changes, system versions, and retention constraints.

## Context to inspect
Review onboarding, feature releases, model changes, usage patterns, support issues, organizational incentives, and baseline workflow before adoption.

## Core knowledge
Initial enthusiasm and first-use usability do not predict sustained value. Longitudinal research must distinguish user learning from system changes and survivorship effects from true improvement.

## Procedure
1. Define baseline workflow and pre-adoption measures.
2. Select a period long enough to observe meaningful learning or attrition.
3. Establish recurring qualitative and behavioral checkpoints.
4. Record model, feature, policy, and organizational changes during the study.
5. Track task mix, frequency, success, verification, workarounds, and abandonment.
6. Collect critical incidents soon after they occur.
7. Examine how mental models and reliance evolve.
8. Identify users who reduce or stop use and actively study their reasons.
9. Compare novice, adapting, expert, and lapsed-use phases.
10. Separate learning effects from changes in system capability.
11. Synthesize stable patterns and transient novelty effects.

## Decision points
Use diary methods for contextual incidents, interviews for evolving interpretation, and telemetry for behavior frequency. Increase checkpoint frequency during onboarding and major system changes.

## Common failure patterns
Studying only retained users, attributing improvement to learning when the model changed, excessive diary burden, ignoring workflow substitution, and measuring usage as value.

## Verification
Ensure major system changes are timestamped against participant data and attrition is analyzed rather than silently removed.

## Expected output
A longitudinal adoption model describing learning stages, sustained value, failure accumulation, workarounds, attrition drivers, and product implications.

## Stop conditions
Stop when system changes make periods incomparable without adjustment, participant burden becomes excessive, or longitudinal data retention violates approved privacy constraints.
