# Field Research and Contextual Inquiry

## Purpose
Understand AI use within real work environments, social arrangements, tools, interruptions, incentives, and constraints that laboratory studies often remove.

## When to use
Use when workflow context, collaboration, physical environment, organizational policy, or downstream consequences materially shape AI use.

## Inputs
Research questions, target setting, participant roles, current workflow, access permissions, privacy plan, and observation scope.

## Context to inspect
Inspect existing tools, artifacts, handoffs, communication channels, environmental constraints, policy, accountability, and unofficial workarounds.

## Core knowledge
Situated work differs from described work. Contextual inquiry combines observation with lightweight probing while preserving task flow. AI adoption may redistribute work across roles and create hidden verification or coordination costs.

## Procedure
1. Define contextual phenomena that cannot be answered reliably in a lab.
2. Obtain appropriate access and consent for people, artifacts, and environments.
3. Observe current workflow before focusing on the AI feature.
4. Capture triggers, interruptions, dependencies, handoffs, and exceptions.
5. Ask participants to explain decisions at natural breakpoints.
6. Record how AI outputs move into other tools and decisions.
7. Observe verification, collaboration, escalation, and informal safeguards.
8. Identify mismatches between official process and actual practice.
9. Compare across roles and contexts rather than generalizing one site.
10. Validate interpretations with participants and domain stakeholders.

## Decision points
Prefer direct observation when behavior is routine or tacit; interviews when access is limited; diary methods when critical events are intermittent; shadowing when sequence and coordination matter.

## Common failure patterns
Treating self-report as workflow truth, focusing only on screen interaction, ignoring organizational incentives, recording sensitive material unnecessarily, and assuming one workplace represents the domain.

## Verification
Triangulate observations with artifacts, interviews, or telemetry where appropriate. Clearly separate observed behavior, participant explanation, and researcher inference.

## Expected output
A contextual workflow account with observed practices, constraints, coordination patterns, AI-related adaptations, risks, and design opportunities.

## Stop conditions
Stop when observation would expose unauthorized sensitive information, consent boundaries cannot be maintained, or researcher presence creates unacceptable operational risk.
