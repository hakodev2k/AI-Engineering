# Research Communication and Decision Support

## Purpose
Communicate human-AI research so decision makers understand evidence, uncertainty, trade-offs, and recommended actions without converting nuanced findings into false certainty.

## When to use
Use for readouts, launch reviews, roadmap decisions, design critiques, executive briefings, incident follow-ups, and cross-functional planning.

## Inputs
Research findings, evidence strength, audience, decision deadline, alternatives, risks, constraints, and unresolved questions.

## Context to inspect
Inspect the audience's decision authority, technical background, prior beliefs, incentives, competing evidence, and what actions remain feasible.

## Core knowledge
Effective research communication is decision-centered. Senior researchers distinguish observation, interpretation, inference, recommendation, and speculation. Visuals and quotes should clarify evidence rather than manufacture confidence.

## Procedure
1. State the decision and why the evidence matters.
2. Lead with the most consequential findings rather than study chronology.
3. For each finding, state affected users/tasks, evidence, confidence, and consequence.
4. Separate facts from interpretation and recommendation.
5. Show important contradictions and limitations explicitly.
6. Quantify magnitude and uncertainty when supported.
7. Present alternatives and trade-offs rather than a single unexplained preference.
8. Identify launch blockers, mitigations, follow-up research, and owners where appropriate.
9. Tailor depth to the audience while preserving essential caveats.
10. Capture decisions made and evidence gaps that remain.

## Decision points
Use concise briefs for time-sensitive decisions, detailed reports for durable reference, and live workshops when trade-offs require cross-functional negotiation. Escalate severe risks rather than burying them in appendices.

## Common failure patterns
Research theater, excessive methodology detail before implications, cherry-picked quotes, unsupported percentages, hiding uncertainty, presenting recommendations as findings, and failing to document the resulting decision.

## Verification
A decision maker should be able to restate the key evidence, uncertainty, alternatives, and required action accurately after the readout.

## Expected output
A decision-ready research communication artifact with evidence, confidence, implications, trade-offs, recommendations, risks, and unresolved questions.

## Stop conditions
Stop when the evidence does not support the requested claim, stakeholders ask to remove material limitations, or a safety-critical finding lacks appropriate escalation.
