# Multi-User and Collaborative AI Research

## Purpose
Study how AI changes coordination, authorship, shared understanding, handoffs, conflict resolution, and accountability when multiple people collaborate around the same system or artifact.

## When to use
Use for team copilots, shared agents, collaborative editors, enterprise assistants, review workflows, and AI-mediated group decisions.

## Inputs
Team roles, workflow, shared artifacts, AI permissions, communication channels, authorship model, task goals, and organizational constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect who can prompt, view context, approve actions, edit outputs, see provenance, change agent settings, and access shared history. Review handoffs and accountability rules.

## Core knowledge
AI can alter team power and coordination by centralizing information, obscuring authorship, creating asymmetric context, or accelerating output faster than teams can align. Individual usability does not predict team effectiveness.

## Procedure
1. Map roles, responsibilities, information access, and handoffs.
2. Identify where AI participates in shared decisions or artifacts.
3. Observe teams performing realistic collaborative tasks.
4. Track who initiates, accepts, edits, verifies, and attributes AI contributions.
5. Examine whether members share the same understanding of AI state and capability.
6. Test conflicting instructions, concurrent edits, and role-based permissions.
7. Observe disagreement resolution and escalation around AI output.
8. Evaluate provenance and accountability after handoffs.
9. Measure coordination cost, duplication, omissions, and decision quality.
10. Recommend shared-state, permission, provenance, or workflow changes.

## Decision points
Use shared AI state when collaboration benefits from common context; private assistance when confidentiality or independent judgment matters. Require explicit ownership for consequential shared actions.

## Common failure patterns
Testing individuals and inferring team behavior, unclear authorship, hidden private context affecting shared outputs, permission leakage, duplicated AI work, and no owner for verification.

## Verification
Teams should be able to reconstruct who did what, understand current AI state, and complete handoffs without losing critical context or accountability.

## Expected output
A collaborative interaction assessment with coordination patterns, shared-state failures, permission risks, authorship issues, and design recommendations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when study access would expose unauthorized team information, role permissions cannot be safely simulated, or accountability for shared high-impact actions is undefined.
