# Mixed-Methods AI Product Research

## Purpose
Combine qualitative and quantitative evidence so each method addresses a defined uncertainty and the synthesis produces stronger product decisions than either source alone.

## When to use
Use when teams need both behavioral scale and explanatory depth, especially for adoption, failure analysis, trust calibration, workflow impact, or product evaluation.

## Inputs
Research questions, telemetry, qualitative data sources, model evaluations, product metrics, user segments, and decision requirements.

## Context to inspect
Review metric definitions, event instrumentation, sampling frames, study protocols, model versions, release history, and known data-quality limitations.

## Core knowledge
Mixed methods is not simply collecting interviews plus analytics. Designs may be convergent, explanatory sequential, or exploratory sequential. Integration requires compatible units, time periods, populations, and constructs.

## Procedure
1. Define what uncertainty each method will address.
2. Choose an integration design: convergent, quantitative-to-qualitative, or qualitative-to-quantitative.
3. Align populations, time windows, system versions, and construct definitions.
4. Assess instrumentation and sampling bias before combining evidence.
5. Analyze each evidence source using appropriate methods independently.
6. Build a joint display linking quantitative patterns to qualitative explanations.
7. Investigate contradictions rather than averaging them away.
8. Segment where aggregate metrics hide materially different experiences.
9. Distinguish correlation, explanation, and causal evidence.
10. Translate convergent and divergent findings into decisions and follow-up questions.

## Decision points
Start quantitative when a known pattern needs explanation; start qualitative when constructs or categories are not yet known; run convergently when independent corroboration is valuable.

## Common failure patterns
Using quotes to decorate metrics, incompatible samples, mismatched time periods, metric definitions that do not reflect user value, cherry-picking anecdotes, and claiming causality from triangulation alone.

## Verification
Confirm every integrated claim is traceable to its evidence sources and that contradictions, missingness, and population differences are visible in the synthesis.

## Expected output
An integrated evidence report with joint displays, convergent findings, contradictions, uncertainty, segment differences, and decision implications.

## Stop conditions
Stop when datasets refer to materially different systems or populations without a defensible bridge, or when data quality prevents valid integration.
