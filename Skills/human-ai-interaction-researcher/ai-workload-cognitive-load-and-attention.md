# AI Workload, Cognitive Load, and Attention

## Purpose
Evaluate whether AI reduces meaningful work or merely shifts burden into prompting, monitoring, verification, comparison, and exception handling.

## When to use
Use for copilots, decision support, high-volume review, multi-agent supervision, alerting, and workflows where attention is a scarce resource.

## Inputs
Task workflow, AI interaction, user roles, time pressure, error consequences, workload measures, and baseline process.

## Context to inspect
Inspect task switching, notification frequency, output volume, verification requirements, parallel tasks, latency, interruptions, and current manual workload.

## Core knowledge
Automation can create out-of-the-loop effects, vigilance burden, decision fatigue, and attention fragmentation. Faster generation may increase total review load. Subjective workload should be interpreted alongside task performance and observed behavior.

## Procedure
1. Establish baseline task demands without the proposed AI change where feasible.
2. Map new cognitive activities introduced by AI.
3. Identify attention-critical moments and competing tasks.
4. Observe users under realistic volume and time pressure.
5. Measure task success, time, interruptions, review effort, missed signals, and recovery.
6. Use validated workload measures when appropriate, without relying on them alone.
7. Test long sessions or repeated decisions when fatigue is plausible.
8. Examine whether batching, prioritization, summaries, or automation reduce or shift burden.
9. Segment by expertise and task complexity.
10. Recommend changes to information density, timing, automation level, and review workflow.

## Decision points
Automate routine low-risk work when monitoring cost remains low; batch low-urgency information; interrupt for high-severity time-sensitive events; preserve drill-down for verification without forcing constant detail.

## Common failure patterns
Measuring only task time, assuming generated output is free to review, testing one action instead of sustained workload, excessive alerts, and increasing automation while leaving users accountable for impossible monitoring.

## Verification
Compare workload and outcome evidence before and after proposed changes, including sustained-use scenarios where relevant.

## Expected output
A workload and attention assessment with burden sources, performance effects, failure risks, and design recommendations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when the study would impose unsafe workload, baseline comparison is impossible and required for the claim, or participant fatigue invalidates continued data collection.
