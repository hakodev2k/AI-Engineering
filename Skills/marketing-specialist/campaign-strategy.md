# Campaign Strategy

## Purpose
Design integrated campaigns that connect a defined business outcome to audience, insight, proposition, channels, creative, budget, and measurement.

## When to use
Use for acquisition, launch, demand generation, activation, retention, re-engagement, or brand campaigns with meaningful investment.

## Inputs
Objective, audience, customer insight, offer, budget, timing, channel options, creative resources, historical performance, and constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect funnel bottlenecks, baseline metrics, audience saturation, previous campaign learnings, channel attribution limits, seasonality, brand guidance, and operational capacity.

## Core knowledge
Start with the behavior or business outcome, not channel tactics. Distinguish strategy from execution. Campaigns need a coherent hypothesis connecting audience insight to message and action.

## Procedure
1. Define one primary objective and decision metric.
2. Establish baseline and target.
3. Identify priority audience and behavioral barrier.
4. Form a campaign hypothesis.
5. Select proposition, offer, and proof.
6. Map channel roles across the journey.
7. Define creative territories and required variants.
8. Allocate budget using expected marginal value and learning needs.
9. Design experiments and holdouts where feasible.
10. Specify tracking, reporting, optimization rules, and stop criteria.
11. Launch with quality assurance.
12. Review incremental results and capture reusable learning.

## Decision points
Optimize for reach when penetration is the constraint, frequency when recall is insufficient, conversion when intent exists, and retention when acquisition quality is weak. Avoid optimizing a local channel metric that harms the full funnel.

## Common failure patterns
Multiple competing objectives, channel-first planning, weak audience insight, no baseline, changing variables simultaneously, attribution overconfidence, and endless optimization without stop rules.

## Verification
Confirm the campaign has a testable hypothesis, tracking is validated, spend reconciles, downstream quality is monitored, and conclusions separate correlation from causality.

## Expected output
A campaign brief with objective, audience, insight, proposition, channel roles, budget, experiments, measurement plan, risks, and optimization rules.

## Stop conditions
Stop when tracking is materially broken, the offer cannot be fulfilled, claims are unapproved, or spend exceeds authorized limits.
