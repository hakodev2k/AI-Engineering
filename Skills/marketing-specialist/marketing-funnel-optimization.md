# Marketing Funnel Optimization

## Purpose
Diagnose and improve customer progression across awareness, consideration, conversion, activation, retention, and expansion without optimizing one stage at the expense of the system.

## When to use
Use when growth stalls, conversion drops, lead quality is poor, acquisition costs rise, or teams disagree about the main funnel constraint.

## Inputs
Funnel definitions, event data, cohort data, channel data, customer research, sales outcomes, retention data, and operational constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect event instrumentation, stage definitions, time-to-convert, cohort behavior, segment differences, leakage points, lead quality, activation, churn, and attribution limitations.

## Core knowledge
Funnels are simplified models of non-linear journeys. Optimize the constraint that limits valuable outcomes. Stage conversion must be interpreted with volume, quality, lag, and downstream value.

## Procedure
1. Define the valuable end outcome and funnel stages.
2. Validate event and identity tracking.
3. Establish baseline volumes, conversion rates, latency, and value by cohort.
4. Segment results by meaningful dimensions.
5. Identify the largest economically relevant constraint.
6. Combine quantitative evidence with customer research to form causes.
7. Prioritize interventions by expected impact, confidence, effort, and risk.
8. Test changes with guardrail metrics.
9. Monitor downstream quality and delayed effects.
10. Document learning and update the funnel model.

## Decision points
Fix measurement before optimization when data is unreliable. Improve acquisition quality rather than landing-page conversion when downstream retention is weak. Remove friction only when it does not protect trust, qualification, or compliance.

## Common failure patterns
Optimizing percentages without volume, ignoring cohorts, treating all leads equally, confusing correlation with cause, measuring only first conversion, and hiding poor retention behind acquisition growth.

## Verification
Confirm instrumentation integrity, experiment validity, downstream guardrails, and sustained improvement over an appropriate observation window.

## Expected output
A prioritized funnel diagnosis, evidence-backed hypotheses, experiments, results, and next decisions.

## Stop conditions
Stop when event data is materially inconsistent, sample size cannot support the conclusion, or proposed changes create unacceptable compliance or customer-risk exposure.
