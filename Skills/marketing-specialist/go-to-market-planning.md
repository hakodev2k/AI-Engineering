# Go-to-Market Planning

## Purpose
Coordinate audience, offer, positioning, channels, sales readiness, launch sequencing, measurement, and feedback into an executable market-entry plan.

## When to use
Use for product launches, major feature launches, new geography or segment entry, pricing changes, and material repositioning.

## Inputs
Business goals, product readiness, target segments, positioning, pricing, budget, channel capabilities, sales model, operational constraints, and launch date.

## Context to inspect
Review customer evidence, product dependencies, channel economics, sales capacity, support readiness, legal constraints, analytics instrumentation, and previous launches.

## Core knowledge
A GTM plan is a cross-functional operating model, not a campaign calendar. Distribution, product readiness, sales motion, customer success, and measurement must support the same market hypothesis.

## Procedure
1. Define launch objective and success criteria.
2. Select target segments and use cases.
3. Validate positioning, offer, and proof.
4. Define customer acquisition and sales motions.
5. Choose channels based on audience reach and economics.
6. Map the journey from awareness through adoption and retention.
7. Identify launch dependencies and owners.
8. Prepare sales, support, partner, and customer-success enablement.
9. Establish instrumentation, leading indicators, and decision thresholds.
10. Sequence pre-launch, launch, and post-launch activities.
11. Run readiness review and risk scenarios.
12. Operate a feedback loop and adjust based on evidence.

## Decision points
Use a phased launch when uncertainty or operational risk is high. Concentrate channels when learning speed matters; diversify when a proven motion risks concentration dependence.

## Common failure patterns
Marketing-only launch plans, vanity launch metrics, no post-launch ownership, broad targeting, untested claims, missing onboarding, and spending before instrumentation works.

## Verification
Confirm dependencies are owned, tracking works end to end, customer-facing teams are ready, launch criteria are measurable, and rollback or adjustment paths exist.

## Expected output
An executable GTM plan with audience, proposition, channels, milestones, owners, dependencies, risks, metrics, and feedback cadence.

## Stop conditions
Stop when the product is materially unready, critical legal or operational approvals are missing, or the market hypothesis lacks minimum evidence.
