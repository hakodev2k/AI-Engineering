# Customer Retention Marketing

## Purpose
Reduce avoidable churn and increase durable customer value through relevant lifecycle interventions grounded in customer behavior and product value.

## When to use
Use when retention, repeat purchase, renewal, engagement, or expansion materially affects business economics.

## Inputs
Cohort retention, churn reasons, usage or purchase behavior, customer research, support data, lifecycle events, value metrics, and communication channels.

## Context to inspect
Inspect retention curves, activation milestones, segment differences, involuntary churn, product issues, customer complaints, subscription terms, messaging frequency, and existing save offers.

## Core knowledge
Marketing cannot permanently compensate for weak product value. Retention work should identify why valuable customers disengage and intervene at moments where communication, education, service, or offers can change outcomes.

## Procedure
1. Define retention outcome and observation window.
2. Segment cohorts by value, acquisition source, behavior, and lifecycle state.
3. Identify churn timing and leading signals.
4. Combine behavioral data with qualitative reasons.
5. Separate product, service, price, payment, and communication causes.
6. Prioritize addressable causes.
7. Design interventions such as onboarding, education, reminders, service recovery, or targeted offers.
8. Include untreated controls where feasible.
9. Measure incremental retention and downstream margin.
10. Monitor unintended discount dependence or fatigue.
11. Feed structural causes back to product and operations.

## Decision points
Use incentives when price or timing is genuinely causal; avoid discounts when they merely subsidize customers who would stay. Prefer product or service fixes for structural churn.

## Common failure patterns
Treating all churn equally, blanket discounts, measuring clicks instead of retention, ignoring involuntary churn, over-messaging at-risk users, and claiming credit for naturally high-retention customers.

## Verification
Compare cohorts or holdouts, track sustained retention beyond the intervention window, inspect margin impact, and verify churn reasons change as expected.

## Expected output
A retention diagnosis, prioritized interventions, targeting rules, experiments, economics, and cross-functional recommendations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when intervention requires misleading dark patterns, violates cancellation rights, or the root cause requires product or service remediation outside marketing authority.
