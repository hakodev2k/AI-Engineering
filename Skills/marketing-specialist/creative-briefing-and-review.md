# Creative Briefing and Review

## Purpose
Translate strategy into clear creative direction and evaluate work against customer insight, communication goals, channel context, brand, accessibility, and evidence.

## When to use
Use for campaigns, ads, landing experiences, video, social content, launch assets, and other material creative investments.

## Inputs
Campaign objective, audience, insight, positioning, offer, proof, channel, required formats, brand guidance, constraints, and measurement plan.

## Context to inspect
Inspect prior creative performance, customer language, competitive conventions, channel specifications, accessibility requirements, legal claim constraints, and production timelines.

## Core knowledge
A creative brief should define the problem and boundaries without prescribing every execution detail. Review should distinguish strategic issues from personal taste. Creative quality and media strategy interact; one cannot fully compensate for the other.

## Procedure
1. Define the behavior or perception the creative must influence.
2. Specify the priority audience and relevant insight.
3. State one primary message or proposition.
4. Provide proof and mandatory constraints.
5. Describe channel context and expected attention conditions.
6. Define deliverables and variant needs.
7. Give creators room to solve the communication problem.
8. Review concepts first against strategy, then craft and execution.
9. Consolidate feedback into prioritized actionable points.
10. QA final assets for claims, accessibility, technical specs, links, and tracking.
11. Measure creative performance and feed learning into future briefs.

## Decision points
Reject work that is strategically wrong even if polished; preserve distinctive creative ideas when feedback is merely subjective. Create variants when audiences or placements genuinely require different communication.

## Common failure patterns
Briefs full of background but no decision, multiple primary messages, subjective committee feedback, late mandatory changes, inaccessible assets, unsupported claims, and judging creative without performance context.

## Verification
Confirm stakeholders agree on objective before production, final assets meet specifications, claims have evidence, and creative variants can be measured reliably.

## Expected output
A focused creative brief, review decisions, approved assets, QA evidence, and documented creative learning.

## Stop conditions
Stop approval when material claims lack evidence, required rights are unclear, accessibility defects are unresolved, or production cannot meet essential channel specifications.
