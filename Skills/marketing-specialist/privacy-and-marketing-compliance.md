# Privacy and Marketing Compliance

## Purpose
Design marketing activities that respect consent, privacy, platform rules, truthful claims, customer choice, and applicable organizational controls.

## When to use
Use before collecting customer data, launching tracking, buying audiences, sending communications, running promotions, making material claims, or introducing new marketing technology.

## Inputs
Campaign plan, data collected, purpose, consent mechanism, jurisdictions, communication channels, vendors, claims, retention needs, and internal policies.

## Context to inspect
Inspect data sources, lawful or approved processing basis, consent records, preference controls, vendor terms, retention, access, tracking technologies, audience matching, claim evidence, and required disclosures.

## Core knowledge
Compliance requirements vary by jurisdiction and context; marketers should recognize risk and involve qualified privacy or legal specialists rather than invent legal conclusions. Data minimization, purpose limitation, truthful representation, and usable customer choice are durable principles.

## Procedure
1. Map data collected, source, purpose, destination, and retention.
2. Confirm approved basis and consent requirements with applicable policy or specialists.
3. Minimize fields and tracking to what the use case needs.
4. Verify preference, opt-out, unsubscribe, and suppression behavior.
5. Review vendor data sharing and access boundaries.
6. Substantiate material advertising claims.
7. Check promotion, endorsement, testimonial, and disclosure requirements.
8. QA customer-facing notices and controls.
9. Document approvals and evidence.
10. Monitor complaints, policy changes, and vendor behavior.

## Decision points
Use aggregated or less identifiable data when individual-level data is unnecessary. Do not deploy tracking simply because a platform supports it. Escalate jurisdiction-specific interpretation.

## Common failure patterns
Dark patterns, bundled consent, hidden tracking, stale suppression lists, unsupported claims, collecting data without a defined purpose, retaining data indefinitely, and assuming vendor compliance transfers responsibility.

## Verification
Test consent and opt-out paths, audit tags and data destinations, sample suppression behavior, confirm claim evidence, and retain required approvals.

## Expected output
A marketing control checklist with data map, consent requirements, disclosures, vendor risks, claim evidence, approvals, and monitoring actions.

## Stop conditions
Stop launch when required consent, disclosure, evidence, or specialist approval is missing, or when customer choice cannot be honored reliably.
