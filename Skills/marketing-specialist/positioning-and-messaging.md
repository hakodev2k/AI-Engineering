# Positioning and Messaging

## Purpose
Define why a target customer should choose an offering and translate that strategic position into credible, differentiated messaging.

## When to use
Use for launches, repositioning, new segments, weak conversion, inconsistent communications, or strong competitive pressure.

## Inputs
Customer research, segment priorities, category context, competitor claims, product capabilities, proof points, brand constraints, and sales feedback.

## Context to inspect
Inspect customer language, alternatives, switching triggers, objections, product evidence, competitive parity, existing claims, and funnel performance.

## Core knowledge
Positioning defines the competitive frame, target, differentiated value, and reasons to believe. Messaging expresses that position for specific audiences and stages. Claims must be supportable; clever wording cannot compensate for weak differentiation.

## Procedure
1. Select the priority customer and buying context.
2. Identify the job, problem, and current alternative.
3. Determine attributes customers value and competitors can credibly claim.
4. Choose defensible differentiators.
5. State the value created by those differentiators.
6. Collect proof for each important claim.
7. Build a message hierarchy from core promise to supporting benefits and evidence.
8. Adapt language by audience and funnel stage without changing the underlying position.
9. Test comprehension, relevance, credibility, and differentiation.
10. Document approved claims and usage guidance.

## Decision points
Lead with a category when it accelerates understanding; redefine the frame only when the benefit outweighs education cost. Prefer specific evidence over broad superlatives.

## Common failure patterns
Feature dumping, claiming everything for everyone, copying competitor language, unsupported superiority claims, confusing slogans with positioning, and changing strategy for every channel.

## Verification
Use customer tests, conversion experiments, sales feedback, and competitive review to confirm audiences understand the offer and perceive a relevant difference.

## Expected output
A positioning statement, message hierarchy, proof library, audience adaptations, objection responses, and claim constraints.

## Stop conditions
Stop when product evidence contradicts the desired claim, legal review is required, or the target segment and competitive alternative remain unclear.
