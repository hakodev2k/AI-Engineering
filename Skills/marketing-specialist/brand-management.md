# Brand Management

## Purpose
Protect and grow coherent brand meaning across customer experiences while enabling teams to communicate consistently without unnecessary rigidity.

## When to use
Use for brand development, repositioning, multi-channel campaigns, partnerships, new markets, governance, and when customer perception diverges from intended positioning.

## Inputs
Brand strategy, positioning, audience research, visual and verbal guidance, customer perception data, channel assets, product experience, and business strategy.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current touchpoints, customer associations, message consistency, distinctive assets, competitive conventions, internal adoption, accessibility, localization, and reputation signals.

## Core knowledge
A brand is accumulated customer memory and expectation, not a logo system alone. Consistency should reinforce recognizable meaning while allowing contextual execution. Brand metrics need behavioral and commercial context.

## Procedure
1. Define intended brand associations and strategic role.
2. Audit major customer touchpoints and current perception.
3. Identify distinctive assets and message elements worth protecting.
4. Define principles for voice, claims, identity, and experience.
5. Create governance proportional to brand risk.
6. Equip teams with examples, templates, and decision rules.
7. Review high-impact campaigns and partnerships for fit.
8. Measure awareness, consideration, preference, sentiment, and behavioral outcomes where appropriate.
9. Investigate perception gaps and root causes.
10. Evolve guidance when market or strategy changes materially.

## Decision points
Enforce tightly on high-risk identity and claims; allow flexibility where local relevance improves effectiveness without changing core meaning. Rebrand only when strategic benefit exceeds memory loss and migration cost.

## Common failure patterns
Treating brand as visual policing, inconsistent claims, endless guidelines nobody uses, trend-driven identity changes, measuring awareness without relevance, and ignoring product experience as a brand signal.

## Verification
Audit representative touchpoints, test recognition and comprehension with customers, monitor perception trends, and confirm teams can apply guidance without constant escalation.

## Expected output
Practical brand principles, governance, reusable assets, perception findings, and prioritized improvement actions.

## Stop conditions
Stop high-impact changes when trademark, legal, accessibility, cultural, or reputation risks require specialist approval.
