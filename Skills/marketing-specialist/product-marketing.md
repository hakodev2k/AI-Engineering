# Product Marketing

## Purpose
Connect customer and market understanding to product positioning, launches, adoption, competitive differentiation, and sales or customer-success enablement.

## When to use
Use for new products or features, adoption challenges, segment expansion, competitive pressure, packaging changes, and sales enablement.

## Inputs
Product capabilities, roadmap context, customer research, usage data, segments, competitive intelligence, pricing, sales feedback, and business goals.

## Context to inspect
Inspect customer jobs, adoption barriers, feature usage, alternatives, objections, proof points, support issues, roadmap dependencies, and existing enablement.

## Core knowledge
Product marketing translates capabilities into customer value and market action. It should influence both how products are understood and what teams learn from the market. Feature availability does not equal customer value.

## Procedure
1. Define target users, buyers, and use cases.
2. Identify problems, triggers, alternatives, and desired outcomes.
3. Map capabilities to differentiated customer value.
4. Build positioning and proof.
5. Define launch tier based on customer and business impact.
6. Create messaging and enablement by audience.
7. Prepare adoption journeys and customer education.
8. Equip sales and success teams with qualification, objections, proof, and competitive guidance.
9. Instrument awareness, trial, adoption, usage depth, retention, and revenue outcomes.
10. Collect market feedback and feed it into product decisions.

## Decision points
Use broad launches for meaningful market value; use targeted communication for niche improvements. Emphasize outcomes over feature detail unless technical capability is itself a buying criterion.

## Common failure patterns
Launching every feature, internal-language messaging, weak proof, no adoption plan, enablement delivered after launch, and reporting impressions instead of usage or business impact.

## Verification
Confirm target customers understand the value, enablement is usable, tracking covers adoption, and post-launch behavior supports the intended use case.

## Expected output
Positioning, launch plan, messaging, enablement, adoption strategy, competitive guidance, and feedback loop.

## Stop conditions
Stop claims or launch commitments when product behavior, availability, legal approval, or customer evidence does not support them.
