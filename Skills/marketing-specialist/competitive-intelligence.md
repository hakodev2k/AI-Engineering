# Competitive Intelligence

## Purpose
Maintain an evidence-based view of competitors and substitutes so marketing decisions reflect real customer alternatives rather than internal assumptions.

## When to use
Use for positioning, sales enablement, launches, market entry, pricing discussions, campaign planning, and when competitors materially change strategy.

## Inputs
Public competitor materials, customer interviews, win/loss data, sales feedback, reviews, analyst or market sources, product observations, and historical intelligence.

## Context to inspect
Inspect source dates, market segment, competitor target customer, pricing model, claims, distribution, partnerships, product changes, customer sentiment, and alternative non-product solutions.

## Core knowledge
Competitive intelligence should explain choices customers make, not produce feature spreadsheets alone. Distinguish verified facts, credible inference, and rumor. Gather information ethically and lawfully.

## Procedure
1. Define the decision the intelligence must support.
2. Identify direct competitors, indirect substitutes, and status-quo alternatives.
3. Collect current public and customer-derived evidence.
4. Classify evidence by confidence and date.
5. Compare target segments, problems, positioning, proof, pricing, channels, and customer experience.
6. Analyze likely strengths, weaknesses, strategic moves, and constraints.
7. Identify whitespace and vulnerable assumptions in your own strategy.
8. Translate findings into messaging, campaign, product, or sales implications.
9. Create monitoring triggers for material changes.
10. Retire stale intelligence.

## Decision points
Track deeply when a competitor influences material revenue; monitor lightly when relevance is peripheral. Respond to competitor moves only when they change customer choice, not merely because they are visible.

## Common failure patterns
Feature obsession, stale screenshots, treating sales anecdotes as facts, unethical collection, copying competitors, ignoring substitutes, and reactive strategy changes.

## Verification
Cross-check material claims across sources, timestamp evidence, validate customer relevance, and label uncertainty explicitly.

## Expected output
A current competitive brief with evidence, implications, differentiation opportunities, risks, and monitoring priorities.

## Stop conditions
Stop collection when methods would violate law, contracts, access controls, privacy, or ethical standards; escalate material legal uncertainty.
