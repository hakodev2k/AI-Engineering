# Customer Segmentation

## Purpose
Create actionable customer segments that improve targeting, messaging, channel choice, offers, and measurement.

## When to use
Use when audiences are too broad, acquisition efficiency differs materially, messaging needs personalization, or product adoption varies across customer groups.

## Inputs
Customer records, behavioral data, research, transaction data, firmographic or demographic attributes where lawful, needs, lifecycle state, and campaign outcomes.

## Context to inspect
Inspect data quality, consent constraints, current segments, customer journeys, purchase frequency, value distribution, churn, acquisition source, and qualitative research.

## Core knowledge
Useful segments are measurable, reachable, meaningfully different, stable enough to act on, and connected to customer needs or behavior. Avoid sensitive or discriminatory segmentation and proxy variables that create unfair outcomes.

## Procedure
1. Define the marketing decision segmentation must improve.
2. Select candidate dimensions tied to behavior, needs, value, or context.
3. Clean and validate source data.
4. Identify meaningful patterns without overfitting noise.
5. Describe each segment using needs, triggers, barriers, behaviors, and value.
6. Estimate segment size and reachability.
7. Map relevant propositions and channels.
8. Test whether differentiated treatment improves outcomes.
9. Monitor migration between segments.
10. Retire segments that no longer drive decisions.

## Decision points
Prefer needs or behavioral segmentation when it predicts action better than demographics. Use lifecycle segments when timing is the main differentiator. Keep segmentation simple when operational systems cannot support fine-grained activation.

## Common failure patterns
Persona theater, excessive micro-segments, segments that cannot be targeted, circular definitions based on campaign outcomes, weak data quality, and discriminatory proxies.

## Verification
Confirm segments differ on relevant outcomes, can be identified operationally, receive meaningfully different treatment, and improve decision quality through experiments or historical validation.

## Expected output
A segment framework with definitions, evidence, sizing, targeting rules, needs, messaging implications, and measurement criteria.

## Stop conditions
Stop when data is insufficient, segmentation depends on prohibited attributes, or no actionable difference exists between proposed groups.
