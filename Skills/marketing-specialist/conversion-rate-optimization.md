# Conversion Rate Optimization

## Purpose
Improve valuable customer actions through evidence-driven changes to journeys, pages, forms, offers, and experiences.

## When to use
Use when meaningful traffic exists and conversion friction or uncertainty limits outcomes. Do not use CRO to disguise poor product-market fit or low-quality acquisition.

## Inputs
Analytics, funnel data, user research, recordings or feedback where lawful, page content, experiment history, technical constraints, and business economics.

## Context to inspect
Inspect traffic sources, device mix, page speed, accessibility, error rates, form abandonment, segment behavior, customer objections, trust signals, and downstream quality.

## Core knowledge
Conversion rate is conditional on audience mix and intent. Optimize for valuable outcomes, not clicks. Strong experimentation requires a clear hypothesis, adequate exposure, stable instrumentation, and predefined success criteria.

## Procedure
1. Define the target conversion and downstream guardrails.
2. Validate baseline tracking and traffic composition.
3. Identify high-impact friction using quantitative and qualitative evidence.
4. Form hypotheses linking a cause to a proposed change.
5. Prioritize by expected impact, evidence, effort, and risk.
6. Design the smallest test that isolates the hypothesis.
7. QA variants, accessibility, performance, and analytics.
8. Run until predefined validity conditions are met.
9. Analyze primary, guardrail, segment, and downstream metrics.
10. Ship, iterate, or reject based on evidence.
11. Record results to prevent repeated failed ideas.

## Decision points
Use controlled experiments when traffic and infrastructure permit; use sequential evidence and usability testing when they do not. Remove steps only if qualification, security, trust, and compliance remain intact.

## Common failure patterns
Testing cosmetic changes without evidence, peeking and stopping early, multiple simultaneous variables, ignoring novelty effects, optimizing low-value conversions, and generalizing segment-specific wins.

## Verification
Confirm event accuracy, experiment assignment, sample integrity, guardrail health, and sustained downstream value after rollout.

## Expected output
A CRO backlog, experiment specifications, validated results, implementation decisions, and reusable learning.

## Stop conditions
Stop when instrumentation is unreliable, traffic cannot support inference, or the proposed test risks security, accessibility, legal compliance, or customer trust.
