# Marketing Budget Allocation

## Purpose
Allocate marketing investment across channels, audiences, campaigns, and experiments based on marginal value, strategic objectives, risk, and learning needs.

## When to use
Use for annual or quarterly planning, reforecasting, campaign scaling, channel diversification, or when acquisition economics change.

## Inputs
Budget ceiling, business targets, historical spend and outcomes, incremental evidence, unit economics, capacity constraints, seasonality, strategic priorities, and risk tolerance.

## Context to inspect
Inspect marginal returns, channel saturation, conversion lag, customer quality, fixed commitments, minimum viable spend, operational capacity, attribution uncertainty, and cash-flow constraints.

## Core knowledge
Average historical ROAS is not a sufficient allocation rule. The next unit of spend matters. Allocation should balance proven returns, strategic coverage, diversification, and deliberate learning.

## Procedure
1. Define business outcomes and financial guardrails.
2. Separate committed, discretionary, and experimental budget.
3. Normalize channel performance to comparable value metrics.
4. Adjust for attribution and incrementality uncertainty.
5. Estimate response curves or practical saturation signals.
6. Identify minimum and maximum useful spend ranges.
7. Allocate first to highest-confidence marginal opportunities.
8. Reserve budget for strategically valuable experiments.
9. Stress-test downside scenarios and capacity constraints.
10. Define reallocation thresholds and review cadence.
11. Reforecast using actual incremental performance.

## Decision points
Concentrate spend when marginal returns are strong and stable; diversify when concentration risk or learning value justifies it. Fund experiments explicitly rather than hiding them inside performance budgets.

## Common failure patterns
Last-year-plus-percentage budgeting, equal allocation by channel, scaling on average ROAS, ignoring lag and retention, starving new-channel learning, and continuing spend due to sunk cost.

## Verification
Reconcile allocated budget to authorization, validate economic assumptions, compare forecast to actual marginal outcomes, and confirm reallocation rules are followed.

## Expected output
A budget plan with allocation rationale, expected outcomes, uncertainty, experiment reserve, guardrails, and reforecast triggers.

## Stop conditions
Stop when financial inputs are materially inconsistent, spend exceeds authorization, or measurement cannot support the requested precision.
