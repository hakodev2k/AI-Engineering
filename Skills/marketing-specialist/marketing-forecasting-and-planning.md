# Marketing Forecasting and Planning

## Purpose
Build transparent marketing forecasts and operating plans that connect activity, funnel assumptions, economics, capacity, and uncertainty to business targets.

## When to use
Use for annual or quarterly plans, campaign forecasts, pipeline or revenue planning, budget requests, and scenario analysis.

## Inputs
Business targets, historical funnel data, spend, channel capacity, conversion lag, retention, unit economics, seasonality, planned initiatives, and known constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect metric definitions, historical volatility, mix changes, one-time events, saturation, sales or fulfillment capacity, conversion delays, product changes, and data quality.

## Core knowledge
Forecasts are conditional models, not promises. Make assumptions explicit, separate controllable inputs from uncertain response rates, and provide ranges or scenarios when uncertainty is material.

## Procedure
1. Define the target outcome and forecast horizon.
2. Establish clean historical baselines.
3. Decompose the outcome into measurable drivers.
4. Estimate realistic ranges for each driver.
5. Model lag, seasonality, saturation, and capacity constraints.
6. Build base, upside, and downside scenarios.
7. Reconcile spend and activity assumptions with operational capacity.
8. Identify leading indicators that reveal scenario drift early.
9. Document assumptions and sensitivity.
10. Review with finance, sales, product, or operations where dependencies exist.
11. Reforecast using actuals and explain variance.

## Decision points
Use simple driver models when they explain decisions adequately; add complexity only when it materially improves forecast usefulness. Prefer ranges over false precision for volatile channels.

## Common failure patterns
Straight-line growth, ignoring lag, using best-ever conversion rates, double-counting channels, no capacity constraints, unexplained manual overrides, and treating forecast misses as execution failures without examining assumptions.

## Verification
Back-test against prior periods, reconcile model outputs to historical actuals, run sensitivity analysis, and ensure assumptions are reviewable and versioned.

## Expected output
A driver-based forecast with scenarios, assumptions, sensitivities, leading indicators, risks, and reforecast cadence.

## Stop conditions
Stop when baseline data is too inconsistent for meaningful modeling or required financial assumptions lack an accountable owner.
