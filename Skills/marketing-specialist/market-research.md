# Market Research

## Purpose
Turn uncertain market questions into evidence-backed decisions about audiences, problems, competitors, positioning, channels, and opportunities.

## When to use
Use before entering a market, launching an offer, changing positioning, planning campaigns, or when performance assumptions are weak. Do not use research to postpone decisions indefinitely.

## Inputs
Business objective, target market, existing customer data, analytics, sales feedback, competitor information, budget, timeline, and known constraints.

## Preconditions
Define the decision the research must support and the confidence required.

## Context to inspect
Review existing research, customer segments, funnel data, win/loss reasons, search behavior, category language, competitor claims, pricing, and distribution channels.

## Core knowledge
Separate primary from secondary research and facts from hypotheses. Market size alone does not prove reachable demand. Behavioral evidence is generally stronger than stated preference. Sampling bias, survivorship bias, and leading questions can invalidate conclusions.

## Procedure
1. Frame the business decision and research questions.
2. Record current assumptions and evidence gaps.
3. Segment the market into meaningful groups.
4. Gather reliable secondary evidence.
5. Design primary research where gaps remain.
6. Compare customer problems, alternatives, buying triggers, objections, and willingness to act.
7. Map competitors and substitutes by customer outcome rather than feature list only.
8. Quantify opportunity where evidence supports it.
9. Triangulate conflicting sources.
10. Produce findings, confidence levels, implications, and recommended experiments.

## Decision points
Use interviews for depth, surveys for validated quantification, behavioral analytics for observed actions, and experiments for causal evidence. Choose methods based on the decision, not convenience.

## Common failure patterns
Confirmation bias, tiny unrepresentative samples, confusing interest with purchase intent, relying on stale reports, ignoring substitutes, and presenting estimates as facts.

## Verification
Trace major conclusions to evidence, document sample limitations, challenge findings with contradictory data, and confirm recommendations answer the original decision.

## Expected output
A concise evidence pack containing findings, segments, opportunity signals, risks, confidence levels, and next actions.

## Stop conditions
Stop and escalate when reliable evidence is unavailable, the target market is undefined, research would violate privacy or consent, or the requested certainty cannot be supported.
