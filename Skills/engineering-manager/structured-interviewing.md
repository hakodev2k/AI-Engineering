# Structured Interviewing

## Purpose
Run fair, repeatable engineering interviews that produce evidence about job-relevant competencies and reduce intuition-driven hiring errors.

## When to use
Use when designing or operating interview loops for engineering roles.

## Inputs
Role definition, competency rubric, seniority expectations, interview format, legal constraints, and candidate information appropriate for interview use.

## Context to inspect
Inspect previous hiring signals, interviewer calibration, role responsibilities, and whether each interview stage measures a distinct competency.

## Core knowledge
Reliable interviews use consistent prompts, behavioral anchors, independent scoring, and evidence. Puzzle difficulty and interviewer similarity are weak proxies for job performance.

## Procedure
1. Define competencies and observable behaviors for the role.
2. Assign each competency to an interview stage.
3. Create consistent prompts and follow-up probes.
4. Define scoring anchors before interviews begin.
5. Train interviewers on bias, evidence capture, and candidate experience.
6. Conduct interviews without leaking prior scores.
7. Record evidence separately from conclusions.
8. Submit independent scores before debrief.
9. Resolve disagreements by comparing evidence to rubric.
10. Periodically audit interview outcomes and recalibrate.

## Decision points
Use work samples when they resemble real work and can be completed fairly. Avoid unnecessary algorithmic exercises when they do not predict role performance.

## Common failure patterns
Unstructured conversations, charisma bias, inconsistent difficulty, leading candidates, changing standards mid-loop, and debriefing before independent scoring.

## Verification
Confirm all required competencies received evidence, scores reference rubric anchors, interviewers submitted independently, and no decision depends on protected characteristics or unsupported intuition.

## Expected output
A defensible hiring recommendation with competency-level evidence and uncertainty clearly stated.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when the interview process violates policy, evidence is insufficient for a decision, or conflicts of interest compromise fairness.
