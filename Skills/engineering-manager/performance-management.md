# Performance Management

## Purpose
Evaluate performance consistently, make expectations explicit, recognize strong contribution, and address sustained gaps with evidence and fairness.

## When to use
Use continuously for expectation management and formally during review, calibration, promotion, or performance-improvement processes.

## Inputs
Role expectations, goals, observable outcomes, feedback, incident and delivery context, collaboration evidence, and organizational performance policy.

## Context to inspect
Review impact over an appropriate period, scope and difficulty of work, opportunities available, previous expectations, and systemic constraints. Avoid judging by activity metrics alone.

## Core knowledge
Performance is multidimensional: outcomes, technical judgment, quality, collaboration, ownership, and role-specific leadership. Evaluation should distinguish isolated mistakes from sustained patterns.

## Procedure
1. Establish clear role and level expectations early.
2. Collect evidence throughout the period.
3. Evaluate outcomes in context, including quality and operational impact.
4. Compare patterns against documented expectations, not peers' personalities.
5. Surface gaps promptly with specific examples.
6. Define measurable improvement expectations and support.
7. Recognize high-impact behaviors and expanded scope.
8. Calibrate ratings using consistent evidence standards.
9. Document decisions according to policy.
10. Reassess after the agreed evidence window.

## Decision points
Use coaching for developing capability; use formal performance processes for persistent material gaps after expectations and support are clear. Do not use promotion as a retention substitute when level evidence is absent.

## Common failure patterns
Surprise reviews, recency bias, rewarding visible heroics over sustainable outcomes, stack ranking without context, vague improvement goals, and conflating disagreement with poor performance.

## Verification
Verify every material judgment has evidence, expectations were previously communicated, comparable standards were applied, and the engineer understands next steps.

## Expected output
A fair performance assessment with evidence, strengths, gaps, expectations, development actions, and documented decisions.

## Stop conditions
Escalate to the required HR or leadership process for termination risk, protected leave, discrimination concerns, or policy-governed formal action.
