# Stakeholder Communication

## Purpose
Communicate engineering status, risk, trade-offs, and decisions in a form that enables stakeholders to act without drowning them in implementation detail.

## When to use
Use for roadmap updates, incidents, executive reviews, risk escalation, delivery changes, investment proposals, and major technical decisions.

## Inputs
Audience, decision needed, current evidence, forecast, risks, alternatives, technical context, and prior commitments.

## Context to inspect
Understand what the audience owns, what they need to decide, their technical depth, prior expectations, and the consequence of delayed communication.

## Core knowledge
Good communication is audience-specific but evidence-consistent. Uncertainty should be expressed explicitly. A status update should distinguish facts, forecasts, risks, and asks.

## Procedure
1. Identify the audience and desired decision or awareness.
2. Lead with outcome and material change.
3. Quantify impact where evidence supports it.
4. Explain uncertainty and assumptions without false precision.
5. Present options and trade-offs for decisions.
6. State recommendation and rationale.
7. Make risks, owners, and required actions explicit.
8. Tailor technical depth to the audience.
9. Record consequential decisions.
10. Update stakeholders promptly when material facts change.

## Decision points
Use asynchronous written communication for durable status and decisions; use synchronous discussion for ambiguity, conflict, or rapid coordination.

## Common failure patterns
Green status until the deadline fails, technical detail without impact, vague risk language, hiding uncertainty, excessive optimism, and updates without a clear ask.

## Verification
Verify recipients understand current outcome, forecast, major risks, decisions required, and next update point.

## Expected output
A concise, decision-oriented stakeholder communication grounded in current evidence.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when disclosure is restricted by security, legal, personnel, or contractual policy; use the authorized communication channel instead.
