# Career Development and Promotion

## Purpose
Help engineers grow toward meaningful career goals and make promotion decisions based on sustained next-level evidence rather than tenure or advocacy alone.

## When to use
Use during development planning, recurring career conversations, succession planning, and promotion cycles.

## Inputs
Career framework, current-level evidence, engineer goals, available opportunities, feedback, business needs, and promotion policy.

## Context to inspect
Inspect demonstrated scope, impact, technical and organizational behaviors, opportunities the engineer has actually had, and whether expectations are equally accessible across the team.

## Core knowledge
Development and promotion are related but different. Development should continue even when no promotion is available. Promotion should recognize sustained operation at the next level, not promise future potential.

## Procedure
1. Understand the engineer's goals without assuming management is the default path.
2. Compare current evidence with relevant competency expectations.
3. Identify one or two high-leverage development gaps.
4. Create opportunities that require the target behaviors in real work.
5. Provide mentorship, sponsorship, feedback, and visibility proportionate to need.
6. Track evidence over time rather than counting completed tasks.
7. Review whether opportunity allocation is equitable.
8. For promotion, assemble evidence against next-level expectations.
9. Calibrate consistently across candidates.
10. Communicate decisions and remaining gaps clearly.

## Decision points
Choose specialist, staff-plus, management, or lateral growth based on strengths and interests. Do not manufacture oversized projects solely to create promotion evidence.

## Common failure patterns
Tenure-based promotion, vague career plans, hidden criteria, promoting potential instead of sustained evidence, unequal access to high-visibility work, and making management the only growth path.

## Verification
Verify development actions map to explicit competencies, evidence spans enough time and contexts, and promotion recommendations can be defended independently of manager advocacy.

## Expected output
A development plan and, when applicable, an evidence-based promotion recommendation.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when framework expectations conflict, promotion policy requires higher approval, or opportunity inequity prevents a fair assessment.
