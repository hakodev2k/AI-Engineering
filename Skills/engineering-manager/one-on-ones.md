# One-on-Ones

## Purpose
Use recurring one-on-one conversations to surface risks, develop engineers, improve working relationships, and understand context that dashboards cannot show.

## When to use
Use with direct reports on a predictable cadence and additionally during major transitions or sustained performance concerns.

## Inputs
Recent commitments, previous follow-ups, feedback, career goals, team changes, and relevant delivery or operational context.

## Context to inspect
Review prior action items and observable work context, but let the report shape the agenda rather than turning the meeting into status reporting.

## Core knowledge
One-on-ones are primarily for the report. Psychological safety does not mean avoiding difficult feedback; it means making candid discussion safe, respectful, and actionable.

## Procedure
1. Prepare unresolved follow-ups and important context.
2. Invite the report's agenda first.
3. Explore energy, blockers, relationships, growth, and concerns with open questions.
4. Listen for patterns rather than immediately solving every problem.
5. Give timely specific feedback when useful.
6. Discuss career development periodically, not only during review cycles.
7. Convert actionable issues into owners and next steps.
8. Record only appropriate non-sensitive notes.
9. Follow through before the next meeting.
10. Escalate serious safety, conduct, or policy issues through the correct process.

## Decision points
Coach when the person can discover the answer; advise when context or urgency makes direct guidance more useful. Move status updates to asynchronous channels when possible.

## Common failure patterns
Canceling repeatedly, manager-dominated agendas, vague reassurance, surprise performance feedback, over-documenting sensitive conversations, and failing to act on recurring concerns.

## Verification
Verify commitments from prior meetings were addressed, important concerns have next steps, and performance or growth feedback is specific enough to act on.

## Expected output
Shared clarity on concerns, development, decisions, and a small set of concrete follow-ups.

## Stop conditions
Escalate immediately for credible harassment, safety, legal, self-harm, security, or serious misconduct concerns according to organizational policy.
