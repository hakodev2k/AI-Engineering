# Feedback and Coaching

## Purpose
Improve engineering judgment and behavior through timely evidence-based feedback and coaching rather than waiting for formal review cycles.

## When to use
Use after meaningful work, repeated patterns, collaboration issues, leadership opportunities, or when an engineer needs help developing a capability.

## Inputs
Observed behavior, impact, role expectations, relevant context, previous feedback, and the engineer's goals.

## Context to inspect
Separate direct observation from hearsay, verify context, and understand whether the issue is skill, clarity, motivation, environment, or capacity.

## Core knowledge
Useful feedback describes observable behavior and impact, then creates a path forward. Coaching develops judgment by helping the engineer reason; instruction is appropriate when expectations or required behavior are non-negotiable.

## Procedure
1. Gather specific evidence close to the event.
2. Check whether expectations were clear.
3. Describe behavior and impact without labeling personality.
4. Ask for the engineer's perspective and missing context.
5. Agree on the desired behavior or capability.
6. Decide whether coaching, instruction, practice, or environmental change is needed.
7. Define a small observable next step.
8. Provide support and opportunities to practice.
9. Follow up with reinforcing or corrective feedback.
10. Record material performance expectations appropriately.

## Decision points
Coach when discovery builds transferable judgment; direct when risk, policy, or basic expectations require clarity. Address systemic causes rather than repeatedly coaching individuals around broken processes.

## Common failure patterns
Vague feedback, feedback about personality, praise without explaining value, saving criticism for reviews, over-coaching when direct expectations are needed, and ignoring environmental causes.

## Verification
Verify the engineer can restate the expected change, has an opportunity to demonstrate it, and subsequent evidence shows improvement or identifies a different root cause.

## Expected output
Clear feedback, agreed development action, support plan, and follow-up evidence.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when behavior involves misconduct, discrimination, safety, or formal performance action outside the manager's delegated process.
