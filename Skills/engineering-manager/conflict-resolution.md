# Conflict Resolution

## Purpose
Resolve engineering conflicts constructively while preserving candor, accountability, working relationships, and decision quality.

## When to use
Use for persistent technical disagreement, ownership conflict, interpersonal friction, priority disputes, or behavior that is degrading collaboration.

## Inputs
Observed behavior, decision context, stakeholder perspectives, role expectations, relevant policies, and prior attempts to resolve the issue.

## Context to inspect
Separate disagreement about facts, goals, values, incentives, process, and relationships. Verify direct evidence before attributing motives.

## Core knowledge
Healthy task conflict can improve decisions; relationship conflict and unresolved ambiguity can damage teams. Managers should make decision rights and behavioral expectations explicit rather than forcing superficial harmony.

## Procedure
1. Define the concrete conflict and its impact.
2. Speak with involved people and gather perspectives.
3. Separate observable facts from interpretations.
4. Identify shared goals and genuine points of disagreement.
5. Clarify decision rights, constraints, and behavioral expectations.
6. Facilitate direct discussion when safe and useful.
7. Evaluate options using evidence and agreed criteria.
8. Make or route the decision if consensus is unnecessary or unavailable.
9. Record commitments and follow up on behavior and outcomes.
10. Address recurring systemic causes such as unclear ownership or incentives.

## Decision points
Mediate when parties can safely resolve the issue; decide directly when authority is clear and delay is costly. Use formal processes for misconduct rather than informal mediation.

## Common failure patterns
Avoiding conflict, forcing compromise between unequal evidence, triangulation, labeling personalities, rewarding aggression, and treating policy violations as ordinary disagreements.

## Verification
Verify the decision or behavioral agreement is explicit, owners understand expectations, and follow-up evidence shows reduced friction or reveals a deeper issue.

## Expected output
A resolved decision or working agreement with clear expectations and follow-up.

## Stop conditions
Escalate harassment, discrimination, threats, retaliation, serious misconduct, or conflicts requiring formal HR, legal, or security processes.
