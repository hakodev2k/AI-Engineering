# Manager Operating System

## Purpose
Create a lightweight recurring management system that keeps people, delivery, risk, operations, and strategy visible without replacing judgment with meetings.

## When to use
Use when managing one or more engineering teams, especially as scope grows and important follow-ups begin depending on memory.

## Inputs
Team structure, goals, delivery plans, operational metrics, one-on-one cadence, risks, decisions, hiring, and organizational obligations.

## Context to inspect
Inspect existing meetings and reports, duplicate status channels, missed decisions, follow-up failures, information latency, and manager attention bottlenecks.

## Core knowledge
A management operating system is a set of feedback loops. Each recurring mechanism should have a purpose, owner, inputs, decision output, and cadence appropriate to how quickly the underlying condition changes.

## Procedure
1. Identify recurring decisions and information needs.
2. Establish minimal cadences for one-on-ones, team planning, delivery review, operational health, hiring, and strategy as applicable.
3. Move passive status reporting asynchronous where possible.
4. Maintain a concise decision, risk, and follow-up system.
5. Define which metrics are reviewed and why.
6. Protect focus time for engineers and managers.
7. Route urgent operational signals outside slow planning cadences.
8. Periodically audit meetings and reports for decision value.
9. Remove mechanisms that duplicate information or no longer drive action.
10. Adapt the system as team size, maturity, and risk change.

## Decision points
Increase cadence for fast-changing high-risk work; reduce cadence for stable domains. Prefer written artifacts when history and broad distribution matter.

## Common failure patterns
Meeting accumulation, status theater, dashboards without decisions, manager as information router, no follow-up tracking, and applying the same cadence to every team.

## Verification
Verify each recurring mechanism has a clear decision or outcome, important risks surface in time, follow-ups close, and team focus is not unnecessarily fragmented.

## Expected output
A sustainable management cadence and information system with clear purposes, owners, and review loops.

## Stop conditions
Redesign the operating system when it consumes disproportionate team capacity, creates duplicate reporting, or consistently fails to surface important issues early.
