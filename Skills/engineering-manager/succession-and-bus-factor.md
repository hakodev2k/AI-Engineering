# Succession and Bus-Factor Management

## Purpose
Reduce organizational fragility caused by critical knowledge, authority, or operational capability being concentrated in too few people.

## When to use
Use when key systems depend on one expert, managers or technical leaders have no backup, on-call knowledge is narrow, or upcoming leave or attrition creates continuity risk.

## Inputs
Ownership map, critical systems, role responsibilities, on-call rotations, knowledge distribution, planned transitions, and capability assessments.

## Context to inspect
Inspect who can deploy, approve, diagnose, recover, access critical systems, make domain decisions, and explain undocumented behavior.

## Core knowledge
Bus factor is not solved by documentation alone. Durable resilience requires repeated practice, shared authority, accessible systems, and multiple people performing real responsibilities.

## Procedure
1. Identify critical responsibilities and systems.
2. Map primary and backup capability for each.
3. Rank concentration risks by business and operational impact.
4. Remove unnecessary access or approval monopolies.
5. Pair or rotate ownership on real work.
6. Capture decision context and operating procedures.
7. Exercise backups through incidents, releases, reviews, or temporary ownership.
8. Develop successors for leadership and specialist roles.
9. Verify transitions before planned departures or leave.
10. Reassess after reorganizations or major architecture changes.

## Decision points
Use specialization where depth is valuable, but create sufficient backup for critical operations. Do not distribute sensitive privileges more broadly than necessary; use controlled emergency access where appropriate.

## Common failure patterns
Wiki-only knowledge transfer, nominal backups who never practice, single approvers, hero culture, and succession discussed only after resignation.

## Verification
Verify backups can independently execute critical workflows, access is available through approved controls, and key decisions and escalation paths are documented.

## Expected output
A continuity plan with concentration risks, backups, development actions, and readiness evidence.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when required separation of duties conflicts with backup design, critical access cannot be delegated safely, or imminent departure creates unresolved continuity risk.
