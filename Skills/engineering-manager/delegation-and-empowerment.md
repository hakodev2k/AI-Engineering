# Delegation and Empowerment

## Purpose
Delegate outcomes and decision authority at the right level so engineers grow while the manager avoids becoming a delivery bottleneck.

## When to use
Use when assigning technical leadership, project ownership, incident roles, planning responsibilities, or recurring operational decisions.

## Inputs
Desired outcome, risk level, engineer capability, decision scope, dependencies, constraints, and escalation expectations.

## Context to inspect
Inspect who has relevant context, whether authority matches responsibility, prior experience, reversibility of decisions, and the cost of failure.

## Core knowledge
Delegation is not task dumping. Effective delegation defines outcome, boundaries, authority, support, and checkpoints while leaving implementation choices appropriately open.

## Procedure
1. Define the outcome and why it matters.
2. Identify decisions that can safely move closer to the work.
3. Match scope to the engineer's capability and growth goals.
4. Clarify constraints, non-negotiables, and available resources.
5. Explicitly transfer decision authority within the agreed boundary.
6. Agree on checkpoints based on risk, not manager anxiety.
7. Provide context and remove blockers without retaking ownership.
8. Let reversible mistakes become learning when blast radius is acceptable.
9. Review outcome and decision quality afterward.
10. Expand authority as demonstrated judgment grows.

## Decision points
Delegate more aggressively for reversible low-blast-radius decisions. Retain or require review for regulatory, security, personnel, or irreversible high-impact decisions.

## Common failure patterns
Delegating tasks without authority, micromanaging after delegation, unclear success criteria, assigning only undesirable work, and taking ownership back at the first disagreement.

## Verification
Verify the owner can state the outcome, boundaries, authority, escalation triggers, and checkpoints without needing hidden manager approval.

## Expected output
A clear delegated ownership agreement and evidence that decisions can proceed independently within bounds.

## Stop conditions
Stop or escalate when the decision exceeds delegated authority, new evidence materially changes risk, or required governance mandates approval.
