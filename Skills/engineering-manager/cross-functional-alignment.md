# Cross-Functional Alignment

## Purpose
Align engineering, product, design, operations, security, data, and business stakeholders around outcomes, constraints, decisions, and ownership.

## When to use
Use for ambiguous initiatives, conflicting priorities, cross-functional launches, dependency negotiations, or repeated misunderstandings about scope and responsibility.

## Inputs
Objectives, stakeholder map, requirements, constraints, roadmap, risks, decision history, and team responsibilities.

## Context to inspect
Inspect incentives, vocabulary differences, decision rights, unresolved assumptions, dependency commitments, and where previous communication failed.

## Core knowledge
Alignment does not require unanimous preference. It requires shared understanding of the decision, rationale, consequences, ownership, and escalation path.

## Procedure
1. Define the outcome and decision that requires alignment.
2. Identify affected stakeholders and decision owner.
3. Surface constraints, assumptions, and conflicting incentives.
4. Translate technical concerns into impact and options.
5. Separate facts, hypotheses, preferences, and commitments.
6. Compare alternatives using agreed criteria.
7. Record the decision, rationale, dissent, and consequences.
8. Assign owners and dates for dependencies.
9. Communicate changes to affected groups.
10. Revisit when material assumptions change.

## Decision points
Seek consensus when it improves decision quality at reasonable cost; use explicit accountable decision-making when consensus stalls. Escalate only after options and trade-offs are clear.

## Common failure patterns
Meeting-heavy alignment without decisions, hidden vetoes, engineering jargon, agreeing verbally without ownership, and reopening settled decisions without new evidence.

## Verification
Verify stakeholders can state the chosen outcome, key trade-offs, their responsibilities, dependencies, and escalation path consistently.

## Expected output
A shared decision record with ownership, dependencies, assumptions, and communication plan.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when accountable owners disagree on business priorities, contractual commitments conflict, or no participant has authority to resolve the decision.
