# Managing Engineering Managers

## Purpose
Lead managers through clear organizational outcomes, consistent management standards, delegation, coaching, and system-level accountability.

## When to use
Use when responsible for multiple engineering teams through other managers or when preparing a senior manager to operate at that scope.

## Inputs
Organizational goals, team portfolio, manager responsibilities, delivery and health signals, staffing, succession plans, and cross-team dependencies.

## Context to inspect
Inspect whether managers own outcomes or merely relay information, consistency of people practices, decision bottlenecks, team health differences, and where the senior leader is bypassing managers.

## Core knowledge
Managing managers shifts leverage from direct team decisions to designing the management system. The leader should evaluate whether managers create healthy, effective teams, not duplicate their operational work.

## Procedure
1. Define each manager's outcome scope and decision authority.
2. Establish consistent expectations for people, delivery, reliability, and communication.
3. Review team health and outcomes through evidence rather than skip-level anecdotes alone.
4. Coach managers on judgment, delegation, feedback, and organizational design.
5. Avoid routinely bypassing managers for team decisions.
6. Use skip-level conversations to learn system signals, not secretly manage reports.
7. Calibrate performance and promotion standards across teams.
8. Identify manager capacity, succession, and support risks.
9. Resolve cross-team structural issues at the appropriate level.
10. Increase manager autonomy as judgment and organizational reliability grow.

## Decision points
Intervene directly when safety, misconduct, severe delivery risk, or manager incapacity requires it; otherwise coach the accountable manager to solve the problem.

## Common failure patterns
Shadow-managing teams, managers as status relays, inconsistent standards, excessive layers, skip-level promises that undermine managers, and promoting strong engineers into management without support.

## Verification
Verify managers can state their outcomes and authority, teams receive consistent management standards, decisions occur at appropriate levels, and senior leadership is not a routine bottleneck.

## Expected output
A scalable management model with clear manager accountability, coaching priorities, calibration, and succession coverage.

## Stop conditions
Escalate serious personnel, legal, safety, or policy issues through required organizational processes rather than handling them solely through management coaching.
