# Developer Ecosystem Mapping

## Purpose
Map the tools, frameworks, communities, platforms, and decision-makers around a developer product to identify high-leverage integration and education opportunities.

## When to use
Use when entering a market, prioritizing integrations, selecting communities/events, or diagnosing ecosystem adoption gaps.

## Inputs
Target personas, product capabilities, usage telemetry, package/dependency data, community research, partner landscape.

## Context to inspect
Languages, frameworks, IDEs, CI/CD, cloud platforms, data stores, adjacent APIs, influencers, communities, standards, and adoption constraints.

## Core knowledge
Ecosystems are networks, not ranked vendor lists. Relevance depends on workflow adjacency, developer trust, switching cost, interoperability, and maintenance burden.

## Procedure
1. Define the developer workflow around the product.
2. Identify upstream inputs, downstream destinations, and operational tooling.
3. Map common technology combinations by segment.
4. Identify communities and trusted knowledge sources.
5. Estimate integration demand from evidence, not brand popularity.
6. Assess technical feasibility and long-term maintenance cost.
7. Rank opportunities by developer value, strategic fit, reach, and ownership.
8. Validate assumptions with practitioners and telemetry.
9. Revisit the map as standards and tooling change.

## Decision points
Prioritize native integrations when they remove repeated friction; educational interoperability when integration is simple; partnerships when joint maintenance/distribution creates durable value.

## Common failure patterns
Popularity-only rankings, ignoring maintenance cost, mapping vendors instead of workflows, overvaluing social reach, and assuming all developers in a language share one stack.

## Verification
Confirm high-priority nodes with multiple evidence sources and validate that proposed interventions remove a real workflow cost.

## Expected output
An evidence-backed ecosystem map with prioritized integrations, communities, content opportunities, dependencies, and risks.

## Stop conditions
Stop when data is too stale or biased to support prioritization, partnership constraints are unknown, or an integration would require unsupported commitments.
