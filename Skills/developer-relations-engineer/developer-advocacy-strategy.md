# Developer Advocacy Strategy

## Purpose
Define a repeatable developer-advocacy strategy that converts developer needs into useful education, product feedback, and measurable ecosystem outcomes.

## When to use
Use when planning a quarter, entering a new developer segment, or correcting unfocused advocacy. Do not use as a substitute for product strategy.

## Inputs
Product goals, target developers, adoption funnel, community signals, roadmap constraints, prior program metrics.

## Context to inspect
Review product positioning, personas, documentation gaps, support themes, community channels, competitor ecosystems, and existing content/events before proposing activity.

## Core knowledge
Senior DevRel optimizes for developer success rather than raw impressions. Advocacy must connect audience problem, technical value, credible proof, distribution, feedback, and measurable behavior change. Vanity metrics are diagnostic, not outcomes.

## Procedure
1. Define the developer segment and jobs-to-be-done.
2. Map awareness-to-production adoption friction.
3. Rank problems by frequency, severity, strategic fit, and evidence.
4. Select interventions: docs, samples, talks, workshops, community programs, product feedback, or partnerships.
5. Define outcome and leading indicators for each intervention.
6. Establish editorial and event cadence without overcommitting capacity.
7. Assign feedback routes to product/engineering owners.
8. Pilot high-uncertainty programs before scaling.
9. Review outcomes and retire low-leverage work.

## Decision points
Prefer durable technical assets when questions repeat; live engagement when ambiguity is high; community amplification when trusted practitioners already exist. Choose reach only after relevance and technical credibility.

## Common failure patterns
Activity without audience definition, optimizing impressions, evangelizing unsupported claims, ignoring onboarding friction, weak feedback closure, and spreading effort across too many channels.

## Verification
Verify every initiative has an audience, evidence-backed problem, owner, success metric, feedback path, and review date. Confirm metrics show downstream developer behavior where possible.

## Expected output
A prioritized advocacy portfolio with rationale, measures, dependencies, and explicit non-goals.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when product positioning is contradictory, claims cannot be substantiated, developer data is unavailable, or requested advocacy would misrepresent product capability.
