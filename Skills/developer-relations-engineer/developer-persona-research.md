# Developer Persona Research

## Purpose
Build evidence-based developer personas that improve messaging, documentation, samples, events, and product feedback.

## When to use
Use before targeting a new audience, revising onboarding, or when content performance conflicts with assumptions.

## Inputs
Interviews, surveys, support data, analytics, community discussions, product telemetry, sales or solutions feedback.

## Preconditions
Have a defined research question and lawful access to data. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.

## Context to inspect
Existing segmentation, adoption stages, technical stacks, organizational constraints, purchasing influence, deployment environments, and recurring objections.

## Core knowledge
Useful personas describe behavior, context, constraints, goals, and decision criteria—not stereotypes. Separate user, buyer, champion, operator, and evaluator roles where they differ.

## Procedure
1. State hypotheses to test.
2. Sample across adoption stages and outcomes.
3. Ask about actual recent behavior rather than hypothetical preference.
4. Code recurring jobs, blockers, triggers, trust signals, and constraints.
5. Cluster only where differences change an intervention.
6. Document evidence strength and counterexamples.
7. Validate personas against telemetry and additional interviews.
8. Translate findings into concrete content, product, and channel decisions.
9. Schedule reassessment as the ecosystem changes.

## Decision points
Use qualitative research for why/context; quantitative data for prevalence; both when making expensive program decisions. Split personas only when the distinction changes what you do.

## Common failure patterns
Demographic caricatures, interviewing only enthusiasts, leading questions, treating anecdotes as prevalence, and creating personas that never influence work.

## Verification
Confirm each persona has evidence, behavioral distinctions, actionable implications, known uncertainties, and at least one disconfirming test.

## Expected output
A concise persona set with jobs, environment, pain points, decision criteria, adoption barriers, preferred evidence, and implications.

## Stop conditions
Stop when consent or data-use boundaries are unclear, sample bias makes conclusions unsafe, or stakeholder pressure demands unsupported conclusions.
