# Developer Community Program Design

## Purpose
Design community programs that create durable peer value, healthy participation, and trustworthy technical exchange.

## When to use
Use when launching or restructuring forums, ambassador programs, office hours, meetups, or contributor initiatives.

## Inputs
Community goals, target members, channel behavior, moderation policy, staff capacity, product ecosystem, historical engagement.

## Context to inspect
Existing communities, member jobs, participation barriers, safety risks, geographic/time-zone needs, moderation load, and overlap with support.

## Core knowledge
Healthy communities depend on member-to-member value, clear norms, psychological safety, credible stewardship, and sustainable contributor pathways—not constant vendor broadcasting.

## Procedure
1. Define the member value proposition and boundaries.
2. Identify recurring interaction formats that solve real developer needs.
3. Establish code of conduct, moderation, escalation, and privacy rules.
4. Design onboarding and contributor progression.
5. Seed high-quality technical discussions without manufacturing engagement.
6. Recognize contribution transparently and equitably.
7. Equip moderators and community leaders with playbooks.
8. Track participation quality, repeat contribution, unanswered questions, and safety signals.
9. Gather feedback from active and inactive members.
10. Retire formats that create staff activity without member value.

## Decision points
Use owned communities when persistent identity/context matters; participate in existing communities when developers already gather there. Scale only after moderation capacity exists.

## Common failure patterns
Broadcast-only channels, engagement bait, unclear support expectations, favoritism, unpaid hidden labor, weak moderation, and optimizing member count over healthy participation.

## Verification
Confirm norms and escalation work in practice, member-to-member help occurs, unanswered technical questions decline, and contributor retention is healthy without staff overextension.

## Expected output
A sustainable community operating model with programs, governance, moderation, metrics, and contributor pathways.

## Stop conditions
Pause growth when moderation capacity is inadequate, harassment/safety issues are unresolved, or incentives encourage spam or misleading advocacy.
