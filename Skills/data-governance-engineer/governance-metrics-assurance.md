# Governance Metrics and Assurance

## Purpose
Measure whether governance controls actually reduce risk and improve trustworthy data rather than merely producing activity.

## When to use
Use for governance dashboards, executive reporting, control assurance, maturity reviews, or program prioritization.

## Inputs
Governance objectives, policies, controls, incidents, catalog/quality/access metrics, exceptions, audit findings, business outcomes.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current KPIs, data sources, metric definitions, incentives, reporting audiences, baselines, and known gaming behavior.

## Core knowledge
Distinguish activity, coverage, effectiveness, risk, and outcome metrics. Leading indicators predict exposure; lagging indicators show realized failures. Metrics require definitions, owners, lineage, and thresholds just like business data.

## Procedure
1. Start from governance outcomes and material risks.
2. Define questions each audience must answer.
3. Select a small balanced set of leading/lagging measures.
4. Define denominator, scope, calculation, owner, and refresh cadence.
5. Establish baseline and target/tolerance.
6. Automate collection from authoritative sources.
7. Segment by domain and criticality where useful.
8. Validate metrics against incidents and manual samples.
9. Investigate adverse trends and assign actions.
10. Retire metrics that do not drive decisions.
11. Periodically assure control design and operating effectiveness.

## Decision points
Prefer risk-weighted coverage over raw counts. Use maturity scoring only when criteria are evidence-based and comparable.

## Common failure patterns
Percent-complete vanity metrics, no denominator, self-reported compliance, targets that encourage gaming, dashboards without actions, and treating control presence as effectiveness.

## Verification
Recalculate samples independently, trace metrics to source evidence, and confirm trends correlate sensibly with control failures/incidents.

## Expected output
Metric catalog, dashboard specification, assurance plan, thresholds, and action workflow.

## Stop conditions
Escalate unreliable source data, metrics used for material decisions without validation, or evidence of systematic metric manipulation.
