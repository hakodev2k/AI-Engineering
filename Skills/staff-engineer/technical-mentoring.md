# Technical Mentoring

## Purpose
Increase organizational engineering capability by mentoring senior and mid-level engineers in technical judgment, architecture, debugging, and decision making rather than merely giving answers.

## When to use
Use when engineers are taking on larger scope, recurring judgment gaps appear, or critical knowledge is concentrated in a few individuals.

## Inputs
Engineer goals, current responsibilities, design work, incidents, code reviews, feedback, competency expectations.

## Preconditions
The mentee has real work where learning can be applied and feedback can be observed.

## Context to inspect
Recent decisions, technical strengths, repeated mistakes, communication patterns, ownership scope, and opportunities for increasing autonomy.

## Core knowledge
Effective Staff-level mentoring builds decision quality and independence. Use questions, modeling, delegated ownership, calibrated feedback, and progressively larger scope; avoid becoming a permanent approval dependency.

## Procedure
1. Identify the capability to develop.
2. Agree on a real work context for practice.
3. Ask the mentee to explain their model and alternatives first.
4. Surface missing constraints or evidence with questions.
5. Demonstrate reasoning only where necessary.
6. Delegate a meaningful decision with clear boundaries.
7. Review outcomes and decision process separately.
8. Give specific feedback tied to observable behavior.
9. Increase scope as judgment improves.
10. Track whether dependence on mentor decreases.

## Decision points
Coach when the cost of learning is acceptable. Be directive during high-severity incidents or irreversible risk, then debrief afterward. Match challenge level to current capability.

## Common failure patterns
Giving solutions immediately, mentoring only through code style comments, creating gatekeeper dependency, vague praise or criticism, and withholding consequential ownership.

## Verification
Look for improved independent decisions, stronger technical explanations, better risk identification, and reduced need for intervention over time.

## Expected output
A capability-growth plan supported by delegated work, concrete feedback, and observable progression.

## Stop conditions
Escalate performance or role-expectation issues to the appropriate manager when they exceed technical mentoring scope.
