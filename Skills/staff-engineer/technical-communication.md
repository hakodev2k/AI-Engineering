# Technical Communication

## Purpose
Communicate complex technical decisions, risks, and system behavior clearly to engineers, product leaders, executives, and adjacent functions without losing decision-relevant precision.

## When to use
Use for architecture proposals, incident briefings, strategy reviews, migration plans, executive updates, and cross-team alignment on technical trade-offs.

## Inputs
Audience, decision required, technical evidence, risks, alternatives, constraints, recommendation, supporting diagrams or metrics.

## Preconditions
The communicator understands the underlying technical issue well enough to distinguish facts, assumptions, and uncertainty.

## Context to inspect
Audience responsibilities, decision authority, prior context, terminology, business impact, unresolved risks, and the level of detail needed for action.

## Core knowledge
Communication quality is measured by shared understanding and better decisions, not by volume. Tailor abstraction level while preserving causal relationships, uncertainty, trade-offs, and operational consequences.

## Procedure
1. Identify the audience and decision or action required.
2. Lead with the problem, impact, and recommendation.
3. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and forecasts.
4. Explain only the technical mechanisms needed to understand trade-offs.
5. Quantify impact, cost, risk, and confidence when possible.
6. Present credible alternatives fairly.
7. Make ownership and requested decisions explicit.
8. Use diagrams or examples for complex dependency or failure paths.
9. Invite challenge on assumptions and evidence.
10. Record resulting decisions and follow-up actions.

## Decision points
Use concise executive framing for strategic decisions and deeper engineering detail for implementation decisions. Prefer written records for consequential or durable choices.

## Common failure patterns
Jargon without context, burying the recommendation, overstating certainty, presenting only one option, excessive implementation detail, and failing to state the required decision.

## Verification
Ask whether recipients can accurately restate the problem, trade-offs, recommendation, decision owner, and next action; verify resulting decisions are recorded.

## Expected output
A decision-oriented technical communication artifact appropriate to the audience, with clear evidence, trade-offs, risks, and actions.

## Stop conditions
Stop when critical facts remain unverified or the communication would imply approval or certainty the engineering evidence does not support.
