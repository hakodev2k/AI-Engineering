# Scope and Change Control

## Purpose
Control material changes to program scope, commitments, and assumptions without blocking necessary adaptation.

## When to use
Use when requirements, architecture, timeline, compliance, or stakeholder commitments change after planning baseline.

## Inputs
Current charter, baseline plan, proposed change, impact analysis, owner recommendations, risk and dependency data.

## Context to inspect
Approved scope, decision log, contractual commitments, architecture constraints, launch dependencies, and previous changes.

## Core knowledge
Change is not failure; unmanaged change is. Senior TPMs make impacts explicit across schedule, cost, quality, risk, and dependencies before commitments move.

## Procedure
1. Describe the proposed change and its driver.
2. Determine whether it changes outcomes, scope, constraints, or only implementation detail.
3. Identify affected workstreams and dependencies.
4. Estimate schedule, capacity, risk, and quality impact.
5. Produce options: absorb, defer, trade scope, re-sequence, or reject.
6. Route the decision to the correct authority.
7. Record the approved change and rationale.
8. Update plans, metrics, risks, and stakeholder commitments.

## Decision points
Avoid formal change control for low-impact reversible implementation choices. Require explicit approval for changes affecting committed outcomes or external obligations.

## Common failure patterns
Silent scope growth, updating dates without rationale, treating every request as mandatory, and failing to update dependent teams.

## Verification
Compare current scope against the approved baseline and ensure every material delta has a recorded decision and impact assessment.

## Expected output
A traceable change record and synchronized program baseline.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when no authority owns the trade-off, impact cannot be estimated sufficiently, or the change creates unacceptable risk.
