# Stakeholder Alignment

## Purpose
Create durable alignment among engineering, product, operations, security, finance, vendors, and executives around decisions, priorities, and trade-offs.

## When to use
Use when a program spans organizations, has conflicting incentives, or depends on decisions from multiple accountable groups.

## Inputs
Stakeholder map, charter, program plan, open decisions, risks, business constraints.

## Context to inspect
Decision authority, incentives, prior commitments, escalation paths, meeting history, and unresolved objections.

## Core knowledge
Alignment is not universal agreement. Senior TPMs establish shared facts, explicit decision rights, and durable records of commitments while surfacing conflicts early.

## Procedure
1. Identify decision makers, contributors, approvers, and affected parties.
2. Map incentives, concerns, and constraints.
3. Separate factual disagreement from preference or ownership conflict.
4. Prepare decisions with options, trade-offs, and recommendation.
5. Resolve disagreements at the lowest effective level.
6. Record decisions, owners, and consequences.
7. Confirm downstream teams understand changed commitments.
8. Revisit alignment when assumptions materially change.

## Decision points
Escalate when decision authority is unclear or local incentives block program outcomes. Avoid broad consensus processes for reversible low-risk decisions.

## Common failure patterns
Status-heavy meetings, hidden dissent, ambiguous approval, late executive surprises, and decisions without recorded owners.

## Verification
Confirm key stakeholders can independently state the goal, current decision, responsibilities, and major trade-offs consistently.

## Expected output
Clear decisions, commitments, ownership, and a traceable alignment record.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when required authority is unavailable, stakeholder conflict threatens critical outcomes, or ethical/compliance concerns remain unresolved.
