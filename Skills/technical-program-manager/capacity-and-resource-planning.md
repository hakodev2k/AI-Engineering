# Capacity and Resource Planning

## Purpose
Align program commitments with realistic team capacity, specialized skill availability, operational load, and competing priorities.

## When to use
Use during planning, reforecasting, staffing changes, major incidents, or when multiple programs compete for the same technical teams.

## Inputs
Workstream estimates, staffing plans, on-call load, roadmap commitments, dependency demand, skill requirements.

## Context to inspect
Team calendars, hiring plans, maintenance obligations, historical throughput, PTO, operational burden, and shared platform demand.

## Core knowledge
Headcount is not interchangeable capacity. Senior TPMs account for specialization, ramp time, interrupt load, coordination cost, and critical single points of expertise.

## Procedure
1. Map workstreams to required teams and skills.
2. Estimate capacity ranges rather than nominal headcount.
3. Subtract planned operational and maintenance load.
4. Identify overloaded teams and scarce expertise.
5. Compare demand against committed priorities.
6. Model defer, descope, staff, or sequence alternatives.
7. Secure owner agreement on the chosen plan.
8. Reforecast when staffing or incident load changes materially.

## Decision points
Prefer scope or sequencing changes before assuming sustained overtime. Add people only when onboarding time and communication cost still produce net capacity.

## Common failure patterns
Counting people instead of effective capacity, ignoring on-call load, assuming immediate productivity from new staffing, and double-booking specialists.

## Verification
Confirm team leads agree with capacity assumptions and that critical-path work has named qualified ownership.

## Expected output
A capacity-backed plan with explicit constraints, trade-offs, and staffing risks.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when commitments exceed feasible capacity, required expertise is unavailable, or staffing decisions require executive authority.
