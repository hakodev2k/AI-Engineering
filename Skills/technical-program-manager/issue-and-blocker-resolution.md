# Issue and Blocker Resolution

## Purpose
Drive active technical and organizational blockers to resolution using evidence, ownership, deadlines, and appropriate escalation.

## When to use
Use when a current issue threatens milestones, integration, quality, availability, security, or cross-team execution.

## Inputs
Issue description, logs or evidence, affected owners, dependency context, impact, attempted mitigations.

## Context to inspect
Recent changes, incident history, ownership maps, decision records, dependencies, and escalation paths.

## Core knowledge
Senior TPMs do not solve every technical issue themselves; they create the conditions for rapid resolution. A blocker needs impact, accountable owner, next action, deadline, and escalation threshold.

## Procedure
1. Verify the blocker is current and materially affects the program.
2. Define impact and affected milestones.
3. Identify the technically accountable owner.
4. Gather minimum evidence and known hypotheses.
5. Define the next diagnostic or resolution action.
6. Set a time-bound checkpoint.
7. Remove coordination, access, or decision impediments.
8. Escalate based on impact and aging, not emotion.
9. Confirm resolution with evidence and update dependent plans.

## Decision points
Escalate immediately for security, safety, regulatory, or high-severity production impact. Keep routine technical investigation with the owning team.

## Common failure patterns
Blockers without owners, endless status updates, TPM prescribing unvalidated technical fixes, escalation too late, and closing issues without verification.

## Verification
Confirm the root blocking condition is removed and affected milestones or dependencies have been revalidated.

## Expected output
A resolved issue or a clear escalation package with evidence, impact, owner, and decision needed.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when required permissions, authority, expertise, or external vendor action is unavailable within the required timeframe.
