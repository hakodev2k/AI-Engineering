# Program Status and Executive Reporting

## Purpose
Produce concise, evidence-based program reporting that communicates outcomes, confidence, risks, decisions, and required executive action.

## When to use
Use for recurring program reviews, steering committees, launch readiness, or material changes in scope, schedule, risk, or cost.

## Inputs
Milestone evidence, dependency status, risks, issues, decisions, metrics, forecast, and owner updates.

## Context to inspect
Previous reports, committed baselines, executive concerns, decision history, and current technical evidence.

## Core knowledge
Senior reporting distinguishes facts, forecast, and interpretation. Green/yellow/red labels are insufficient without evidence, trend, impact, and action.

## Procedure
1. Compare current evidence to committed outcomes and milestones.
2. Summarize what materially changed since the prior update.
3. Report milestone confidence and critical-path movement.
4. Surface top risks and issues with owners and mitigations.
5. Identify decisions required and deadlines.
6. Quantify impact where possible.
7. Remove low-value activity details.
8. State the recommended executive action explicitly.
9. Preserve a dated record for trend analysis.

## Decision points
Use exception-based reporting for stable workstreams and deeper detail for deteriorating confidence or irreversible decisions.

## Common failure patterns
Optimistic status without evidence, burying decisions, reporting activity instead of outcomes, stale risk lists, and inconsistent metrics.

## Verification
Ensure every material claim traces to evidence and every requested action has an owner and deadline.

## Expected output
A decision-oriented status report that enables leaders to understand program health quickly.

## Stop conditions
Stop and validate before publishing when data conflicts materially, milestone confidence is unsupported, or sensitive information has unclear audience authorization.
