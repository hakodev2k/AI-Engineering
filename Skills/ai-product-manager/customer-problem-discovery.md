# Customer Problem Discovery

## Purpose
Discover AI-relevant customer problems before committing to a model, feature, or workflow.

## When to use
Use for new opportunities, weak adoption, unclear product-market fit, or when a proposed AI feature is technology-led rather than problem-led.

## Inputs
Target segments, interview access, support data, usage analytics, sales notes, current workflows, known constraints.

## Context to inspect
Existing research, churn reasons, feature requests, workarounds, task frequency, economic value, user risk tolerance, and current non-AI alternatives.

## Core knowledge
Good AI opportunities usually involve costly cognitive work, unstructured inputs, high variance, or repeated judgment. User tolerance for errors depends on consequence severity and reviewability.

## Procedure
1. Define the target segment and job-to-be-done.
2. Interview users about current behavior, not hypothetical preferences.
3. Map workflow steps, decisions, handoffs, delays, and failure costs.
4. Quantify task frequency, time, money, risk, and emotional friction.
5. Identify where automation, assistance, retrieval, prediction, or generation could help.
6. Record acceptable error rates and required human oversight.
7. Rank problems by value, urgency, feasibility, and strategic fit.
8. Convert the strongest problems into testable opportunity statements.

## Decision points
Prioritize painful, frequent, measurable problems. Avoid AI where the workflow is rare, low-value, or demands deterministic correctness without verification.

## Common failure patterns
Leading interviews, asking users to design the solution, over-weighting vocal customers, and treating feature requests as validated needs.

## Verification
Confirm findings across multiple evidence sources and test whether users already spend meaningful time or money solving the problem.

## Expected output
Prioritized opportunity statements with evidence, user workflow, value, risk tolerance, and unresolved assumptions.

## Stop conditions
Stop when the segment cannot be identified, evidence conflicts materially, or the proposed problem lacks measurable user value.
