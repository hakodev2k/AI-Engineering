# AI Product Strategy

## Purpose
Define a defensible AI product strategy that links customer value, model capability, data advantage, delivery constraints, risk, and economics.

## When to use
Use when setting product direction, evaluating an AI opportunity, entering a new market, or revising strategy after model or market changes. Do not use as a substitute for customer evidence.

## Inputs
Business goals, target users, problem statements, market context, model options, data assets, technical constraints, cost targets, risk constraints.

## Context to inspect
Current roadmap, customer research, product metrics, model evaluations, data availability, competitive alternatives, legal/security constraints, serving costs.

## Core knowledge
AI products are constrained by probabilistic behavior, data quality, model drift, inference cost, latency, safety, and changing provider capabilities. Strategy must distinguish durable user value from temporary model novelty.

## Procedure
1. Define target customer and high-value problem.
2. Separate user outcome from AI implementation.
3. Identify where AI creates a meaningful advantage over deterministic software.
4. Assess required model capabilities and data dependencies.
5. Estimate unit economics and operational constraints.
6. Identify trust, safety, privacy, and regulatory risks.
7. Compare build, buy, fine-tune, and workflow alternatives.
8. Define strategic bets, non-goals, success metrics, and review triggers.
9. Stress-test the strategy against model commoditization and competitor parity.

## Decision points
Prefer AI where probabilistic automation or generation materially improves the outcome. Prefer conventional software where rules are stable, exactness is mandatory, or AI adds little differentiated value.

## Common failure patterns
AI-first problem selection, vague customer value, ignoring inference economics, betting on a single provider capability, and confusing demo quality with product-market fit.

## Verification
Verify that the strategy has measurable outcomes, explicit assumptions, a credible delivery path, and clear reasons why AI is necessary.

## Expected output
A concise AI product strategy with target users, value thesis, capability requirements, risks, economics, strategic choices, and review criteria.

## Stop conditions
Stop and escalate when the use case depends on unavailable data, unacceptable safety risk, prohibited data use, or economics that cannot meet required margins.
