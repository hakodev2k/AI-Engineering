# Intelligence Analysis

## Purpose
Produce defensible judgments from incomplete, conflicting evidence using structured reasoning and calibrated uncertainty.

## When to use
Use for tactical, operational, or strategic assessments where evidence does not directly answer the intelligence question.

## Inputs
PIR, collected evidence, source assessments, timelines, competing explanations, stakeholder decision context.

## Context to inspect
Separate facts, assumptions, gaps, historical baselines, source dependencies, and stakeholder consequence of error.

## Core knowledge
Analysis is inference under uncertainty. Confidence reflects evidence quality and consistency, not impact or probability; probability and confidence should not be conflated.

## Procedure
1. Restate the intelligence question precisely.
2. Build a fact base with provenance.
3. Identify assumptions and gaps.
4. Generate plausible hypotheses.
5. Seek diagnostic evidence and disconfirming signals.
6. Compare hypotheses using structured criteria.
7. Form the narrowest judgment supported by evidence.
8. Express probability and confidence consistently.
9. State implications, indicators to watch, and what could change the judgment.
10. Peer review consequential assessments.

## Decision points
Use structured analytic techniques when bias risk, ambiguity, or consequence is high; use lighter methods for routine low-impact questions.

## Common failure patterns
Confirmation bias, evidence counting instead of weighting, hidden assumptions, narrative coherence mistaken for truth, and false precision.

## Verification
A reviewer can reproduce the evidence chain, understand alternatives, and distinguish observed facts from analytic judgments.

## Expected output
Concise assessment with key judgment, evidence, alternatives, confidence, implications, and gaps.

## Stop conditions
Stop when evidence is insufficient for the requested precision; provide bounded uncertainty rather than inventing certainty.
