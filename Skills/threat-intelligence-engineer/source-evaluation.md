# Source Evaluation

## Purpose
Assess source credibility, information reliability, bias, timeliness, and operational value before using intelligence.

## When to use
Use for new feeds, reports, anonymous claims, social sources, leaked data, or conflicting reporting.

## Inputs
Source material, provenance, historical accuracy, corroborating evidence, timestamps, collection method.

## Context to inspect
Check original publication, intermediaries, incentives, prior reporting, technical artifacts, and independent sources.

## Core knowledge
Source reliability and information credibility are separate judgments. Corroboration, provenance, recency, and access matter more than reputation alone.

## Procedure
1. Trace the claim to the earliest obtainable source.
2. Separate firsthand evidence from repetition.
3. Assess source access, competence, incentives, and track record.
4. Validate timestamps and technical artifacts.
5. Seek independent corroboration.
6. Identify contradictions and missing context.
7. Assign confidence with explicit reasons.
8. Preserve provenance and caveats downstream.

## Decision points
Use low-confidence reporting as a lead, not a fact. Raise confidence only with independent evidence, not multiple outlets repeating one origin.

## Common failure patterns
Circular reporting, authority bias, screenshot-only evidence, stale indicators, mistranslation, and collapsing source reliability into claim certainty.

## Verification
Another analyst can reproduce provenance, distinguish corroboration from repetition, and understand the confidence judgment.

## Expected output
Source assessment with provenance, reliability, corroboration, caveats, and confidence.

## Stop conditions
Stop attribution or escalation when provenance cannot be established and the consequence of error is material.
