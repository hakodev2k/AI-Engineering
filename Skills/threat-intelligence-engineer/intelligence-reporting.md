# Intelligence Reporting

## Purpose
Deliver intelligence products that are concise, evidence-based, audience-appropriate, and directly tied to decisions or defensive actions.

## When to use
Use for alerts, campaign reports, executive briefs, strategic assessments, or recurring threat updates.

## Inputs
Analytic judgments, evidence, PIR, audience, urgency, distribution constraints, recommended actions.

## Context to inspect
Understand consumer expertise, decision horizon, classification/sensitivity, prior reporting, and operational ownership.

## Core knowledge
Lead with the judgment and implication, not the research journey. Separate facts, assessments, and recommendations; preserve provenance without overwhelming readers.

## Procedure
1. Define audience and decision.
2. Select only evidence relevant to the PIR.
3. Write key judgments first.
4. State why each judgment matters.
5. Add concise supporting evidence and confidence.
6. Provide prioritized actions with owners when appropriate.
7. Include indicators/technical detail in appendices or machine-readable form.
8. Mark sensitivity and handling constraints.
9. Peer review factual accuracy and analytic language.
10. Capture consumer feedback.

## Decision points
Use an alert for urgent actionable change, a tactical report for defenders, and a strategic assessment for longer-horizon decisions.

## Common failure patterns
Chronological data dumps, unexplained jargon, unsupported recommendations, buried confidence, and reports with no consumer action.

## Verification
Consumer can identify the key judgment, confidence, implication, and next action without analyst explanation.

## Expected output
Audience-specific intelligence product with key judgments, evidence, confidence, implications, and actions.

## Stop conditions
Stop publication when material facts are unverified, handling rules are unclear, or disclosure could compromise an active investigation.
