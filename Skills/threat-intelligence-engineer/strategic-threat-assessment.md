# Strategic Threat Assessment

## Purpose
Assess longer-term adversary intent, capability, opportunity, and environmental change to support security strategy and investment.

## When to use
Use for annual planning, market/geography expansion, major technology shifts, M&A, or material changes in the threat landscape.

## Inputs
Business strategy, critical assets, historical incidents, actor/campaign trends, geopolitical and technology developments, control posture.

## Context to inspect
Review business dependencies, geographic exposure, sector targeting, supply chain, technology roadmap, and plausible threat scenarios.

## Core knowledge
Strategic intelligence should explain change over time and implications, not summarize news. Separate capability from intent and threat from vulnerability.

## Procedure
1. Define decision horizon and strategic questions.
2. Establish baseline threat and exposure.
3. Identify relevant actors and structural drivers.
4. Analyze capability, intent, access, and opportunity separately.
5. Develop plausible scenarios and indicators.
6. Assess business impact and control dependencies.
7. Compare risk trajectories rather than point estimates.
8. Recommend strategic options and trade-offs.
9. Define watch indicators and review triggers.

## Decision points
Use scenarios when uncertainty is high; use trend assessment when evidence supports directional change. Avoid forecasting beyond evidence-supported horizons.

## Common failure patterns
News aggregation, geopolitical speculation detached from assets, static risk scores, and recommendations unrelated to business decisions.

## Verification
Stakeholders can connect each judgment to a business exposure and understand indicators that would change the assessment.

## Expected output
Strategic assessment with key judgments, scenarios, risk trajectory, indicators, and decision options.

## Stop conditions
Stop when business assumptions are unavailable or geopolitical/legal sensitivity requires specialist review.
