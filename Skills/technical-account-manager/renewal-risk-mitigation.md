# Renewal Risk Mitigation

## Purpose
Identify and reduce technical risks that could undermine renewal, expansion, or long-term customer confidence without turning the TAM role into commercial negotiation.

## When to use
Use months before renewal, after repeated incidents, during stalled adoption, or when technical dissatisfaction is reported.

## Inputs
Renewal timeline, health indicators, usage, incidents, support history, unresolved product gaps, success outcomes, and stakeholder feedback.

## Context to inspect
Critical blockers, adoption depth, reliability, unmet commitments, architecture debt, executive sentiment, competitive technical concerns, and dependency on roadmap items.

## Core knowledge
Technical renewal risk usually accumulates before it becomes explicit. Senior TAMs separate fixable execution gaps from structural product-fit issues and communicate uncertainty early.

## Procedure
1. Establish the renewal decision timeline and technical decision makers.
2. Review evidence across adoption, support, reliability, and outcome attainment.
3. Rank risks by impact, recoverability, and time to remediate.
4. Define corrective actions with owners and proof points.
5. Escalate structural product gaps to the correct internal teams.
6. Avoid promising unavailable features or dates.
7. Create customer-visible checkpoints for recovery progress.
8. Document residual risks before commercial decisions are due.

## Decision points
Prioritize recoverable high-impact technical blockers first. Escalate product-fit gaps when remediation requires capability the product does not currently provide.

## Common failure patterns
Discovering risk too late, masking poor health with meeting activity, overpromising roadmap changes, and treating commercial pressure as evidence.

## Verification
Confirm mitigation actions changed measurable account-health indicators and that customer stakeholders acknowledge progress.

## Expected output
A technical renewal-risk plan with evidence, priorities, actions, owners, proof points, and residual risk.

## Stop conditions
Stop when the issue is primarily legal, pricing, contractual, or requires unauthorized product commitments.
