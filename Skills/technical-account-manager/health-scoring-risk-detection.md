# Health Scoring and Risk Detection

## Purpose
Build a defensible technical account-health view using adoption, reliability, support, architecture, stakeholder, and delivery evidence to surface risk before renewal or escalation.

## When to use
Use for portfolio reviews, strategic-account governance, renewal preparation, or when account sentiment diverges from technical evidence.

## Inputs
Usage telemetry, incident history, support cases, success-plan progress, stakeholder engagement, architecture findings, project status, and customer feedback.

## Context to inspect
Trend direction, critical workloads, unresolved risks, case aging, adoption concentration, single-threaded relationships, roadmap dependencies, and recent organizational changes.

## Core knowledge
Health scores are decision aids, not truth. Senior TAMs avoid arbitrary averages and distinguish leading indicators from lagging outcomes.

## Procedure
1. Define health dimensions relevant to the account.
2. Choose evidence-based indicators and thresholds.
3. Normalize indicators without hiding critical red flags.
4. Compare current values with historical trends.
5. Record qualitative context separately from measured data.
6. Identify the few risks most likely to change customer outcomes.
7. Assign mitigation, owner, and review date.
8. Recalibrate indicators when they stop predicting real outcomes.

## Decision points
Override aggregate scoring when a single high-impact risk dominates. Treat missing data as uncertainty, not automatically as healthy or unhealthy.

## Common failure patterns
Using sentiment alone, averaging away critical incidents, gaming metrics, and creating scores without action thresholds.

## Verification
Back-test the score against known account events and confirm each red or amber indicator has traceable evidence and action.

## Expected output
A health assessment with trends, evidence, prioritized risks, mitigations, and explicit uncertainty.

## Stop conditions
Stop when data quality is too weak for defensible scoring or when sensitive commercial judgments require authorized ownership.
