# Executive Business Reviews

## Purpose
Prepare and run executive reviews that connect technical performance, adoption, risk, and roadmap alignment to customer outcomes without drowning leaders in operational detail.

## When to use
Use for quarterly or strategic reviews, major milestones, recovery plans, and renewal preparation.

## Inputs
Success plan, account health, adoption metrics, incidents, support themes, architecture risks, roadmap context, and agreed business outcomes.

## Context to inspect
What changed since the last review, measurable value delivered, unresolved risks, executive decisions needed, and discrepancies between operational and executive perceptions.

## Core knowledge
Executives need evidence, decisions, and implications. Senior TAMs summarize technical complexity accurately while preserving enough traceability for follow-up.

## Procedure
1. Restate the agreed outcomes and prior commitments.
2. Select a small set of metrics that demonstrate progress or risk.
3. Summarize major technical achievements and incidents with business impact.
4. Highlight unresolved risks and decisions requiring sponsorship.
5. Present roadmap topics as directional unless officially committed.
6. Align next-period priorities to measurable outcomes.
7. Capture decisions, owners, and deadlines during the review.
8. Distribute a concise follow-up record and update the success plan.

## Decision points
Move detailed troubleshooting to working sessions. Elevate only risks or decisions that genuinely require executive authority.

## Common failure patterns
Turning the review into a support-ticket recap, hiding bad news, presenting vanity metrics, and implying roadmap guarantees.

## Verification
Confirm every claim has evidence and every requested executive decision has a clear consequence and owner.

## Expected output
An executive-ready review with outcomes, evidence, risks, decisions, and next priorities.

## Stop conditions
Stop when metrics cannot be substantiated, confidential topics lack authorization, or commercial claims exceed the TAM's remit.
