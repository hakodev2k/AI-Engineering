# Success Metrics and ROI Evidence

## Purpose
Define technical and adoption metrics that demonstrate whether the customer is realizing intended outcomes and provide defensible evidence for value discussions.

## When to use
Use when building a success plan, preparing executive reviews, validating adoption, or investigating whether technical work is producing business value.

## Inputs
Business outcomes, usage telemetry, operational metrics, baseline costs or effort, success criteria, and customer measurement methods.

## Context to inspect
Current baselines, metric definitions, data quality, attribution limits, seasonality, adoption cohorts, reliability, and external factors that may affect outcomes.

## Core knowledge
Correlation is not causation. Senior TAMs avoid fabricated ROI and distinguish product telemetry, operational savings, business outcomes, and qualitative evidence.

## Procedure
1. Start from the customer outcome, not available telemetry.
2. Define leading and lagging indicators.
3. Establish baseline and measurement window.
4. Confirm data source, owner, and calculation method.
5. Identify confounders and attribution limits.
6. Track adoption and technical health alongside outcome metrics.
7. Compare observed change with the baseline and agreed target.
8. Present uncertainty explicitly and avoid unsupported financial claims.
9. Adjust metrics when they no longer represent value.

## Decision points
Use proxy metrics only when direct outcomes cannot be measured and label them clearly. Prefer customer-owned business metrics for ROI claims.

## Common failure patterns
Using vanity metrics, claiming causality from correlation, changing definitions midstream, and extrapolating financial benefit without customer validation.

## Verification
Recalculate metrics from source data, validate definitions with stakeholders, and confirm reported improvements survive reasonable sensitivity checks.

## Expected output
A metric framework with baselines, targets, evidence sources, attribution limits, and measured outcomes.

## Stop conditions
Stop when data quality is insufficient, financial interpretation requires unauthorized assumptions, or stakeholders cannot agree on metric definitions.
