# Cross-Functional Escalation

## Purpose
Escalate customer-impacting technical issues across support, engineering, product, security, services, and account teams with enough evidence and prioritization context to drive action without bypassing ownership.

## When to use
Use when normal workflows are blocked, impact is material, multiple internal teams are required, or a systemic issue threatens customer outcomes.

## Inputs
Customer impact, technical evidence, case history, current owner, attempted actions, deadlines, dependencies, and escalation criteria.

## Context to inspect
Existing support or engineering ownership, severity, business impact, reproduction quality, duplicate efforts, policy constraints, prior commitments, and decision authority.

## Core knowledge
Good escalation increases signal and clarifies decisions. Senior TAMs do not use escalation as a substitute for incomplete diagnostics, and they avoid creating parallel ownership chains.

## Procedure
1. State the customer impact in measurable terms.
2. Summarize confirmed technical facts and unresolved hypotheses separately.
3. Identify the current owner and exact blocker.
4. Attach relevant diagnostics, timelines, and previous actions.
5. Specify the decision or assistance required from the receiving team.
6. Route through the documented escalation path.
7. Maintain a single source of truth for status and ownership.
8. Reassess severity as impact changes.
9. Close the escalation only after the requested decision or remediation path is established.

## Decision points
Escalate for impact, risk, or blocked progress—not visibility alone. Involve product when the issue is a capability gap; involve engineering when reproducible behavior suggests a defect.

## Common failure patterns
Executive escalation without evidence, duplicate channels, unclear asks, bypassing support, and treating roadmap requests as incidents.

## Verification
Confirm the receiving team acknowledges ownership or the requested decision and that the customer-facing plan reflects the latest evidence.

## Expected output
A concise escalation package with impact, evidence, owner, blocker, requested action, and status path.

## Stop conditions
Stop when the issue enters a formal security, legal, or incident-command process that supersedes normal escalation governance.
