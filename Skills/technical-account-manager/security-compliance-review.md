# Security and Compliance Review

## Purpose
Coordinate a technically accurate review of security controls, data handling, identity, network boundaries, and compliance obligations without making unsupported legal or certification claims.

## When to use
Use during onboarding, security assessment, regulated deployments, architecture changes, audits, or renewal due diligence.

## Inputs
Security requirements, data classification, architecture, identity model, encryption controls, compliance scope, product documentation, and customer questionnaires.

## Context to inspect
Authentication, authorization, secrets, encryption, logging, retention, residency, administrative access, vulnerability management, shared-responsibility boundaries, and approved attestations.

## Core knowledge
A Senior TAM distinguishes product capability from customer configuration and legal compliance. Compliance is contextual; documentation and specialist validation matter.

## Procedure
1. Clarify the security or compliance question and applicable scope.
2. Map customer requirements to documented controls and responsibilities.
3. Identify configuration-dependent controls and evidence needed.
4. Validate identity, access, encryption, logging, and data-flow assumptions.
5. Use approved security artifacts for attestations and certifications.
6. Route legal or policy interpretation to authorized specialists.
7. Track gaps with owners, severity, and remediation evidence.
8. Revalidate controls after material architecture changes.

## Decision points
Answer directly only when authoritative documentation supports the claim. Escalate ambiguous regulatory interpretation, exception requests, or undisclosed vulnerabilities.

## Common failure patterns
Overstating certification coverage, confusing encryption availability with correct configuration, exposing sensitive security details, or treating questionnaires as copy-paste exercises.

## Verification
Confirm every material response is traceable to authoritative evidence or an approved specialist and that customer-specific controls are tested where possible.

## Expected output
A documented security review with evidence, responsibility boundaries, gaps, and escalations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when requested information is restricted, legal interpretation is required, or a critical security gap demands formal escalation.
