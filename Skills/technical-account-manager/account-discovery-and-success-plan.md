# Account Discovery and Success Plan

## Purpose
Build an evidence-based technical success plan that connects customer outcomes, architecture, adoption milestones, risks, owners, and measurable checkpoints.

## When to use
Use at account kickoff, after a major expansion, during a recovery plan, or when objectives have become unclear. Do not substitute this for a commercial account plan.

## Inputs
Customer objectives, deployed products, architecture, stakeholders, support history, adoption data, contractual commitments, renewal timeline, and known risks.

## Preconditions
Access to relevant account context and agreement on who can validate business and technical goals.

## Context to inspect
Current architecture, integration dependencies, production criticality, operating model, support posture, adoption gaps, unresolved escalations, and prior commitments.

## Core knowledge
A Senior TAM converts vague success language into verifiable technical outcomes. The plan must separate goals, leading indicators, dependencies, risks, and ownership.

## Procedure
1. Identify the customer outcomes that justify the technology investment.
2. Translate each outcome into observable technical capabilities or adoption milestones.
3. Establish current-state evidence and baseline metrics.
4. Map dependencies, decision makers, operators, and blockers.
5. Define milestones with owners and target dates.
6. Record material risks with mitigation and escalation paths.
7. Align product capabilities and roadmap assumptions without overcommitting.
8. Validate the plan with customer and internal stakeholders.
9. Review progress on a recurring cadence and update only from evidence.

## Decision points
Use a milestone plan for concrete delivery work; use an outcome plan when success depends on adoption or operating change. Escalate when contractual expectations conflict with product capability.

## Common failure patterns
Treating the plan as a meeting log, listing activities instead of outcomes, hiding risks, assigning owners without agreement, or promising roadmap features.

## Verification
Confirm every objective has a measurable indicator, owner, dependency view, and next checkpoint. Validate that customer stakeholders agree with the current state.

## Expected output
A concise technical success plan with outcomes, milestones, risks, owners, evidence, and review cadence.

## Stop conditions
Stop and escalate when objectives cannot be validated, required stakeholders are unavailable, or success depends on unsupported product commitments.
