# Stakeholder Mapping

## Purpose
Identify the technical, operational, executive, security, procurement, and end-user stakeholders who influence account success and define how each should be engaged.

## When to use
Use during onboarding, organizational change, escalation, expansion, or renewal preparation.

## Inputs
Org charts, meeting history, support contacts, project ownership, decision records, escalation paths, and customer goals.

## Context to inspect
Who owns budget, architecture, operations, security approval, implementation, support, and executive sponsorship; also inspect missing or single-threaded relationships.

## Core knowledge
Enterprise outcomes depend on influence networks, not just formal titles. A Senior TAM distinguishes decision authority, technical authority, operational ownership, champions, blockers, and affected users.

## Procedure
1. List known stakeholders and their responsibilities.
2. Classify influence, decision authority, technical depth, and level of engagement.
3. Map stakeholders to success-plan outcomes and risks.
4. Identify gaps such as missing security, operations, or executive sponsorship.
5. Determine the appropriate communication cadence and artifact for each audience.
6. Establish backup contacts for critical functions.
7. Validate assumptions through customer interactions.
8. Update the map after reorgs, escalations, or major project changes.

## Decision points
Engage executives for outcome, risk, and governance topics; engage practitioners for implementation and evidence. Avoid unnecessary escalation when working-level owners can resolve the issue.

## Common failure patterns
Relying on a single champion, confusing title with influence, excluding operations or security, and contacting executives only during crises.

## Verification
Confirm critical decisions and operational areas have named owners and that communication channels are current.

## Expected output
A maintained stakeholder map with roles, influence, ownership, engagement strategy, and relationship gaps.

## Stop conditions
Stop and escalate when ownership is disputed, critical functions have no accountable contact, or engagement is restricted by customer governance.
