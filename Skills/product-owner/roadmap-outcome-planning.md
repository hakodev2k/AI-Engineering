# Outcome-based Roadmap Planning

## Purpose
Create a roadmap that communicates strategic outcomes, sequencing, and uncertainty without converting uncertain future work into false delivery promises.

## When to use
Use for quarterly planning, cross-team coordination, stakeholder communication, and strategy translation.

## Inputs
Strategy, product goals, opportunity evidence, dependencies, constraints, capacity ranges, and existing commitments.

## Context to inspect
Inspect strategic horizons, market or customer deadlines, technical dependencies, discovery maturity, and confidence of estimates.

## Core knowledge
Roadmaps should emphasize problems and outcomes, with confidence decreasing over time. Dates are appropriate for genuine constraints, not as decoration for speculative scope.

## Procedure
1. Translate strategy into outcome themes.
2. Gather evidence and constraints for each theme.
3. Separate committed, planned, and exploratory work.
4. Identify dependencies and sequencing logic.
5. Express uncertainty explicitly.
6. Use date ranges or horizons proportional to confidence.
7. Define measures for each outcome.
8. Align with delivery and stakeholder teams.
9. Update when evidence or capacity changes.
10. Preserve decision history rather than silently moving promises.

## Decision points
Use fixed dates for external obligations; use horizons for uncertain discovery. Prefer fewer outcome themes over detailed feature inventories.

## Common failure patterns
Feature roadmaps disguised as strategy, precise distant dates, no capacity trade-offs, roadmap changes without communication, and measuring success by roadmap completion.

## Verification
Every roadmap item maps to strategy, uncertainty is visible, dependencies are understood, and stakeholders know which items are commitments versus hypotheses.

## Expected output
An outcome-oriented roadmap with sequencing, confidence, measures, and constraints.

## Stop conditions
Stop when strategy is not sufficiently defined or leadership requires incompatible commitments without resolving trade-offs.
