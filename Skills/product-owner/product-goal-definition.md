# Product Goal Definition

## Purpose
Turn strategy and user problems into a concrete product goal that guides backlog decisions and team trade-offs.

## When to use
Use at the start of a product objective, when priorities drift, or when a backlog contains unrelated work.

## Inputs
Product strategy, user problems, business objectives, baseline metrics, constraints, and stakeholder expectations.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current outcomes, customer evidence, existing commitments, strategic boundaries, and measurement capability.

## Core knowledge
A useful product goal describes a valuable outcome rather than a feature list. It should be specific enough to guide choices while leaving solution space to the team.

## Procedure
1. Identify the target user or business problem.
2. Establish baseline evidence and why the problem matters now.
3. Define the desired observable outcome.
4. Select leading and lagging indicators where possible.
5. State important constraints and non-goals.
6. Test whether multiple solutions could satisfy the goal.
7. Validate alignment with strategy and stakeholders.
8. Communicate the goal with decision criteria.
9. Link backlog work to the goal.
10. Review progress using evidence, not completion percentage alone.

## Decision points
Prefer outcome metrics over output counts. If measurement is weak, define a learning goal and instrumentation before committing to a large solution.

## Common failure patterns
Goals that are feature lists, vague aspirations, conflicting metrics, missing baseline, and goals that cannot influence prioritization.

## Verification
Ask whether the team can use the goal to reject unrelated work and whether success can be observed independently of shipping a named feature.

## Expected output
A concise product goal with target outcome, evidence, metrics, constraints, and non-goals.

## Stop conditions
Stop when strategy is contradictory, success cannot be defined, or the required business decision belongs to accountable leadership.
