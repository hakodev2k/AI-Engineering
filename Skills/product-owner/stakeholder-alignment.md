# Stakeholder Alignment

## Purpose
Build durable alignment around product goals, priorities, constraints, and decisions while preserving a single coherent backlog.

## When to use
Use when stakeholders disagree, requests compete, decisions have cross-functional impact, or expectations are drifting.

## Inputs
Stakeholder map, goals, evidence, constraints, roadmap, backlog, decision history, and unresolved conflicts.

## Context to inspect
Inspect decision rights, incentives, affected teams, customer commitments, dependencies, and prior agreements.

## Core knowledge
Alignment does not mean unanimous preference. It means shared understanding of objectives, decision criteria, ownership, and consequences. Product Owners should surface conflict early rather than hide it in backlog ordering.

## Procedure
1. Identify decision makers, contributors, and affected parties.
2. Clarify the decision and its deadline.
3. Re-anchor discussion on product goals and evidence.
4. Separate facts, assumptions, preferences, and constraints.
5. Make trade-offs and opportunity costs visible.
6. Seek options that satisfy the underlying needs.
7. Apply agreed decision rights when consensus is unavailable.
8. Document the decision and rationale.
9. Communicate implications to affected teams.
10. Reopen only when new material evidence appears.

## Decision points
Use collaborative workshops for complex shared problems; use accountable-owner decisions when delay costs exceed the value of further consensus.

## Common failure patterns
Private promises, stakeholder voting without strategy, endless consensus seeking, unclear decision authority, and treating disagreement as a communication failure rather than a trade-off.

## Verification
Relevant stakeholders understand the decision, rationale, impact, and escalation path even if they preferred another option.

## Expected output
A documented, communicated product decision with clear ownership and consequences.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when decision rights are disputed or contractual, legal, financial, or executive commitments exceed Product Owner authority.
