# Product Cost and Value Analysis

## Purpose
Evaluate product choices using expected value, implementation and operating cost, risk, reversibility, and lifecycle impact rather than feature desirability alone.

## When to use
Use for expensive features, vendor choices, build-versus-buy discussions, high-cloud-cost capabilities, and competing solution options.

## Inputs
Expected outcomes, affected users, revenue or savings assumptions, delivery estimates, operating cost, vendor terms, risk, and alternatives.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current cost baseline, support burden, scale assumptions, lock-in, migration cost, maintenance ownership, and uncertainty in value estimates.

## Core knowledge
Product economics includes total cost of ownership and option value. Estimates should be ranges with assumptions; sunk cost should not justify continuing weak investments.

## Procedure
1. Define the decision and alternatives, including doing nothing.
2. Estimate user/business value with evidence confidence.
3. Gather delivery and recurring cost ranges.
4. Include support, operations, migration, and exit costs.
5. Identify major risks and irreversible commitments.
6. Compare time-to-value and learning speed.
7. Test sensitivity to uncertain assumptions.
8. Prefer smaller validation when uncertainty dominates.
9. Document rationale and expected review point.
10. Compare actual value and cost after delivery.

## Decision points
Build when differentiation and control justify ownership; buy when commodity capability and speed dominate; defer when value uncertainty is high relative to cost.

## Common failure patterns
Ignoring operating cost, false precision, excluding opportunity cost, sunk-cost reasoning, and assuming vendor price is total cost.

## Verification
The decision compares realistic alternatives, includes lifecycle cost, states assumptions, and has a measurable value hypothesis.

## Expected output
A product investment decision with value range, cost range, risks, assumptions, and review trigger.

## Stop conditions
Escalate material financial commitments, contractual obligations, or accounting interpretations to authorized owners.
