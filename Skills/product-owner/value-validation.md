# Value Validation

## Purpose
Determine whether a delivered capability creates meaningful user or business value rather than merely achieving adoption or completion.

## When to use
Use after MVPs, major releases, experiments, pilots, and investments whose expected value drove prioritization.

## Inputs
Original value hypothesis, baseline, product metrics, user feedback, business outcomes, costs, and guardrail metrics.

## Context to inspect
Inspect target segment, exposure, adoption funnel, retention, task outcomes, support signals, unintended effects, and measurement limitations.

## Core knowledge
Usage is not automatically value. Validation should compare actual outcomes with the original hypothesis and consider counterfactual explanations, selection bias, novelty effects, and operating cost.

## Procedure
1. Retrieve the original hypothesis and success criteria.
2. Confirm sufficient exposure and observation time.
3. Compare target metrics with baseline.
4. Segment adoption and outcome differences.
5. Examine qualitative evidence for why behavior changed.
6. Review guardrails and unintended consequences.
7. Compare realized value with lifecycle cost.
8. Assess confidence and alternative explanations.
9. Decide to scale, improve, reposition, maintain, or retire.
10. Record learning for future prioritization.

## Decision points
Scale when value is repeatable and guardrails remain healthy; iterate when value exists but friction limits realization; stop when evidence remains weak relative to ongoing cost.

## Common failure patterns
Success equals shipped, cherry-picking metrics, ignoring non-adopters, measuring too early, and continuing because of sunk cost.

## Verification
The decision references pre-defined outcomes, multiple evidence sources where appropriate, and explicit uncertainty.

## Expected output
A post-delivery value assessment and clear next investment decision.

## Stop conditions
Stop when instrumentation is too weak to support a conclusion; define the missing evidence rather than inventing certainty.
