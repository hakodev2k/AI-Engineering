# Culturalization and Content Adaptation

## Purpose
Adapt AI-generated and static product content so it is culturally appropriate without changing the product’s intended meaning, policy, or brand position.

## When to use
Use when examples, metaphors, tone, names, imagery descriptions, workflows, or recommendations do not transfer cleanly between markets.

## Inputs
Source content, target locales, brand guidance, user research, policy constraints, domain terminology, and market feedback.

## Preconditions
The invariant business and policy intent is known.

## Context to inspect
Inspect prompts, templates, onboarding, generated examples, recommendations, help content, safety language, and regional product conventions.

## Core knowledge
Culturalization goes beyond translation. Relevance, politeness, directness, symbolism, examples, social norms, and expectations of authority vary by market. Adaptation must not introduce stereotypes or silently alter policy.

## Procedure
1. Identify content with cultural assumptions.
2. Separate invariant meaning from adaptable presentation.
3. Review target-market norms and user research.
4. Replace examples and references that create confusion or exclusion.
5. Validate tone, honorifics, directness, and social context.
6. Check adaptations against policy and brand constraints.
7. Test with native reviewers and representative users.
8. Record approved deviations from source content.

## Decision points
Adapt when local relevance improves comprehension or trust; retain global wording when legal, safety, or product semantics must remain identical.

## Common failure patterns
Stereotyping, literal idiom translation, inappropriate humor, changing policy meaning for naturalness, and relying on a single reviewer as cultural authority.

## Verification
Native reviewers confirm meaning, tone, relevance, and policy fidelity; user testing shows no material comprehension regression.

## Expected output
Approved locale adaptations with rationale and source-to-target intent traceability.

## Stop conditions
Stop when cultural guidance conflicts with safety, law, or authoritative product requirements and ownership is unresolved.
