# Project Communications

## Purpose
Create concise, decision-oriented communication that gives each audience the information needed to act without hiding uncertainty or flooding them with noise.

## When to use
Use for status reporting, governance reviews, milestone updates, risk communication, and cross-team coordination.

## Inputs
Current plan, forecast, risks, issues, decisions, milestones, metrics, stakeholder needs, and prior commitments.

## Context to inspect
Review audience authority, information sensitivity, required cadence, reporting standards, and which decisions are pending.

## Core knowledge
Good project communication separates facts, forecasts, assumptions, risks, decisions, and asks. A red status with a recovery plan is more useful than a misleading green status.

## Procedure
1. Identify the audience and decisions the communication should enable.
2. Report outcome and milestone health against agreed baselines.
3. Explain material variance and forecast changes.
4. Highlight top risks, issues, dependencies, and mitigations.
5. State decisions required, owner, and deadline.
6. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and projections.
7. Tailor detail while preserving a single source of truth.
8. Record decisions and commitments from governance forums.
9. Distribute updates in time for action.
10. Remove metrics and sections that do not drive decisions.

## Decision points
Use dashboards for recurring measurable health; narrative for causality and trade-offs; synchronous meetings for conflict or high-impact decisions.

## Common failure patterns
Activity lists instead of outcomes, traffic-light reporting without evidence, burying bad news, inconsistent numbers across audiences, and meetings without decisions.

## Verification
Recipients can identify current forecast, major variance, top exposure, and decisions required without seeking another explanation.

## Expected output
Audience-appropriate status and governance communication backed by traceable evidence.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when reporting requirements force material misrepresentation or sensitive information lacks an authorized channel.
