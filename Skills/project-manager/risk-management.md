# Risk Management

## Purpose
Identify, quantify, treat, and monitor uncertainty that could materially affect project outcomes.

## When to use
Use throughout initiation, planning, execution, major change, and before irreversible commitments.

## Inputs
Project objectives, assumptions, dependencies, estimates, architecture constraints, stakeholder concerns, incident history, and external conditions.

## Context to inspect
Inspect commercial, technical, security, regulatory, operational, resource, schedule, vendor, and organizational risks.

## Core knowledge
A risk is uncertain; an issue has already occurred. Risk exposure combines likelihood and impact but may also require urgency, detectability, correlation, and tail-risk judgment. Mitigation should reduce probability or impact; contingency prepares a response if the risk occurs.

## Procedure
1. Elicit risks from delivery owners and specialists.
2. Write each risk as cause, uncertain event, and consequence.
3. Assess probability, impact, proximity, and affected objectives.
4. Identify correlated and systemic risks.
5. Assign an accountable risk owner.
6. Choose avoid, reduce, transfer/share, or accept.
7. Define mitigation actions, triggers, and contingency plans.
8. Allocate contingency where justified.
9. Review top risks on a cadence tied to volatility.
10. Close risks only with evidence or convert realized risks into issues.

## Decision points
Escalate low-probability catastrophic risks even when simple scores appear moderate. Spend mitigation effort proportional to exposure and reversibility.

## Common failure patterns
Generic risk statements, no owner, confusing actions with outcomes, scoring without treatment, hiding bad news, and treating the risk register as static documentation.

## Verification
Top risks have current evidence, owners, actions, triggers, residual exposure, and contingency. Forecasts reflect material risk rather than assuming perfect execution.

## Expected output
An actively managed risk register and treatment plan connected to project decisions.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when residual exposure exceeds delegated tolerance, mandatory controls are absent, or a risk threatens project viability.
