# Stakeholder Engagement

## Purpose
Build the alignment, participation, and decision flow required for project success across stakeholders with different incentives and authority.

## When to use
Use from initiation through transition, especially for cross-functional, politically sensitive, or change-heavy projects.

## Inputs
Stakeholder list, organization context, project outcomes, decision rights, concerns, influence, impact, and communication history.

## Context to inspect
Inspect formal authority, informal influence, affected user groups, sponsors, delivery teams, operations, compliance, vendors, and known conflicts.

## Core knowledge
Stakeholder management is not broadcast communication. Engagement should reflect influence, impact, information needs, decision responsibilities, and resistance drivers.

## Procedure
1. Identify stakeholders and affected groups beyond the obvious sponsor chain.
2. Assess influence, impact, interest, expectations, and current support.
3. Identify decisions or behaviors required from each critical stakeholder.
4. Tailor engagement cadence and channel to those needs.
5. Surface conflicts and incentives early.
6. Create forums for meaningful decisions rather than status theater.
7. Track commitments and unresolved concerns.
8. Adjust engagement as influence and project impact change.
9. Protect teams from contradictory direction by clarifying authority.
10. Validate readiness before major transitions.

## Decision points
Use direct one-to-one engagement for sensitive conflict; group forums for shared decisions; written records for durable commitments and governance.

## Common failure patterns
Equating stakeholder management with email distribution, ignoring frontline users, overloading executives with detail, failing to record decisions, and treating resistance as a communication defect only.

## Verification
Critical stakeholders understand outcomes, their role, upcoming decisions, and current risks; conflicting instructions have an identified resolution path.

## Expected output
A stakeholder engagement strategy with owners, objectives, cadence, decision needs, and tracked concerns.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when unresolved authority conflicts block delivery or when required stakeholder participation cannot be secured.
