# Change Control

## Purpose
Evaluate and govern material changes so approved commitments remain coherent across scope, schedule, cost, risk, quality, and benefits.

## When to use
Use when proposed changes affect a baseline, contract, milestone, budget, architecture commitment, compliance obligation, or major dependency.

## Inputs
Change request, rationale, benefit, affected baselines, estimates, risk analysis, alternatives, and decision authority.

## Context to inspect
Inspect prior decisions, cumulative changes, contractual terms, downstream dependencies, release plans, and stakeholder commitments.

## Core knowledge
Change control should be proportional. The purpose is informed trade-offs and traceability, not bureaucracy. Cumulative small changes can be material even when each is individually minor.

## Procedure
1. Describe the requested change and business reason.
2. Confirm whether it changes an approved baseline.
3. Analyze impact across scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, dependencies, and risk.
4. Identify options including no change, defer, substitute, or partial change.
5. State recommendation and residual consequences.
6. Route to the authorized decision owner.
7. Record decision, rationale, conditions, and effective date.
8. Update all affected baselines and artifacts.
9. Communicate changes to impacted teams and stakeholders.
10. Monitor implementation and cumulative change volume.

## Decision points
Use lightweight approval for reversible low-impact changes; formal governance for material, regulated, contractual, or cross-program changes.

## Common failure patterns
Approval without impact analysis, updating only the schedule, using change control to resist valid learning, and allowing verbal decisions to bypass baselines.

## Verification
Approved changes are traceable through updated scope, schedule, budget, risks, and stakeholder commitments.

## Expected output
An auditable change record and synchronized project baselines.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when impact cannot be assessed, decision authority is absent, or a requested change violates mandatory constraints.
