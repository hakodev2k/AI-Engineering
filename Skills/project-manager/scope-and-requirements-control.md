# Scope and Requirements Control

## Purpose
Control project scope so delivery remains aligned to approved outcomes while legitimate change can be evaluated transparently.

## When to use
Use during planning, refinement, delivery, and any request that changes commitments, deliverables, acceptance conditions, or boundaries.

## Inputs
Charter, requirements, work breakdown, baseline scope, change requests, estimates, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect contractual commitments, product ownership, technical dependencies, regulatory obligations, release plans, and previous scope decisions.

## Core knowledge
Scope control is not change prevention. It makes cost, schedule, risk, quality, and benefit impacts visible before commitment. Requirements uncertainty should be managed differently from unauthorized scope growth.

## Procedure
1. Establish a clear scope baseline and traceable deliverables.
2. Separate clarification from genuine scope change.
3. Capture each proposed change with rationale and expected benefit.
4. Analyze schedule, cost, resource, dependency, quality, and risk impacts.
5. Identify alternatives such as defer, split, substitute, or reduce scope.
6. Route the decision to the correct authority.
7. Record approval, rejection, conditions, and rationale.
8. Update baselines, plans, requirements, risks, and communications after approval.
9. Verify downstream teams received the changed commitment.
10. Monitor cumulative scope movement, not only individual requests.

## Decision points
Fast-track low-impact reversible changes when governance allows. Require formal review for contractual, security, regulatory, budget, milestone, or cross-team impacts.

## Common failure patterns
Silent scope creep, treating all clarifications as changes, approving without estimates, protecting schedule by sacrificing hidden quality, and updating tickets without updating project baselines.

## Verification
Compare approved scope with active work and acceptance evidence. Confirm every material deviation has an explicit decision and downstream plans reflect it.

## Expected output
A controlled scope baseline and auditable change history with impact analysis and decisions.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when decision authority is unclear, impacts cannot be estimated credibly, or requested changes invalidate project viability.
