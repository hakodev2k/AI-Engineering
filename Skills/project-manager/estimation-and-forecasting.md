# Estimation and Forecasting

## Purpose
Produce decision-useful forecasts from uncertain estimates without disguising uncertainty as certainty.

## When to use
Use for delivery dates, budgets, capacity planning, scenario analysis, and reforecasting.

## Inputs
Historical throughput, work breakdown, estimate ranges, capacity, dependencies, risks, actual progress, and remaining work.

## Context to inspect
Review estimation method, team stability, novelty, external lead times, rework rates, interruptions, and whether historical data is comparable.

## Core knowledge
An estimate describes uncertainty; a forecast combines estimates with current evidence and assumptions. Senior practice uses ranges, confidence, reference-class evidence, and frequent reforecasting rather than padding every task.

## Procedure
1. Define what is being forecast and the decision it supports.
2. Choose an estimation method appropriate to uncertainty and available evidence.
3. Separate effort, duration, and elapsed lead time.
4. Capture ranges and assumptions for uncertain work.
5. Use historical throughput or analogous projects where relevant.
6. Include dependencies, queues, rework, and non-delivery capacity.
7. Build optimistic, expected, and adverse scenarios when material.
8. State confidence and key forecast drivers.
9. Update forecasts using actual completion and remaining-work evidence.
10. Communicate changes early with causal explanation.

## Decision points
Use deterministic estimates for well-known repeatable work; probabilistic or scenario-based forecasts for uncertain portfolios and complex projects. Prefer evidence over negotiated optimism.

## Common failure patterns
Equating story points with days, averaging incomparable estimates, hiding contingency, treating estimates as promises, and failing to reforecast after scope or capacity changes.

## Verification
Back-test forecasts against actual outcomes, track forecast error, and confirm assumptions and confidence levels are visible to decision-makers.

## Expected output
A transparent forecast with ranges, assumptions, confidence, scenarios, and current completion outlook.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when stakeholders demand unsupported certainty or when missing scope/capacity information makes the forecast materially misleading.
