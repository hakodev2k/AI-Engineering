# Resource and Capacity Management

## Purpose
Align project demand with realistic team capacity, specialist availability, and organizational constraints without relying on sustained overload.

## When to use
Use during planning, reforecasting, staffing changes, portfolio contention, and recovery planning.

## Inputs
Work demand, skill needs, team capacity, calendars, allocation data, hiring/procurement lead times, and competing commitments.

## Context to inspect
Inspect actual availability, support/on-call load, leave, parallel projects, specialist bottlenecks, onboarding time, and organizational allocation policies.

## Core knowledge
People are not interchangeable units. Capacity planning must account for skills, coordination overhead, focus loss, ramp-up, and sustainable utilization. Adding people late can increase short-term load.

## Procedure
1. Translate planned work into capability and timing needs.
2. Measure effective capacity rather than nominal headcount.
3. Identify scarce skills and single points of dependency.
4. Compare demand with capacity by time period.
5. Resolve over-allocation through priority, sequencing, scope, staffing, or date changes.
6. Include onboarding and handover time in staffing decisions.
7. Protect critical specialists from fragmented assignments.
8. Track planned versus actual allocation and throughput.
9. Reforecast when attrition, leave, incidents, or priorities change.
10. Escalate structural capacity gaps early.

## Decision points
Prefer reducing simultaneous work before assuming overtime. Add resources when work is parallelizable and onboarding cost is justified; otherwise adjust scope or dates.

## Common failure patterns
Planning at 100% utilization, double-booking shared specialists, ignoring operational work, treating headcount as skill-equivalent, and using overtime as baseline capacity.

## Verification
Committed work fits effective capacity, critical skills are available when needed, and allocation conflicts have explicit decisions.

## Expected output
A capacity-aware staffing plan linked to schedule, risks, and forecast.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when required capability cannot be secured within project constraints or delivery depends on unsafe sustained workload.
