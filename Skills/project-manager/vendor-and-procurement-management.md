# Vendor and Procurement Management

## Purpose
Manage external suppliers so commercial commitments, delivery obligations, quality, dependencies, and project outcomes remain aligned.

## When to use
Use when project success depends on vendors, contractors, licenses, managed services, equipment, or procurement lead times.

## Inputs
Requirements, procurement policy, proposals, contracts, SLAs, milestones, acceptance criteria, costs, and supplier risks.

## Context to inspect
Inspect legal/procurement rules, data/security obligations, lead times, renewal terms, exit clauses, subcontractors, and internal vendor owners.

## Core knowledge
Vendor management joins commercial and delivery realities. A signed contract does not remove dependency risk; acceptance, incentives, change mechanisms, and exit conditions must support the project.

## Procedure
1. Define required outcomes and measurable acceptance criteria.
2. Engage procurement, legal, security, and technical specialists early.
3. Evaluate suppliers on capability, risk, total cost, support, and fit—not price alone.
4. Align contract milestones with project dependencies.
5. Define responsibilities, interfaces, SLAs, escalation, and change procedures.
6. Track supplier deliverables and confidence against needed-by dates.
7. Verify acceptance evidence before milestone approval where permitted.
8. Manage commercial changes through authorized channels.
9. Maintain contingency for critical supplier failure.
10. Plan transition, renewal, or exit before lock-in becomes urgent.

## Decision points
Prefer outcome-based commitments when deliverables are measurable; use time-and-materials where scope uncertainty makes fixed commitments counterproductive.

## Common failure patterns
Selecting on headline cost, vague acceptance, ignoring integration effort, late security review, and assuming contractual penalties recover lost project time.

## Verification
Supplier obligations map to project milestones, acceptance evidence exists, commercial changes are authorized, and critical vendor risks have contingencies.

## Expected output
A supplier management plan integrated with project schedule, budget, risk, and acceptance.

## Stop conditions
Escalate unauthorized commitments, unacceptable legal/security terms, supplier insolvency risk, or material breach beyond delegated authority.
