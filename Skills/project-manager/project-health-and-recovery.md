# Project Health and Recovery

## Purpose
Diagnose a struggling project using evidence and construct a credible recovery plan rather than masking variance with optimistic reporting.

## When to use
Use when milestones slip, forecasts lose credibility, risks compound, quality deteriorates, or teams are chronically overloaded.

## Inputs
Baselines, actual progress, forecast history, scope, defects, risks, issues, dependencies, costs, capacity, decisions, and stakeholder feedback.

## Context to inspect
Inspect root causes across scope, planning, architecture, leadership, dependencies, capability, governance, incentives, quality, and external constraints.

## Core knowledge
Recovery begins with re-establishing truth. Protecting an obsolete baseline creates more failure. Recovery options trade scope, time, cost, quality risk, sequencing, or delivery approach.

## Procedure
1. Gather objective delivery evidence.
2. Reconstruct current scope, completed outcomes, remaining work, and critical dependencies.
3. Identify root causes rather than schedule symptoms.
4. Re-estimate remaining work and capacity.
5. Quantify exposure and likely completion range.
6. Develop recovery scenarios with explicit trade-offs.
7. Recommend a feasible option and required decisions.
8. Re-baseline only with authorized agreement.
9. Increase monitoring temporarily around recovery drivers.
10. Restore normal governance after sustained evidence of control.

## Decision points
Cancel or materially reshape a project when expected value no longer justifies recovery cost or risk. Do not preserve sunk-cost commitments automatically.

## Common failure patterns
Adding overtime without root-cause analysis, cutting testing invisibly, changing dates without changing assumptions, and reporting percentage complete without accepted deliverables.

## Verification
Recovery milestones are evidence-based, owners accept revised commitments, causes have treatments, and forecast reliability improves.

## Expected output
An approved recovery plan with reforecast, trade-offs, actions, and control metrics.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when no viable recovery scenario meets minimum business, safety, security, regulatory, or financial constraints.
