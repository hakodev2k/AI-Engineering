# Quality Management

## Purpose
Define and govern the quality conditions required for project deliverables to be fit for purpose, supportable, and accepted.

## When to use
Use during planning, delivery, release readiness, and when schedule pressure creates pressure to reduce validation.

## Inputs
Acceptance criteria, non-functional requirements, test strategy, defect data, operational standards, compliance controls, and release criteria.

## Context to inspect
Inspect product quality expectations, technical standards, test environments, defect trends, support readiness, security requirements, and regulatory obligations.

## Core knowledge
Quality is planned into delivery, not inspected in at the end. Project management owns visibility and governance of quality risk while specialists own technical validation.

## Procedure
1. Translate stakeholder expectations into measurable quality criteria.
2. Define required reviews, tests, controls, and evidence.
3. Assign owners for each quality gate.
4. Include quality work in schedule and capacity plans.
5. Track defect severity, escape trends, rework, and unresolved exceptions.
6. Prevent schedule recovery from silently deleting required controls.
7. Review release readiness against agreed evidence.
8. Record approved exceptions and residual risk owners.
9. Confirm operational support and documentation readiness.
10. Feed recurring defects into process improvement.

## Decision points
Use risk-based depth: critical security, safety, financial, and irreversible changes require stronger evidence than low-impact reversible changes.

## Common failure patterns
Treating test completion as quality, measuring defect count without severity, deferring all non-functional validation, and accepting risk without an authorized owner.

## Verification
Every release criterion has evidence or an explicit approved exception; unresolved defects and operational risks are visible to decision-makers.

## Expected output
A quality plan and release evidence set tied to acceptance and risk decisions.

## Stop conditions
Stop release recommendation when mandatory controls fail, evidence is missing, or residual risk exceeds authorized tolerance.
