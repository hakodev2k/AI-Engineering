# AI Risk Classification

## Purpose
Classify AI use cases consistently so governance effort, controls, review depth, and approval authority match material risk.

## When to use
Use at intake and whenever purpose, users, data, autonomy, model, geography, or deployment context changes materially.

## Inputs
Use-case description, affected users, decisions supported or automated, data classes, model capabilities, autonomy, scale, jurisdictions, failure consequences.

## Core knowledge
Risk depends on context and impact, not model sophistication alone. Classification should combine inherent risk with applicable regulatory categories and organizational risk appetite.

## Procedure
1. Confirm intended purpose and prohibited uses.
2. Identify affected people and business processes.
3. Assess safety, rights, privacy, security, financial, operational, reputational, and misuse impacts.
4. Assess autonomy, reversibility, human oversight, scale, and exposure.
5. Identify regulated or high-impact contexts.
6. Assign inherent risk using documented criteria.
7. Map required controls and approval authority.
8. Record rationale and evidence.
9. Require independent challenge for high-risk classifications.
10. Define triggers for reclassification.

## Decision points
When evidence is incomplete, classify conservatively until uncertainty is resolved. Do not lower a tier merely because controls are planned; distinguish inherent from residual risk.

## Common failure patterns
Scoring model capability instead of use-case impact, hiding uncertainty, inconsistent exceptions, collapsing inherent and residual risk.

## Verification
Run calibration exercises across reviewers and test edge cases for consistent outcomes.

## Expected output
Risk tier, rationale, obligations, control set, approval route, and reassessment triggers.

## Stop conditions
Escalate ambiguous regulated-use classifications or impacts outside delegated risk authority.
