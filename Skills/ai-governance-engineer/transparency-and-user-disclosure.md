# Transparency and User Disclosure

## Purpose
Design accurate, useful disclosures about AI use, capabilities, limitations, data practices, and recourse.

## When to use
Use for user-facing AI, consequential AI-assisted decisions, synthetic content, or regulated transparency obligations.

## Inputs
User journey, system purpose, AI role, known limitations, data practices, legal requirements, support and appeal processes.

## Procedure
1. Identify audiences and decisions affected by AI.
2. Determine mandatory and risk-relevant disclosures.
3. Explain AI involvement at the point it matters.
4. Describe material limitations without misleading certainty.
5. Explain data use and user choices where applicable.
6. Provide recourse, correction, or human-contact paths for consequential outcomes.
7. Validate readability, accessibility, localization, and timing.
8. Align public claims with evaluation evidence.
9. Version disclosures with system changes.
10. Test user comprehension for high-impact contexts.

## Decision points
Layer information when full detail would overwhelm users; concise notices must link to deeper explanations where needed.

## Common failure patterns
Boilerplate notices, hidden AI involvement, exaggerated capability claims, disclosure after the decision, inaccessible language.

## Verification
Trace each material claim to evidence and test whether representative users understand AI involvement and available recourse.

## Expected output
Audience-specific disclosure requirements and validated notice content specification.

## Stop conditions
Escalate conflicting legal, product, or evidence constraints that prevent truthful disclosure.
