# AI Impact Assessment

## Purpose
Evaluate foreseeable benefits, harms, affected groups, dependencies, and mitigations before consequential AI deployment.

## When to use
Use for high-impact systems, major changes, sensitive populations, consequential decisions, or governance gates requiring structured impact analysis.

## Inputs
Purpose, process maps, user groups, data flows, model behavior, evaluation results, complaints, legal/privacy assessments, operational controls.

## Procedure
1. Define decision context and baseline without the AI system.
2. Identify direct, indirect, and non-user affected groups.
3. Map benefits and plausible harm pathways.
4. Evaluate severity, likelihood, scale, duration, reversibility, and detectability.
5. Examine differential impacts and accessibility constraints.
6. Assess human oversight and contestability.
7. Map mitigations to each material harm.
8. Identify residual risks and accountable acceptors.
9. Define monitoring indicators and reassessment triggers.
10. Obtain required independent review.

## Decision points
Prefer measurable mitigations over policy statements. If a harm cannot be mitigated to acceptable residual risk, recommend redesign, restricted deployment, or rejection.

## Common failure patterns
Generic templates, benefit-only analysis, ignoring non-users, unsupported likelihood estimates, mitigation without owners, no post-launch monitoring.

## Verification
Every material harm has evidence, mitigation, owner, residual-risk decision, and monitoring signal.

## Expected output
Impact assessment with affected groups, risk pathways, mitigations, residual risk, and monitoring plan.

## Stop conditions
Stop when critical facts are unavailable or residual risk exceeds authorized appetite.
