# Governance Operating Model

## Purpose
Design an accountable AI governance system that converts policy into repeatable decisions without blocking legitimate delivery.

## When to use
Use when establishing or revising enterprise AI governance, ownership, committees, decision rights, or escalation paths. Do not use as a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

## Inputs
Organization structure, AI portfolio, policies, risk appetite, regulatory obligations, existing control functions.

## Context to inspect
Map product, engineering, security, privacy, legal, compliance, procurement, audit, and executive responsibilities. Inspect existing approval workflows before proposing new ones.

## Core knowledge
Effective governance separates ownership, independent challenge, approval, and assurance. Controls should be proportional to risk and leave auditable evidence.

## Procedure
1. Define governed AI scope and terminology.
2. Inventory stakeholder groups and existing control functions.
3. Define decision rights using clear accountable owners.
4. Establish risk tiers and approval authorities.
5. Map lifecycle gates from intake through retirement.
6. Define exception and escalation paths.
7. Specify evidence retained at each gate.
8. Set review cadence and effectiveness metrics.
9. Pilot on representative systems and remove redundant controls.
10. Publish the operating model and train accountable roles.

## Decision points
Centralize standards where consistency matters; federate execution where domain context matters. Add committees only for decisions that cannot be owned effectively by a named role.

## Common failure patterns
Committee-only governance, duplicate approvals, unclear accountability, undocumented exceptions, controls disconnected from engineering workflows.

## Verification
Trace several real AI systems through the model and prove each material decision has an owner, criteria, evidence, and escalation route.

## Expected output
Governance charter, RACI/decision-rights model, lifecycle gates, escalation model, and effectiveness measures.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when legal authority, executive risk appetite, or accountable ownership is unresolved.
