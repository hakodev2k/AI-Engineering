# AI Governance Metrics and Reporting

## Purpose
Measure whether AI governance reduces unmanaged risk and improves decision quality rather than merely counting process activity.

## When to use
Use for executive reporting, control improvement, board/risk committee oversight, or governance program maturity reviews.

## Inputs
Inventory, risk tiers, control results, exceptions, incidents, evaluation failures, remediation, monitoring, audit findings.

## Procedure
1. Define decisions the metrics must support.
2. Separate portfolio exposure, control effectiveness, outcomes, and process health.
3. Establish denominators and data lineage.
4. Track high-risk systems, overdue assessments, control failures, exceptions, incidents, and remediation aging.
5. Add outcome indicators tied to material AI risks.
6. Segment by risk tier, business area, provider, or lifecycle state where useful.
7. Define thresholds and escalation actions.
8. Validate metric quality and resistance to gaming.
9. Report trends with uncertainty and narrative context.
10. Retire vanity metrics and revise as risks evolve.

## Decision points
Prefer rates and risk-weighted exposure over raw counts. A faster approval time is not success if assurance quality declines.

## Common failure patterns
Counting meetings, missing denominators, stale inventory feeding dashboards, green status despite accepted severe risks, metrics without action thresholds.

## Verification
Reconcile reported figures to source systems and confirm each executive metric drives a defined decision or escalation.

## Expected output
Governance KPI/KRI catalog and decision-oriented reporting pack.

## Stop conditions
Stop publication when source data is materially incomplete or misleading.
