# Model and AI Vendor Due Diligence

## Purpose
Assess external AI providers and models for governance, security, privacy, reliability, compliance, and concentration risks before adoption.

## When to use
Use during procurement, renewal, model replacement, material provider changes, or high-risk third-party integrations.

## Inputs
Vendor documentation, contracts, security reports, model/system cards, data terms, subprocessors, SLAs, incident history, evaluation results.

## Procedure
1. Define intended use and criticality.
2. Determine data sent, retained, trained on, and transferred.
3. Assess security, privacy, access, isolation, and incident practices.
4. Review model limitations, evaluation evidence, transparency, and change policy.
5. Assess availability, rate limits, support, portability, and concentration risk.
6. Map contractual terms to governance requirements.
7. Test material claims where feasible.
8. Identify compensating controls and exit strategy.
9. Assign residual risk and approval route.
10. Define continuous monitoring and reassessment triggers.

## Decision points
Prefer contractual commitments for material controls; documentation alone is weaker evidence. Higher criticality requires stronger portability and continuity planning.

## Common failure patterns
Brand-based trust, ignoring model updates, unclear training-data terms, no exit plan, vendor questionnaire without validation.

## Verification
Critical requirements are supported by contract, independent evidence, technical tests, or explicit accepted residual risk.

## Expected output
Due-diligence record, risk findings, required clauses, compensating controls, approval decision, monitoring plan.

## Stop conditions
Stop if required evidence is withheld or contractual terms violate non-negotiable policy.
