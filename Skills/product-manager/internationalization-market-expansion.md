# Internationalization and Market Expansion

## Purpose
Adapt a product for new countries, languages, currencies, regulations, and market behaviors without assuming translation alone creates product-market fit.

## When to use
Use for geographic expansion, localization, multi-currency, regional compliance, or international growth prioritization.

## Inputs
Target markets, customer evidence, product usage, revenue potential, language needs, legal requirements, payment methods, support capacity, and technical readiness.

## Context to inspect
Inspect locale handling, dates/numbers, currencies, text expansion, right-to-left needs, data residency, identity, payments, taxes, support hours, and local alternatives.

## Core knowledge
Localization is product adaptation; internationalization is the technical capability that enables it. Market entry also depends on distribution, trust, pricing, compliance, and local workflows.

## Procedure
1. Define expansion objective and target segment.
2. Validate demand and alternatives in the target market.
3. Identify regulatory, payment, data, and contractual requirements.
4. Audit technical internationalization readiness.
5. Prioritize critical journeys and content for localization.
6. Adapt pricing, onboarding, support, and trust signals where needed.
7. Test with native users in context.
8. Launch to a controlled segment or region.
9. Measure activation, retention, support burden, and economics versus baseline.
10. Expand investment only when evidence supports it.

## Decision points
Localize deeply when workflow or trust differences drive value; use lighter translation when behavior and economics are already validated.

## Common failure patterns
Machine translation as market strategy, ignoring text layout, assuming home-market pricing, and launching without local support or compliance readiness.

## Verification
Critical journeys work correctly by locale; legal and payment requirements pass; market metrics and support load are observable.

## Expected output
Market-entry requirements, localization scope, readiness gaps, rollout, and success criteria.

## Stop conditions
Stop when legal entry, data handling, payments, or support obligations cannot be satisfied.
