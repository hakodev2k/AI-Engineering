# Customer Research and Interviewing

## Purpose
Generate reliable customer insight about behaviors, constraints, motivations, and unmet needs for product decisions.

## When to use
Use when problem understanding, adoption barriers, workflows, or value assumptions are uncertain.

## Inputs
Research question, target segment, recruiting criteria, existing evidence, interview guide, and consent/privacy constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect prior studies, analytics, support conversations, segment definitions, and decisions that research must inform.

## Core knowledge
Ask about concrete past behavior rather than hypothetical preference. Avoid leading questions and separate observation from interpretation.

## Procedure
1. Define the decision and learning objectives.
2. Recruit participants matching relevant behavior or segment criteria.
3. Prepare neutral prompts around recent real experiences.
4. Establish consent and recording rules.
5. Explore context, triggers, workflow, workarounds, consequences, and decision criteria.
6. Probe examples and contradictions without selling a solution.
7. Capture evidence and notable quotes sparingly.
8. Synthesize patterns across participants.
9. Compare qualitative findings with behavioral data.
10. Update assumptions and product decisions.

## Decision points
Use contextual observation when users cannot accurately describe workflows. Use surveys after qualitative work when prevalence must be measured.

## Common failure patterns
Pitching during interviews, asking whether users like an idea, recruiting only friendly users, counting mentions as demand, and overgeneralizing small samples.

## Verification
Findings trace to participant evidence, contradictory evidence is retained, and conclusions distinguish observed behavior from inference.

## Expected output
Research findings, patterns, exceptions, confidence, and implications for a defined decision.

## Stop conditions
Stop when consent is absent, sensitive data cannot be handled safely, or recruiting criteria cannot answer the research question.
