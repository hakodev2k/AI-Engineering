# Product Positioning and Messaging

## Purpose
Define how the product should be understood by a target customer relative to alternatives and translate that position into consistent messaging.

## When to use
Use for launches, market entry, repositioning, weak conversion, segment expansion, or inconsistent sales and marketing narratives.

## Inputs
Target segment, customer problems, alternatives, differentiated capabilities, evidence, outcomes, and brand constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect customer language, win/loss data, competitor claims, sales objections, website messaging, onboarding, and actual product experience.

## Core knowledge
Positioning identifies who the product is for, the valuable problem, relevant category or frame, differentiated value, and proof. Messaging expresses that positioning for a context.

## Procedure
1. Select the target segment and buying context.
2. Identify alternatives customers use today.
3. Determine capabilities that are meaningfully different.
4. Translate capabilities into customer outcomes.
5. Select the market frame that makes value understandable.
6. Draft positioning with explicit proof points.
7. Create message hierarchy for key audiences.
8. Test comprehension and relevance with customers or prospects.
9. Align product, sales, marketing, and onboarding language.
10. Update when market or product evidence changes.

## Decision points
Choose a narrower segment when differentiated value is strongest there. Avoid creating a new category unless existing frames materially prevent understanding.

## Common failure patterns
Generic claims, feature lists, unsupported superlatives, targeting everyone, and messaging that promises behavior the product does not deliver.

## Verification
Target customers understand who it is for, what problem it solves, why it differs, and why claims are credible.

## Expected output
Positioning statement, message hierarchy, proof points, objections, and audience adaptations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when differentiation is not supported by product reality or customer evidence.
