# Outcome-Based Roadmapping

## Purpose
Build a roadmap that communicates intended outcomes, sequencing logic, confidence, and learning rather than promising an inflexible feature calendar.

## When to use
Use for quarterly planning, cross-team alignment, stakeholder communication, and strategy execution.

## Inputs
Product strategy, prioritized opportunities, target outcomes, dependencies, capacity, confidence, and mandatory dates.

## Context to inspect
Inspect active initiatives, delivery health, discovery status, platform dependencies, external commitments, and metric baselines.

## Core knowledge
A roadmap is a decision and communication artifact. Separate committed work from options and express uncertainty explicitly.

## Procedure
1. Start from strategic outcomes and problems.
2. Group opportunities by outcome or strategic theme.
3. Identify sequencing constraints and enabling work.
4. Classify confidence and commitment level.
5. Place work into appropriate horizons rather than arbitrary dates.
6. Attach measurable outcomes and decision checkpoints.
7. Validate feasibility with engineering and design.
8. Review conflicts with commercial and operational commitments.
9. Publish assumptions and update when evidence changes.

## Decision points
Use dates for true deadlines or coordinated launches. Use horizons for uncertain discovery work. Keep enabling technical work visible when it protects outcomes or risk.

## Common failure patterns
Feature calendars, hidden dependencies, every request appearing committed, no success measures, and roadmap changes without explaining new evidence.

## Verification
Each initiative links to strategy and an outcome; dependencies and confidence are visible; stakeholders can distinguish commitments from options.

## Expected output
An outcome-oriented roadmap with themes, horizons, measures, dependencies, confidence, and review points.

## Stop conditions
Stop when strategic priorities are unresolved or delivery dependencies make sequencing unknowable without technical input.
