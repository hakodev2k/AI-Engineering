# Go-to-Market and Launch Planning

## Purpose
Coordinate product, marketing, sales, support, operations, and engineering so a release reaches the right users safely and can be evaluated.

## When to use
Use for material launches, new segments, pricing changes, integrations, migrations, or features requiring customer education.

## Inputs
Target audience, positioning, release scope, readiness status, risks, channels, enablement needs, support plan, and success metrics.

## Context to inspect
Inspect rollout capabilities, documentation, sales process, customer commitments, support capacity, legal review, analytics, and rollback mechanisms.

## Core knowledge
Launch size should match customer impact and strategic importance. Shipping code and achieving adoption are separate activities.

## Procedure
1. Define target users, value proposition, and launch objective.
2. Classify launch scale and audience.
3. Confirm product, quality, security, legal, analytics, and operational readiness.
4. Define rollout stages and eligibility.
5. Prepare positioning, documentation, enablement, and support materials.
6. Identify customer migrations or communication requirements.
7. Define success metrics and guardrails.
8. Establish incident and rollback ownership.
9. Execute rollout with monitoring.
10. Review adoption, outcomes, feedback, and incidents after launch.

## Decision points
Use phased rollout for uncertain or high-impact changes. Use broad launch only when readiness, capacity, and rollback confidence justify exposure.

## Common failure patterns
Marketing before readiness, no support preparation, measuring announcement reach instead of adoption, and no rollback or customer communication plan.

## Verification
Readiness owners sign off, telemetry works, support paths are prepared, and launch metrics can be measured.

## Expected output
A launch plan with audience, positioning, rollout, readiness, communications, metrics, and contingency actions.

## Stop conditions
Delay or escalate when critical readiness criteria fail, rollback is unavailable for high-risk changes, or mandatory approvals are missing.
