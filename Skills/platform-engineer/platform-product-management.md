# Platform Product Management

## Purpose
Prioritize platform engineering around developer outcomes rather than infrastructure output.

## When to use
Use for roadmap planning, capability investment, adoption problems, or competing platform requests.

## Inputs
Developer research, support data, usage metrics, strategic constraints, reliability data, and engineering cost.

## Context to inspect
Adoption, workflow lead time, ticket volume, abandonment, incidents, duplicated team solutions, and platform maintenance burden.

## Core knowledge
Internal platforms have users, alternatives, switching costs, and adoption curves. Mandates can hide poor product fit.

## Procedure
1. Segment platform users and workflows.
2. Gather qualitative and quantitative pain evidence.
3. Define outcomes and success metrics.
4. Rank opportunities by impact, reach, risk, and cost.
5. Test assumptions with small pilots.
6. Publish roadmap intent, not false precision.
7. Measure adoption and outcome change.
8. Retire low-value capabilities.

## Decision points
Build when shared value exceeds central maintenance cost; enable team autonomy when needs are specialized and low-risk.

## Common failure patterns
Roadmaps driven by tool enthusiasm, counting features, forced adoption, ignoring migration cost, and supporting every edge case.

## Verification
Priority decisions trace to evidence, pilots validate key assumptions, and shipped capabilities improve defined developer outcomes.

## Expected output
An evidence-based platform roadmap with users, outcomes, priorities, metrics, and retirement decisions.

## Stop conditions
Stop major investment when target users or measurable problems cannot be established.
