# Privacy Impact Assessment

## Purpose
Assess significant privacy risk before launch and ensure mitigations are owned, implemented, and evidenced.

## When to use
Use for high-risk or novel processing, sensitive data, systematic monitoring, large-scale profiling, or when governance requires assessment.

## Inputs
Business purpose, data map, architecture, affected populations, threat model, controls, vendors, and approved legal guidance.

## Context to inspect
Inspect necessity, proportionality, user expectations, vulnerable groups, automated decisions, transfers, retention, and alternatives.

## Core knowledge
An impact assessment is a decision process, not a template exercise. It should connect processing to harms, mitigations, residual risk, and accountable approval.

## Procedure
1. Define scope and processing purpose.
2. Describe data and lifecycle.
3. Assess necessity and less-invasive alternatives.
4. Identify affected groups and plausible harms.
5. Evaluate likelihood and severity.
6. Map existing controls.
7. Define additional mitigations and owners.
8. Reassess residual risk.
9. Obtain required review or approval.
10. Reopen when processing materially changes.

## Decision points
Redesign or avoid processing when residual harm remains disproportionate to value.

## Common failure patterns
Completing assessments after launch, generic risks, no owners, and treating approval as permanent.

## Verification
Confirm every mitigation has implementation evidence and unresolved risks have authorized disposition.

## Expected output
A current assessment supporting a documented launch or redesign decision.

## Stop conditions
Stop launch when required assessment or high-risk approval remains incomplete.
