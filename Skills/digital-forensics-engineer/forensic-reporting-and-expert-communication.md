# Forensic Reporting and Expert Communication

## Purpose
Convert complex forensic findings into clear, reproducible, audience-appropriate reports without overstating evidence.

## When to use
Use for incident reports, legal or compliance handoff, executive briefings, technical remediation, or peer review.

## Inputs
Validated findings, evidence references, timelines, scope, hypotheses, confidence levels, limitations, and intended audience.

## Context to inspect
Reporting standard, legal sensitivity, disclosure constraints, business terminology, evidence identifiers, and remediation ownership.

## Core knowledge
A strong forensic report separates facts, analysis, inference, and recommendations. It must preserve enough provenance for another qualified investigator to reproduce pivotal findings.

## Procedure
1. Restate scope, authority, and investigative questions.
2. Describe evidence sources and acquisition/validation methods.
3. Present a concise executive finding before technical detail.
4. Organize technical findings chronologically or by investigative question.
5. Cite evidence identifiers for consequential statements.
6. State confidence, assumptions, contradictions, and evidence gaps.
7. Separate attacker/user activity from control failures and remediation.
8. Remove unnecessary sensitive content while preserving evidentiary meaning.
9. Perform technical and editorial peer review.

## Decision points
Use exact technical detail when reproducibility requires it; summarize when details add risk without decision value. Avoid attribution language stronger than the evidence.

## Common failure patterns
Mixing speculation with facts, omitting methodology, using unexplained jargon, including irrelevant secrets, and writing recommendations as if they were evidence.

## Verification
A reviewer can trace each major conclusion to evidence and distinguish confirmed, inferred, and unknown elements.

## Expected output
Defensible report tailored to its audience with evidence references, limitations, and actionable conclusions.

## Stop conditions
Stop publication when evidence references are missing, legal review is required but unavailable, or sensitive data has not been minimized.
