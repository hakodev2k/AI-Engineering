# Legal, Privacy, and Investigation Boundaries

## Purpose
Keep forensic work within authorization, privacy, retention, and disclosure constraints while preserving evidentiary value.

## When to use
Use before and during investigations involving employee devices, customer data, cloud tenants, regulated information, cross-border data, or potential litigation.

## Inputs
Investigation authority, scope, jurisdiction, data categories, retention policies, legal holds, contractual restrictions, and stakeholder requirements.

## Context to inspect
Consent or policy basis, ownership of assets/accounts, employee monitoring rules, privileged material, personal data, third-party data, export restrictions, and escalation contacts.

## Core knowledge
Technical capability does not imply authorization. Senior forensic practice minimizes unnecessary collection, separates relevant evidence from unrelated sensitive data, preserves chain of custody, and escalates ambiguous legal questions rather than improvising.

## Procedure
1. Confirm documented authority and investigative purpose.
2. Define systems, users, data classes, and time windows in scope.
3. Identify legal holds, regulatory obligations, and restricted data.
4. Choose the least intrusive collection that can answer the question.
5. Apply access controls, encryption, retention, and need-to-know handling.
6. Segregate privileged or unrelated sensitive content when required.
7. Record scope changes and obtain approval before expanding collection.
8. Coordinate disclosure, transfer, and deletion with authorized stakeholders.
9. Preserve audit records for evidence access and custody.

## Decision points
Prefer targeted collection when full imaging would unnecessarily expose unrelated data, unless evidentiary completeness or preservation requirements justify broader acquisition.

## Common failure patterns
Scope creep, collecting personal data without need, copying evidence into unmanaged locations, bypassing legal hold requirements, and sharing raw evidence too broadly.

## Verification
Confirm evidence handling matches documented authority, retention, access, and disclosure requirements.

## Expected output
A documented investigation boundary, compliant evidence-handling plan, and escalation record for unresolved constraints.

## Stop conditions
Stop when authorization is unclear, jurisdictional constraints conflict, privileged material requires review, or requested collection exceeds approved scope.
