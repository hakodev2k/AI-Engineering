# Developer Tooling Product Management

## Purpose
Manage internal developer tooling as a product with explicit users, outcomes, priorities, lifecycle, and support commitments.

## When to use
Use when a platform/tooling backlog is driven by ad hoc requests, technical enthusiasm, or stakeholder volume rather than measured developer outcomes.

## Inputs
Developer research, telemetry, support load, strategy, platform constraints, engineering capacity, and risk requirements.

## Context to inspect
Inspect user segments, current workflows, adoption, operating cost, dependencies, competing solutions, and ownership boundaries.

## Core knowledge
Internal tools still compete with alternatives such as scripts, direct cloud consoles, or ignoring standards. Adoption follows delivered value and trust.

## Procedure
1. Define users and jobs-to-be-done.
2. Establish measurable outcome goals.
3. Collect evidence from workflows and telemetry.
4. Frame problems before selecting solutions.
5. Prioritize by impact, reach, confidence, risk, and cost.
6. Define minimal useful increments.
7. Launch with documentation and support.
8. Measure adoption and outcome change.
9. Maintain, deprecate, or retire based on evidence.

## Decision points
Build centrally when shared leverage exceeds coordination cost; enable team-owned solutions when needs are specialized and boundaries are safe.

## Common failure patterns
Feature-count roadmaps, mandatory adoption as success, no deprecation strategy, ignoring operational cost, and prioritizing loudest stakeholders.

## Verification
Confirm shipped capabilities change target workflow metrics or observed developer behavior, not merely usage counts.

## Expected output
An evidence-based tooling roadmap with outcome metrics, lifecycle decisions, and explicit support boundaries.

## Stop conditions
Stop when target users, ownership, or success criteria cannot be established.
