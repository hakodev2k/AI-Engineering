# Developer Research and Feedback

## Purpose
Collect reliable developer evidence to guide platform, tooling, and workflow decisions.

## When to use
Use before major DX investments, after launches, when telemetry cannot explain behavior, or when support themes repeat.

## Inputs
Target personas, research questions, usage data, support themes, candidate participants, and product constraints.

## Context to inspect
Inspect who is affected, who is missing from feedback, existing evidence, incentives, and decision deadlines.

## Core knowledge
Ask about observed behavior and concrete recent tasks rather than hypothetical preferences. Triangulate interviews, observation, telemetry, and support evidence.

## Procedure
1. State research questions and decisions.
2. Recruit representative participants.
3. Prepare task-focused prompts.
4. Observe workflows and capture evidence.
5. Separate facts, interpretations, and requests.
6. Cluster recurring friction and root causes.
7. Quantify prevalence using broader data where possible.
8. Prioritize findings and close the feedback loop.

## Decision points
Use interviews for depth, surveys for breadth, telemetry for behavior, and direct observation for workflow reality.

## Common failure patterns
Only interviewing advocates, asking leading questions, treating requested solutions as requirements, and collecting feedback without follow-through.

## Verification
Cross-check themes across multiple evidence sources and validate proposed interpretations with affected developers.

## Expected output
Evidence-backed findings with affected journeys, severity, confidence, and actionable recommendations.

## Stop conditions
Stop when participant privacy cannot be protected or the sample is too biased to support the intended conclusion.
