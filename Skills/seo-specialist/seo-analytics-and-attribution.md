# SEO Analytics and Attribution

## Purpose
Measure organic-search outcomes accurately enough to guide investment, diagnose change, and communicate uncertainty.

## When to use
Use for reporting, experiments, traffic changes, forecasting, or prioritization.

## Inputs
Search-console data, analytics, conversions, revenue, rank tracking, release annotations, and business KPIs.

## Context to inspect
Tracking definitions, consent effects, channel grouping, branded/non-branded queries, devices, countries, templates, and seasonality.

## Core knowledge
SEO data is sampled and incomplete across systems. Correlation is not causation; use triangulation and explicit assumptions.

## Procedure
1. Define business question before metrics.
2. Validate tracking and conversion definitions.
3. Segment by query, page type, market, device, and brand where useful.
4. Establish comparable baselines.
5. Annotate releases and external events.
6. Triangulate impressions, clicks, sessions, conversions, and revenue.
7. Quantify uncertainty and alternative explanations.
8. Report decisions and next actions, not vanity metrics.

## Decision points
Use leading metrics for early diagnosis and business outcomes for value assessment. Choose attribution models according to decision context.

## Common failure patterns
Reporting averages only, mixing incompatible date ranges, claiming causality from rankings, and ignoring tracking changes.

## Verification
Reconcile major discrepancies across data sources and reproduce calculations from documented definitions.

## Expected output
Decision-oriented dashboard/report with segments, assumptions, anomalies, and actions.

## Stop conditions
Stop when tracking integrity is insufficient to support the requested conclusion.
