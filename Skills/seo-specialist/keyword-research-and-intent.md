# Keyword Research and Search Intent

## Purpose
Turn real search demand into prioritized topics and pages aligned with user intent and business value.

## When to use
Use for content planning, new markets, category expansion, or declining organic acquisition.

## Inputs
Products, audiences, seed topics, query data, SERPs, competitors, conversion goals, and existing content.

## Context to inspect
Current rankings, page inventory, audience vocabulary, seasonality, funnel stages, and geographic/language scope.

## Core knowledge
Volume alone is not opportunity. Intent, relevance, ranking feasibility, SERP composition, conversion value, and cannibalization determine priority.

## Procedure
1. Define audience and business outcomes.
2. Expand seed concepts from customer and query evidence.
3. Cluster semantically related queries.
4. Inspect SERPs to infer dominant intent and expected format.
5. Map clusters to existing or proposed pages.
6. Score opportunity by relevance, demand, difficulty, value, and strategic fit.
7. Identify gaps and cannibalization risks.
8. Produce a prioritized keyword-to-page map.

## Decision points
Create a new page only when intent differs materially; consolidate overlapping pages when one authoritative destination better serves the cluster.

## Common failure patterns
Chasing volume, trusting tool intent labels blindly, one-page-per-keyword planning, and ignoring zero-click SERPs.

## Verification
Validate clusters against live SERPs, query data, and content inventory; track ranking and conversion outcomes after publication.

## Expected output
Evidence-backed keyword clusters, intent classification, page mapping, priorities, and measurement plan.

## Stop conditions
Stop when market/language scope or business priorities are unresolved.
