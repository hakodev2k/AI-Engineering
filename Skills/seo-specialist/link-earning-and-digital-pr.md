# Link Earning and Digital PR

## Purpose
Earn authoritative mentions and links through genuinely useful assets, expertise, relationships, and newsworthy work.

## When to use
Use when authority, awareness, or discovery requires external validation beyond owned content.

## Inputs
Audience, expertise, original data, assets, media landscape, competitors, existing backlinks, and brand constraints.

## Context to inspect
Link quality, topical relevance, referral value, journalist needs, prior campaigns, brand risk, and link-spam patterns.

## Core knowledge
Durable links are editorial outcomes, not inventory to purchase. Relevance, credibility, placement, and audience value matter more than raw counts.

## Procedure
1. Define campaign audience and measurable outcome.
2. Identify credible expertise, data, tools, or stories worth citing.
3. Validate novelty and evidence.
4. Build a targeted publisher/professional prospect set.
5. Pitch concise, relevant value rather than demanding links.
6. Support journalists with verifiable sources and assets.
7. Track earned coverage, links, referrals, and brand outcomes.
8. Review patterns for quality and risk.

## Decision points
Prefer fewer editorially justified links over scalable paid placements. Use nofollow/sponsored attributes where commercial relationships require them.

## Common failure patterns
Buying links, mass outreach, fabricated studies, irrelevant guest posts, and measuring only domain metrics.

## Verification
Manually inspect placements, confirm attribution and destination quality, and measure referral/visibility outcomes.

## Expected output
Ethical campaign plan, qualified prospects, evidence assets, and outcome report.

## Stop conditions
Reject deceptive, paid-link, private-network, or policy-violating tactics.
