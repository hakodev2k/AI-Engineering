# Ecommerce SEO

## Purpose
Optimize category, product, filter, inventory, and merchandising systems for scalable organic acquisition.

## When to use
Use for online stores, marketplaces, large catalogs, or commerce platform redesigns.

## Inputs
Catalog taxonomy, product feeds, inventory states, faceted URLs, templates, query demand, and conversion data.

## Context to inspect
Categories, product variants, filters, pagination, out-of-stock behavior, reviews, structured data, internal search, and duplicate descriptions.

## Core knowledge
Commerce SEO is an information-architecture problem at scale. Index only URL states with durable search value and sufficiently distinct content.

## Procedure
1. Map demand to taxonomy and landing pages.
2. Identify valuable versus combinatorial filter states.
3. Define crawl/index rules for facets and sorting.
4. Optimize category usefulness beyond product grids.
5. Handle variants and duplicate products consistently.
6. Define out-of-stock and discontinued-product behavior.
7. Validate product structured data and merchant facts.
8. Strengthen internal links to strategic categories/products.
9. Monitor index bloat, revenue, and organic landing performance.

## Decision points
Index facets only when they represent stable demand and unique value. Preserve discontinued URLs when alternatives or durable information justify them.

## Common failure patterns
Indexing every filter, deleting out-of-stock pages immediately, manufacturer-copy duplication, and relying only on product pages.

## Verification
Crawl catalog segments, inspect indexed samples, validate markup, and measure organic revenue by landing type.

## Expected output
Scalable catalog SEO rules, prioritized template changes, and measurement criteria.

## Stop conditions
Escalate changes that conflict with merchandising or inventory policy.
