# SEO Content Brief Design

## Purpose
Translate search intent, audience needs, and business goals into a useful editorial specification without prescribing keyword stuffing.

## When to use
Use before creating or substantially refreshing search-led content.

## Inputs
Target cluster, SERPs, audience, product context, internal expertise, conversion goal, and existing pages.

## Context to inspect
Ranking formats, recurring user questions, information gain opportunities, internal links, brand constraints, and subject-matter evidence.

## Core knowledge
A strong brief defines the problem, audience, intent, evidence, differentiation, and success criteria. Search competitors reveal expectations, not a template to copy.

## Procedure
1. Define primary audience and job-to-be-done.
2. Confirm dominant and secondary intents.
3. Review top results for expected depth and format.
4. Identify unanswered questions and unique expertise.
5. Specify recommended structure and required facts.
6. Define internal-link and conversion opportunities.
7. Add credibility, sourcing, and freshness requirements.
8. Define acceptance and measurement criteria.

## Decision points
Prefer original expert value over exhaustive coverage when it better satisfies intent. Split content when intents require distinct experiences.

## Common failure patterns
Copying competitor headings, forcing exact-match phrases, vague briefs, unsupported claims, and optimizing before understanding users.

## Verification
Review the brief against SERPs, audience needs, brand standards, and existing content overlap.

## Expected output
A writer-ready brief with intent, scope, evidence needs, differentiation, structure, links, and success criteria.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when subject expertise or claim evidence is unavailable.
