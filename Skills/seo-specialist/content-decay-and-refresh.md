# Content Decay and Refresh

## Purpose
Identify aging content and restore usefulness, accuracy, differentiation, and search performance without unnecessary URL churn.

## When to use
Use for declining evergreen pages, outdated facts, changed SERPs, or large mature content libraries.

## Inputs
Content inventory, query trends, traffic, conversions, backlinks, publish/update dates, and SERPs.

## Context to inspect
Accuracy, intent drift, competitor changes, outdated examples, broken links, cannibalization, authority, and business relevance.

## Core knowledge
A date change is not a refresh. Updates should materially improve accuracy or usefulness and preserve valuable signals when the URL still fits intent.

## Procedure
1. Detect declining pages using comparable periods and segments.
2. Separate demand loss from ranking/content loss.
3. Re-evaluate current search intent.
4. Audit factual freshness and missing information.
5. Decide refresh, consolidate, redirect, retain, or retire.
6. Improve evidence, structure, examples, and internal links.
7. Preserve the URL when intent remains stable.
8. Republish transparently and monitor outcomes.

## Decision points
Consolidate overlapping weak pages; retire content with no durable user value; avoid refreshing pages already performing strongly without a clear reason.

## Common failure patterns
Changing dates only, mass AI rewrites, deleting backlink-rich pages, and measuring immediately after publication.

## Verification
Check accuracy, links, indexability, query coverage, and post-refresh search/conversion trends.

## Expected output
Prioritized refresh portfolio with action type, rationale, changes, and measurement.

## Stop conditions
Escalate regulated or expert claims that require qualified review.
