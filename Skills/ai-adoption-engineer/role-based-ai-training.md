# Role-Based AI Training

## Purpose
Create practical training for users, reviewers, managers, and support teams so they can operate AI-enabled workflows effectively and consistently.

## When to use
Use before rollout to teams with uneven AI experience or when recurring errors show that users misunderstand system limits or review responsibilities.

## Inputs
User roles, workflows, model capabilities, approved usage boundaries, known failure modes, support history, and business risk.

## Context to inspect
Inspect existing onboarding, recurring support questions, review responsibilities, quality incidents, and common misunderstandings about model behavior, context, data handling, and accountability.

## Core knowledge
Useful training should focus on operational judgment: capability boundaries, uncertainty, verification, appropriate data use, context quality, escalation, and responsibility. Different roles require different depth.

## Procedure
1. Identify decisions each role makes around the AI workflow.
2. Define the knowledge required to make those decisions correctly.
3. Separate common concepts from workflow-specific guidance.
4. Build realistic examples of strong, weak, and uncertain AI outputs.
5. Teach users how to verify important claims against authoritative sources.
6. Explain approved and unsupported use cases.
7. Train reviewers on evidence, corrections, and escalation.
8. Train managers on metrics, ownership, and operational risk.
9. Test understanding through realistic scenarios.
10. Refresh training when models, policies, or workflows materially change.

## Decision points
Use mandatory assessment for high-consequence workflows. Keep basic training focused on decisions users actually make rather than unnecessary model internals.

## Common failure patterns
Teaching only prompt tricks, using generic examples, assuming completion equals competence, and failing to update training after system changes.

## Verification
Representative users must demonstrate correct behavior in scenarios involving uncertain output, sensitive context, correction, and escalation.

## Expected output
Role-specific training modules, practical scenarios, assessment criteria, and refresh triggers.

## Stop conditions
Stop when workflow responsibilities or approved usage boundaries are too ambiguous to teach accurately.
