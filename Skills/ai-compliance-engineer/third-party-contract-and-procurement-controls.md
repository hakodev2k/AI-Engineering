# Third-Party Contract and Procurement Controls

## Purpose
Embed AI-specific compliance requirements into procurement and contracts so vendor obligations match actual technical and regulatory risk.

## When to use
Use before buying AI services, renewing agreements, expanding data use, enabling new model capabilities, or changing hosting regions.

## Inputs
Use case, risk classification, data categories, vendor due diligence, architecture, legal requirements, standard contractual clauses, business continuity needs.

## Preconditions
The technical integration and critical compliance requirements are understood before contract execution.

## Context to inspect
Master service agreement, DPA, security schedules, AI terms, subprocessors, service levels, audit rights, deletion terms, model-change terms, termination assistance.

## Core knowledge
AI contracts should address data use, training rights, retention, model changes, incident notification, subprocessors, geographic processing, audit evidence, transparency, intellectual property, safety controls, and exit. Boilerplate cloud terms may not cover these issues.

## Procedure
1. Translate risk and regulatory findings into contractual requirements.
2. Define allowed and prohibited data uses.
3. Specify retention, deletion, and training restrictions.
4. Require material model or service change notification.
5. Define incident and breach notification duties.
6. Address subprocessors and processing locations.
7. Establish audit/evidence rights.
8. Define service continuity and exit obligations.
9. Record accepted deviations and compensating controls.
10. Link contractual commitments to operational monitoring.

## Decision points
Use stronger clauses for high-risk, sensitive-data, or mission-critical systems. Accept vendor standard terms only when residual risk is explicitly reviewed and approved.

## Common failure patterns
Negotiating before technical scope is known, contractual requirements that operations cannot monitor, no model-change clause, and no exit/data-deletion mechanism.

## Verification
Confirm executed terms match required controls and that operational owners can monitor each ongoing vendor obligation.

## Expected output
A contract-control checklist, approved deviations, monitoring obligations, and evidence links.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when mandatory protections are refused, contractual terms contradict actual data flows, or residual vendor risk exceeds approval authority.
