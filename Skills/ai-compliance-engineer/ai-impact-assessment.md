# AI Impact Assessment

## Purpose
Evaluate how an AI system may affect individuals, organizations, rights, safety, and business operations before deployment or material change.

## When to use
Use for high-impact systems, new automated decisions, sensitive populations, material capability expansion, or when policy requires an AI impact assessment.

## Inputs
System purpose, users, decisions, datasets, model behavior, human oversight, evaluation results, risk classification, deployment context.

## Preconditions
The intended use, affected populations, and system boundaries are documented.

## Context to inspect
Product requirements, data flows, model cards, evaluation reports, UX, escalation paths, vendor documentation, complaint mechanisms, monitoring plans.

## Core knowledge
Impact assessment considers not only technical failure but also fairness, autonomy, explainability, contestability, accessibility, misuse, chilling effects, and unequal burden. Risk controls should be evaluated in the actual deployment context.

## Procedure
1. Define system purpose and decision influence.
2. Identify affected people and stakeholders.
3. Describe intended benefits and dependencies.
4. Identify plausible harms and misuse.
5. Assess severity, likelihood, and scale.
6. Evaluate data, fairness, transparency, and oversight risks.
7. Review user recourse and human intervention.
8. Map mitigations to each material impact.
9. Determine residual impact and acceptance authority.
10. Record monitoring and reassessment triggers.

## Decision points
Require deeper assessment when impacts are difficult to reverse, users cannot opt out, or decisions materially affect rights or opportunities.

## Common failure patterns
Treating the assessment as a checklist, ignoring indirect stakeholders, evaluating only intended use, and documenting mitigations without proving they operate.

## Verification
Ensure each material harm has evidence, mitigation, owner, residual-risk judgment, and measurable monitoring signal.

## Expected output
A signed AI impact assessment with harms, benefits, controls, residual risks, approvals, and follow-up requirements.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when residual high risk is not accepted by the authorized owner, affected populations are unclear, or meaningful safeguards cannot be implemented.
