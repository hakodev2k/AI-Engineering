# Compliance Stakeholder Communication

## Purpose
Communicate AI compliance findings, obligations, risks, and decisions clearly across engineering, product, legal, security, privacy, executives, auditors, and business owners.

## When to use
Use for risk reviews, launch decisions, remediation planning, audits, incident updates, executive reporting, and cross-functional governance forums.

## Inputs
Risk assessments, control status, findings, evidence, legal interpretations, technical constraints, business goals, decision deadlines.

## Preconditions
Facts, assumptions, unresolved questions, and decision owners are distinguished.

## Context to inspect
Architecture summaries, compliance records, remediation backlog, incident status, launch timeline, stakeholder responsibilities, governance cadence.

## Core knowledge
Senior compliance engineering requires translating between legal obligations and technical implementation without overstating certainty. Different audiences need different levels of detail, but the underlying facts and risk conclusions must remain consistent.

## Procedure
1. Identify the decision the audience must make.
2. State the system scope and relevant obligation.
3. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and legal questions.
4. Explain the technical control state in plain language.
5. Quantify or rank residual risk where possible.
6. Present feasible options and trade-offs.
7. State recommended action and rationale.
8. Assign owners, deadlines, and approval authority.
9. Record decisions and unresolved dependencies.
10. Update stakeholders when evidence or risk changes.

## Decision points
Use detailed technical evidence for implementers and auditors; use concise risk, options, and decision framing for executives. Escalate rather than simplifying away material uncertainty.

## Common failure patterns
Using legal jargon with engineers, hiding uncertainty, presenting risk without options, treating compliance as a blocker instead of an engineering constraint, and failing to record decisions.

## Verification
Confirm recipients understand the required action, decision owner, residual risk, deadline, and evidence basis; reconcile meeting decisions with the compliance record.

## Expected output
A decision-oriented compliance communication with scope, facts, risk, options, recommendation, owners, approvals, and evidence references.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when stakeholders disagree on factual system behavior, decision authority is unclear, or communication would require unsupported legal conclusions.
