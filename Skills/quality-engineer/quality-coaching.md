# Quality Coaching

## Purpose
Raise team capability so quality is designed and owned throughout delivery rather than delegated to a final testing phase.

## When to use
Use when recurring defects, weak test design, ownership gaps, or inefficient quality practices appear across a team.

## Inputs
Defect patterns, review observations, team workflows, metrics, incident learnings, examples of current practice.

## Context to inspect
Inspect where defects enter and escape, decision ownership, feedback loops, skills gaps, incentives, and delivery constraints.

## Core knowledge
Effective coaching changes systems and decisions, not merely knowledge. Use real team examples, collaborative problem solving, and measurable follow-through. Avoid becoming a permanent quality gatekeeper.

## Procedure
1. Identify a recurring quality problem with evidence.
2. Determine whether cause is skill, tooling, process, architecture, or incentives.
3. Define the desired team behavior.
4. Teach using current work and concrete examples.
5. Pair on applying the practice.
6. Provide lightweight reusable heuristics or templates.
7. Shift ownership to engineers/product partners.
8. Measure whether outcomes improve.
9. Adapt or retire coaching interventions that do not help.

## Decision points
Use workshops for shared gaps; targeted pairing for local gaps; system changes when training cannot solve structural problems.

## Common failure patterns
Lecturing without application, QA-as-police culture, mandatory process for every risk level, and measuring attendance instead of outcomes.

## Verification
Observe independent application and improved defect/feedback outcomes over subsequent work.

## Expected output
Improved team quality capability with reduced dependency on specialist intervention.

## Stop conditions
Escalate organizational incentives or staffing constraints that cannot be solved through coaching.
