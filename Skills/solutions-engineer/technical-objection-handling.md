# Technical Objection Handling

## Purpose
Resolve technical objections with evidence, precise scope, and transparent trade-offs rather than unsupported persuasion.

## When to use
Use during evaluations, architecture reviews, security discussions, and competitive technical conversations.

## Inputs
Objection, stakeholder concern, architecture, requirements, product evidence, test results, known limitations.

## Context to inspect
Underlying risk, stakeholder role, prior incidents, acceptance criteria, competing approaches, and whether the concern is factual or preference-based.

## Core knowledge
An objection often represents unresolved risk. Senior solutions engineers clarify the consequence behind the question, acknowledge real limitations, and distinguish supported behavior from roadmap or speculation.

## Procedure
1. Restate the objection neutrally.
2. Ask what failure or outcome the stakeholder fears.
3. Separate factual claims from assumptions.
4. Gather documentation or empirical evidence.
5. Explain relevant architecture and limitations.
6. Offer mitigation or alternatives where appropriate.
7. Validate whether the concern is resolved.
8. Record unresolved risks and follow-ups.

## Decision points
Demonstrate when evidence is disputed; escalate when authoritative product, security, or legal interpretation is required.

## Common failure patterns
Arguing, overpromising, dismissing edge cases, inventing roadmap commitments, and answering a different question.

## Verification
The objection has a documented evidence-based disposition or explicit unresolved owner.

## Expected output
A technically accurate resolution with evidence and caveats.

## Stop conditions
Stop when answering would require unsupported commitments or privileged decisions outside authority.
