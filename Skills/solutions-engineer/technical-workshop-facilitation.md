# Technical Workshop Facilitation

## Purpose
Run structured technical workshops that convert cross-functional knowledge into decisions, architecture, risks, and owned actions.

## When to use
Use for discovery, design, integration, migration, security, and production-readiness sessions.

## Inputs
Workshop objective, participants, known context, decisions needed, constraints, timebox.

## Context to inspect
Stakeholder roles, unresolved questions, prior decisions, diagrams, terminology, dependencies, and areas of disagreement.

## Core knowledge
A workshop is a decision-making mechanism, not a presentation. Facilitation must balance depth, participation, evidence, and time while separating facts, assumptions, and decisions.

## Procedure
1. Define outcome and agenda.
2. Invite required decision makers and domain owners.
3. Share pre-read and known context.
4. Establish terminology and current state.
5. Work through decision-driving questions.
6. Capture facts, assumptions, risks, and alternatives visibly.
7. Resolve or assign each open item.
8. Publish decisions and actions promptly.

## Decision points
Park low-impact tangents; deepen issues that threaten feasibility or acceptance. Split workshops when participant groups or objectives diverge.

## Common failure patterns
Lecture-style sessions, missing owners, silent disagreement, undocumented decisions, and attempting too much in one meeting.

## Verification
Participants confirm decisions, unresolved items have owners, and artifacts reflect the session accurately.

## Expected output
A decision-oriented workshop record and updated architecture/context.

## Stop conditions
Stop when required decision owners are absent or the session lacks enough evidence to decide safely.
