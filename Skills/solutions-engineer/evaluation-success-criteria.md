# Evaluation Success Criteria

## Purpose
Define objective technical acceptance criteria that allow stakeholders to determine whether an evaluation succeeded.

## When to use
Use before trials, POCs, pilots, bake-offs, and production-readiness evaluations.

## Inputs
Business outcomes, requirements, risks, workload, stakeholders, decision timeline.

## Context to inspect
Baseline behavior, measurable product capabilities, environment constraints, evidence sources, and stakeholder priorities.

## Core knowledge
Criteria must test decision-critical outcomes, not maximize the chance of declaring success. A useful criterion is specific, measurable, relevant, and has a defined evidence method.

## Procedure
1. Identify the decision the evaluation supports.
2. Map critical requirements and risks to testable outcomes.
3. Define baseline and target thresholds.
4. Specify test conditions and evidence sources.
5. Include negative/failure scenarios where material.
6. Agree criteria and scope before execution.
7. Record results without changing thresholds retroactively.
8. Classify pass, fail, partial, or inconclusive with rationale.

## Decision points
Exclude low-value feature checks that do not affect selection. Add criteria when a newly discovered risk materially changes the decision.

## Common failure patterns
Vague goals, moving targets, only happy-path tests, unrepresentative workloads, and criteria owned solely by the vendor.

## Verification
All parties agree on criteria, test method, and final evidence interpretation.

## Expected output
A decision-grade evaluation scorecard.

## Stop conditions
Stop when stakeholders cannot agree on what evidence would constitute technical acceptance.
