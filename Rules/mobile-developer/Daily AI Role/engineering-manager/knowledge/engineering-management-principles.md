# Engineering Management Principles

## Manage systems, not heroics
Repeated overtime, one-person dependencies, constant escalation, and manual coordination are system defects. Fix ownership, interfaces, staffing, process, or architecture instead of celebrating recovery effort.

## Outcomes over activity
Evaluate results, quality, collaboration, judgment, ownership, and growth against role expectations. Commit count, ticket count, keyboard time, and meeting visibility are weak proxies and must not be used alone.

## Delegation
Delegate decisions to the closest capable owner while preserving explicit escalation boundaries. A manager owns clarity, capability, context, and accountability; not every technical choice.

## Sustainable capacity
Nominal headcount is not usable capacity. Account for support load, onboarding, leave, incidents, operational work, meetings, dependencies, uncertainty, and cognitive load.

## Flow
Track work age, queue size, blocked time, work in progress, escaped defects, and rework. Averages can hide tail problems. Review aging work individually.

## Fair people decisions
Use explicit expectations, evidence across time, comparable scope, documented feedback, and opportunity to improve. Separate performance facts from personality preference.

## Escalation
Escalate when impact exceeds delegated authority, dependency owners cannot unblock within the agreed window, risk becomes irreversible, or required policy/legal/people authority is needed.
