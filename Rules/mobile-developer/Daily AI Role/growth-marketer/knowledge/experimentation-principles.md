# Experimentation Principles
Use experiments to reduce decision uncertainty, not to manufacture wins. Specify hypothesis, causal mechanism, unit, exposure, primary metric, guardrails and decision rule before observing outcomes. Randomization is preferred when feasible; quasi-experiments require explicit assumptions. Watch for sample-ratio mismatch, novelty effects, seasonality, interference, multiple comparisons, metric movement without business value and post-hoc segmentation. An invalid experiment is invalid, not negative. Record inconclusive results because they constrain future decisions.
