# Content Strategy Principles

## Content is a system, not a queue
A mature strategy connects audience needs, business outcomes, canonical knowledge, channels, governance, measurement, and maintenance. Publishing more without a system often increases inconsistency and stale content.

## Audience job before format
Choose format and channel after clarifying the reader's situation, problem, decision, and desired action. A blog post, email, landing page, video, or in-product message is an execution choice, not the strategy.

## Canonical source and derivatives
Keep volatile facts, product behavior, approved claims, and core positioning in owned canonical sources. Derivatives inherit meaning and freshness dependencies. This reduces contradictory rewrites and makes updates safer.

## Evidence hierarchy
Prefer authoritative first-party product facts, validated research, direct customer evidence, and reliable analytics. Public secondary sources can broaden context but should not silently replace canonical truth. AI output is synthesis, not evidence.

## Message hierarchy
A strong asset has one primary reader outcome, one primary message, supporting proof, and a clear next action. More messages usually reduce comprehension and make review ambiguous.

## Strategic trade-offs
- Reach vs relevance: broader distribution can dilute message fit.
- Freshness vs durability: fast-moving content requires ownership and expiry.
- SEO/discovery vs reader value: discoverability cannot justify misleading or padded content.
- Consistency vs channel nativeness: preserve semantic truth while adapting form.
- Speed vs verification: urgency can reduce scope, not remove factual or approval gates.

## Common failures
Content without a defined job; invented personas; unverified claims; measuring only traffic; orphaned derivatives; no retirement process; hidden reviewer disagreement; treating publication as completion.
