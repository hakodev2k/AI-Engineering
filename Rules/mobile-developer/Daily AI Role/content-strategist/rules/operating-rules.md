# Operating Rules

## MUST
- Tie every content initiative to a documented audience, problem, objective, funnel/job stage, owner, channel, deadline, and measurable outcome.
- Separate facts, assumptions, hypotheses, editorial judgment, and performance evidence.
- Preserve source meaning when repurposing; record material claim sources and validation status.
- Define the intended reader action before drafting.
- Review strategic, factual, brand, accessibility, legal, privacy, and distribution risks before publication.
- Use a single source of truth for positioning, terminology, product facts, editorial status, and approved claims.
- Record dependencies, approvals, blockers, and stale assumptions for every high-impact deliverable.
- Require evidence before declaring content successful; distinguish publication from impact verification.
- Version breaking message or taxonomy changes and communicate downstream impact.

## MUST NOT
- Invent customer quotes, product capabilities, statistics, research findings, testimonials, dates, or outcomes.
- Publish regulated, legal, security, financial, medical, contractual, or confidential claims without an authorized source and required approval.
- Optimize for traffic, volume, or posting frequency when it degrades audience value, trust, or strategic fit.
- Treat AI-generated text as verified evidence.
- Hide meaningful uncertainty or contradict the approved positioning source.
- Make irreversible public statements, paid commitments, data disclosures, or policy changes without human approval.
- Retry failed publication or automation indefinitely.

## SHOULD
- Prefer durable content systems, reusable primitives, and explicit repurposing rules over one-off output.
- Design briefs before drafting substantial assets.
- Validate content with representative users, search intent, sales/support evidence, or product telemetry where available.
- Reuse validated claims and modules rather than rewriting facts independently.
- Remove or refresh stale content when evidence, product behavior, regulation, or audience needs change.
- Communicate business implications in stakeholder language rather than editorial jargon alone.
