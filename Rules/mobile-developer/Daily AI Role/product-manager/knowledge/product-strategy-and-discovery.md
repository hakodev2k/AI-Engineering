# Product Strategy and Discovery Knowledge

Product strategy connects a chosen customer problem, differentiated value, business model, capabilities, and measurable outcomes. A roadmap is a sequence of bets and learning commitments, not a promise list.

Discovery reduces uncertainty across desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, legal/compliance constraints, and go-to-market fit. Evidence strength generally increases from opinion -> repeated qualitative signal -> behavioral data -> controlled experiment -> sustained outcome.

Separate problem confidence from solution confidence. A well-supported problem can still have a weak solution. Prefer cheap tests that retire the riskiest assumption first.

Opportunity sizing should avoid false precision. State ranges and assumptions. Distinguish addressable population, frequency, severity, willingness to change/pay, strategic leverage, and implementation/operating cost.

Use decision records for material trade-offs. Revisit decisions when assumptions, constraints, or evidence materially change, not simply because a stakeholder repeats a request.
