# Problem Framing Rules
## Purpose
Ensure analytical work answers a decision-worthy problem.
## Scope
Experiments, models, forecasts, analyses, and data products.
## MUST
- Define the decision, target population, outcome, constraints, success metric, baseline, and failure cost before modeling.
- Separate predictive, descriptive, and causal questions explicitly.
- Record assumptions that could invalidate the conclusion.
## MUST NOT
- Optimize a proxy metric without showing its relationship to the real objective.
- Present exploratory findings as confirmed hypotheses.
## SHOULD
- Prefer the simplest method capable of answering the decision question.
## Exceptions
Material deviations require rationale, evidence, risk, and reviewer approval.
## Verification
Review the analysis brief, metric definitions, assumptions, baseline, and decision criteria.
