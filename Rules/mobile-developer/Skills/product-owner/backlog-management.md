# Backlog Management

## Purpose
Maintain a coherent, ordered product backlog that translates outcomes and evidence into executable work without turning it into an uncontrolled request queue.

## When to use
Use during continuous discovery, refinement, planning, scope changes, and when backlog quality or size is degrading.

## Inputs
Product goals, roadmap context, user evidence, stakeholder requests, delivery constraints, dependencies, defects, analytics, and existing backlog items.

## Context to inspect
Review current objectives, item age, duplicates, dependencies, blocked work, acceptance quality, recent delivery outcomes, and team capacity assumptions.

## Core knowledge
A backlog is a decision system. Ordering should reflect value, risk, learning, urgency, dependencies, and cost of delay. Senior ownership includes removing obsolete work, preserving outcome context, and keeping near-term items sufficiently ready without over-specifying distant work.

## Procedure
1. Reconfirm current product goals and measurable outcomes.
2. Group backlog items by problem or outcome rather than requester.
3. Remove duplicates, obsolete items, and requests with no current rationale.
4. Separate discovery questions from delivery commitments.
5. Identify dependencies, risks, and sequencing constraints.
6. Refine near-term items to the level needed by the team.
7. Order work using explicit value and risk reasoning.
8. Keep future items lightweight until evidence justifies detail.
9. Review aging items and challenge why they remain.
10. Reassess ordering when evidence or constraints change.

## Decision points
Use strict prioritization when capacity is constrained; use discovery spikes when uncertainty dominates. Split items when independent value can be delivered, but avoid fragmentation that destroys user outcome context.

## Common failure patterns
Treating the backlog as storage, prioritizing by stakeholder seniority, keeping every idea forever, refining months ahead, hidden dependencies, and accepting technically shaped items without user value.

## Verification
Verify top items map to current goals, have clear rationale, dependencies are visible, stale work is removed, and the team can explain why one item precedes another.

## Expected output
An ordered, maintainable backlog with clear outcome context and actionable near-term work.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when product goals conflict, priority authority is unclear, or critical dependencies require decisions outside the Product Owner's mandate.
