# Product Decision Records

## Purpose
Preserve important product decisions, evidence, assumptions, trade-offs, and revisit conditions so teams can evolve without repeatedly reopening settled context.

## When to use
Use for consequential priority, scope, compatibility, policy, rollout, vendor, metric, and product-behavior decisions.

## Inputs
Decision question, alternatives, evidence, stakeholders, constraints, risks, and chosen option.

## Context to inspect
Inspect prior decisions, current goals, affected users, dependencies, and whether the decision is reversible.

## Core knowledge
Decision records should be concise and immutable as historical snapshots. Later decisions supersede rather than silently rewrite earlier rationale.

## Procedure
1. State the decision question and date.
2. Describe relevant context and constraints.
3. List credible alternatives considered.
4. Summarize evidence and uncertainty.
5. Record the selected option and why.
6. State key trade-offs and consequences.
7. Identify owner and affected stakeholders.
8. Define revisit triggers when uncertainty remains.
9. Link related backlog or roadmap changes.
10. Create a superseding record if the decision changes materially.

## Decision points
Record only decisions whose context is costly to reconstruct. Keep routine reversible choices in normal product artifacts.

## Common failure patterns
Writing records after memory fades, documenting only the chosen option, changing old records to match current thinking, and creating bureaucracy for trivial decisions.

## Verification
A future team member can understand what was decided, why, what alternatives existed, and what evidence would justify reconsideration.

## Expected output
A concise product decision record with context, rationale, consequences, and revisit conditions.

## Stop conditions
Do not record secrets or sensitive personal data; escalate classification concerns to appropriate governance owners.
