# Product Risk Register

## Purpose
Maintain a lightweight, actionable view of material product risks so uncertainty is managed deliberately rather than discovered at release time.

## When to use
Use for complex initiatives, regulated products, cross-team programs, migrations, high-visibility launches, and risky integrations.

## Inputs
Product goals, requirements, architecture findings, dependencies, stakeholder concerns, incidents, research, and delivery evidence.

## Context to inspect
Inspect user harm, business loss, schedule dependency, data/security exposure, adoption uncertainty, operational readiness, and reversibility.

## Core knowledge
A useful risk register drives action. Risks should describe uncertain events and consequences, not generic concerns. Track probability or confidence, impact, mitigation, owner, and trigger.

## Procedure
1. Identify material uncertainties across product, delivery, operational, security, and dependency dimensions.
2. Phrase each risk as cause-event-impact.
3. Rank by impact and likelihood or uncertainty.
4. Assign an accountable owner.
5. Define mitigation and contingency separately.
6. Add observable triggers or leading indicators.
7. Link mitigation work to backlog decisions where needed.
8. Review high risks frequently.
9. Close risks only with evidence.
10. Capture newly discovered systemic risks after incidents or experiments.

## Decision points
Mitigate when cost is justified, avoid when exposure is unacceptable, transfer where contracts legitimately shift responsibility, and accept only with explicit rationale.

## Common failure patterns
Risk lists with no owners, treating issues as risks, never closing entries, scoring without action, and hiding strategic uncertainty behind delivery risks.

## Verification
High risks have owners, actions, triggers, and current status; accepted risks have explicit rationale and authority.

## Expected output
A concise, current product risk register that influences prioritization and release decisions.

## Stop conditions
Escalate risks whose acceptance exceeds Product Owner authority or requires specialist legal, security, privacy, or financial judgment.
