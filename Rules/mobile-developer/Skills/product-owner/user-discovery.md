# User Discovery

## Purpose
Gather direct evidence about user problems, behaviors, constraints, and desired outcomes before committing significant delivery capacity.

## When to use
Use for new opportunities, unclear requirements, low adoption, conflicting stakeholder assumptions, and major workflow changes.

## Inputs
Target users, product data, support signals, hypotheses, existing research, and business goals.

## Context to inspect
Inspect behavioral analytics, support tickets, current journeys, segmentation, prior interviews, and known research gaps.

## Core knowledge
Discovery seeks evidence about problems and behavior, not validation of a preferred feature. Combine qualitative and quantitative evidence and distinguish stated preferences from observed behavior.

## Procedure
1. Write the key assumptions and unknowns.
2. Select representative users or proxies carefully.
3. Prepare behavior-focused questions and tasks.
4. Avoid leading with the proposed solution.
5. Capture workflows, triggers, workarounds, frequency, and consequences.
6. Look for contradictory evidence and segment differences.
7. Triangulate findings with product data where possible.
8. Convert findings into problem hypotheses and risks.
9. Decide what to test next.
10. Feed evidence into goals and backlog ordering.

## Decision points
Use interviews for motivations and context, observation for workflow reality, surveys for broader patterns, and analytics for actual product behavior.

## Common failure patterns
Interviewing only friendly users, asking whether users like an idea, treating anecdotes as prevalence, ignoring non-users, and collecting research without changing decisions.

## Verification
Findings cite evidence, assumptions are updated, contradictions are visible, and the next product decision changes or gains confidence because of the research.

## Expected output
Evidence-backed problem insights, updated assumptions, and recommended next experiments or decisions.

## Stop conditions
Stop when consent or privacy requirements are unmet, target users are inaccessible, or research scope requires specialist expertise.
