# Prioritization and Trade-offs

## Purpose
Make defensible product ordering decisions under constrained capacity, uncertainty, competing stakeholders, and dependencies.

## When to use
Use when selecting between opportunities, reordering a backlog, handling urgent requests, or cutting scope.

## Inputs
Product goals, expected value, user impact, evidence strength, risk, effort ranges, dependencies, deadlines, and cost of delay.

## Context to inspect
Inspect strategic fit, affected users, operational pain, contractual constraints, existing commitments, and what must be displaced by new work.

## Core knowledge
Prioritization is comparative. Frameworks such as RICE, WSJF, or value-versus-effort support thinking but do not replace judgment. Senior decisions expose assumptions and opportunity cost.

## Procedure
1. Define the decision and available capacity.
2. Remove options that do not support current goals unless mandatory.
3. Estimate value, urgency, risk reduction, and learning value.
4. Assess evidence confidence and effort uncertainty.
5. Identify dependencies and sequencing effects.
6. Compare options using a consistent model.
7. Stress-test rankings against strategic and customer consequences.
8. State what will not be done if the priority changes.
9. Record rationale and assumptions.
10. Revisit when evidence materially changes.

## Decision points
Use scoring for broad comparison, explicit cost-of-delay reasoning for time-sensitive work, and risk-first sequencing for irreversible decisions.

## Common failure patterns
False precision in scores, highest-paid-person priority, ignoring opportunity cost, mixing mandatory work with discretionary work, and prioritizing effort already spent.

## Verification
Stakeholders can see the criteria, displaced work is explicit, top priorities support goals, and assumptions are reviewable.

## Expected output
An ordered decision with rationale, trade-offs, assumptions, and displaced alternatives.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when priorities depend on unresolved executive strategy, legal obligations, or commitments the Product Owner cannot change.
