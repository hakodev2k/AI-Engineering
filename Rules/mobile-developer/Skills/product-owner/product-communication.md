# Product Communication

## Purpose
Communicate product context, decisions, priorities, uncertainty, and outcomes so different audiences can act without relying on constant synchronous clarification.

## When to use
Use for roadmap changes, priority decisions, releases, stakeholder updates, trade-offs, incidents, and cross-team coordination.

## Inputs
Decision or update, audience, evidence, impact, risks, requested actions, and known sensitivities.

## Context to inspect
Inspect what the audience already knows, what decision rights they have, how they are affected, and whether information is confidential or time-sensitive.

## Core knowledge
Effective product communication is audience-specific and decision-oriented. Separate verified facts from forecasts and assumptions. Explain why a change matters and what action follows.

## Procedure
1. Identify the audience and desired understanding or action.
2. Lead with the outcome or decision.
3. Provide only context needed to interpret it.
4. State evidence, assumptions, and uncertainty distinctly.
5. Explain impact, trade-offs, and what changed.
6. Identify owner and next action.
7. Use stable written artifacts for consequential decisions.
8. Tailor technical depth to the audience.
9. Invite correction of factual gaps, not endless reopening of settled decisions.
10. Update communication when material facts change.

## Decision points
Use synchronous communication for conflict or urgent ambiguity; asynchronous written communication for durable context and broad distribution.

## Common failure patterns
Status dumps, hiding uncertainty, overpromising dates, unexplained priority changes, excessive technical detail, and communicating decisions differently to different stakeholders.

## Verification
Recipients can explain what changed, why, how it affects them, and what happens next.

## Expected output
Clear, consistent product communication that enables action and preserves decision context.

## Stop conditions
Escalate communications involving legal disclosure, security incidents, regulated claims, or material external commitments to authorized owners.
