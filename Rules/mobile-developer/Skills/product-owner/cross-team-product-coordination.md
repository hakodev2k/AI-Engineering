# Cross-team Product Coordination

## Purpose
Coordinate product outcomes across teams with separate backlogs and ownership boundaries without creating a shadow project-management system.

## When to use
Use for shared journeys, platform dependencies, coordinated migrations, multi-service capabilities, and organization-wide product changes.

## Inputs
Shared outcome, team ownership, dependencies, interface expectations, timelines, risks, and decision rights.

## Context to inspect
Inspect each team's goals, backlog authority, technical boundaries, dependency contracts, release constraints, and communication paths.

## Core knowledge
Cross-team coordination works best through explicit outcomes, contracts, ownership, and dependency milestones rather than synchronized task micromanagement.

## Procedure
1. Define the shared customer or business outcome.
2. Map team responsibilities and decision owners.
3. Identify hard dependencies and interface contracts.
4. Agree on sequencing and integration checkpoints.
5. Clarify what each team can deliver independently.
6. Surface conflicting priorities early.
7. Track dependency evidence and risks, not every internal task.
8. Coordinate end-to-end acceptance and rollout.
9. Communicate changes that affect other teams promptly.
10. Review whether organizational coupling should be reduced after delivery.

## Decision points
Use asynchronous coordination for stable contracts; use joint working sessions for ambiguous interfaces or high-risk integration. Escalate priority conflicts rather than hiding them in dates.

## Common failure patterns
One Product Owner controlling another team's backlog, dependency spreadsheets with no owners, synchronized releases without need, and local success metrics that undermine the shared journey.

## Verification
Ownership, contracts, sequencing, integration evidence, and end-to-end acceptance are explicit across teams.

## Expected output
A coordinated cross-team product plan focused on shared outcomes and dependency control.

## Stop conditions
Escalate unresolved priority or ownership conflicts to the accountable product or engineering leadership level.
