# Opportunity Sizing and Prioritization

## Purpose
Compare product opportunities using value, evidence, strategic fit, cost, risk, and opportunity cost rather than stakeholder volume.

## When to use
Use during portfolio reviews, roadmap planning, discovery intake, and capacity conflicts.

## Inputs
Opportunity statements, customer evidence, expected outcomes, reach estimates, effort ranges, dependencies, risks, and strategic priorities.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current commitments, team capacity, metric baselines, segment economics, technical constraints, and mandatory obligations.

## Core knowledge
Scoring frameworks such as RICE can structure thinking but do not replace judgment. Estimates should expose uncertainty and avoid false precision.

## Procedure
1. Normalize requests into comparable opportunities and outcomes.
2. Identify affected users and estimate reachable population.
3. Estimate impact using an explicit metric or economic mechanism.
4. Assess evidence confidence and uncertainty.
5. Obtain engineering/design effort ranges and dependencies.
6. Identify strategic fit, risk reduction, and time sensitivity.
7. Compare opportunities using one transparent framework.
8. Stress-test ranking against changed assumptions.
9. Make explicit what is deferred and why.

## Decision points
Use quantitative scoring for a broad comparable set; use strategic judgment for platform bets, existential risks, or options whose value is poorly represented by near-term metrics.

## Common failure patterns
Precise invented numbers, double-counting impact, treating effort as known too early, ignoring opportunity cost, and letting framework scores make the decision automatically.

## Verification
Inputs are traceable, uncertainty is visible, ranking changes sensibly under sensitivity tests, and chosen work connects to strategy.

## Expected output
Prioritized opportunities with rationale, assumptions, confidence, dependencies, and deferred alternatives.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when opportunities lack comparable outcomes, capacity is unknown, or prioritization requires unresolved executive trade-offs.
