# Pricing and Packaging

## Purpose
Design pricing and packaging that align customer value, willingness to pay, product differentiation, costs, and sustainable business economics.

## When to use
Use for new monetization, tier changes, enterprise packaging, usage-based models, or persistent conversion and expansion issues.

## Inputs
Customer segments, value drivers, usage patterns, competitive alternatives, costs, sales evidence, willingness-to-pay research, and business goals.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current plans, discounting, churn, expansion, gross margin, procurement behavior, entitlement complexity, and billing capabilities.

## Core knowledge
Price metric and package boundaries influence customer behavior and product architecture. Pricing should reflect value while remaining understandable and operationally feasible.

## Procedure
1. Segment customers by needs and value received.
2. Identify value metrics and usage drivers.
3. Analyze current conversion, expansion, churn, and discount patterns.
4. Research willingness to pay without relying only on stated preference.
5. Define candidate packages and entitlement boundaries.
6. Model revenue, margin, migration, and edge cases.
7. Validate billing and technical feasibility.
8. Test messaging and buying comprehension.
9. Plan migration, grandfathering, communication, and measurement.
10. Monitor behavior and unintended incentives after launch.

## Decision points
Use seat pricing when value scales with users; usage pricing when value and cost track consumption; hybrid models when neither alone reflects economics. Keep packages simpler unless differentiation materially improves outcomes.

## Common failure patterns
Competitor-copy pricing, too many tiers, cost-plus pricing only, hidden entitlement complexity, and ignoring existing-customer migration.

## Verification
Economics are modeled, package rules are implementable, customers understand differences, and post-launch guardrails are defined.

## Expected output
Pricing hypothesis, package design, financial model, migration plan, and measurement plan.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when legal, tax, billing, contractual, or executive approval is required.
