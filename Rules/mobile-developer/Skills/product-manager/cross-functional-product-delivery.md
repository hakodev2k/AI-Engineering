# Cross-Functional Product Delivery

## Purpose
Lead product delivery across engineering, design, data, operations, and go-to-market while keeping decisions tied to outcomes.

## When to use
Use from validated opportunity through implementation, release, and outcome review.

## Inputs
Outcome, requirements, design artifacts, technical plan, dependencies, risks, milestones, and launch needs.

## Context to inspect
Inspect team ownership, capacity, delivery workflow, dependency teams, operational readiness, and decision-making responsibilities.

## Core knowledge
The product manager owns clarity and trade-offs, not every task. Healthy delivery preserves team autonomy while making scope, decisions, risk, and outcomes explicit.

## Procedure
1. Align the team on problem, outcome, scope, and non-goals.
2. Confirm engineering and design understanding before commitment.
3. Identify dependencies, decision owners, and high-risk assumptions.
4. Break uncertainty early through spikes or discovery where needed.
5. Track decisions and outcome risk rather than micromanaging tasks.
6. Resolve scope conflicts using customer value and constraints.
7. Coordinate analytics, support, legal, security, and go-to-market readiness.
8. Review release criteria and rollback considerations.
9. After launch, compare outcomes with expectations and feed learning back into planning.

## Decision points
Reduce scope when it preserves the core outcome and lowers risk. Move dates only when scope reduction would invalidate value or create unacceptable risk.

## Common failure patterns
PM as project secretary, hidden scope growth, late dependency discovery, treating estimates as guarantees, and declaring success at code completion.

## Verification
Decision ownership is clear, dependencies are managed, release readiness is evidenced, and post-launch outcome review is scheduled.

## Expected output
Aligned execution, explicit trade-offs, controlled scope, release readiness, and measurable post-launch learning.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when unresolved dependencies block the critical path, quality or safety criteria cannot be met, or stakeholders demand incompatible commitments.
