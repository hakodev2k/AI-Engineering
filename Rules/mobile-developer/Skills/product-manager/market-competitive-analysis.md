# Market and Competitive Analysis

## Purpose
Assess market structure, alternatives, competitors, and changing forces without turning product decisions into feature imitation.

## When to use
Use for strategy, positioning, market entry, pricing, major roadmap choices, and meaningful competitor moves.

## Inputs
Customer research, market reports, public competitor material, win/loss evidence, sales feedback, product telemetry, and pricing information.

## Context to inspect
Review direct competitors, substitutes, internal/manual alternatives, segment differences, switching costs, channels, and regulatory or technology shifts.

## Core knowledge
Customers compare outcomes and total switching cost, not feature matrices alone. Competitive advantage may come from workflow, data, distribution, trust, cost structure, integration, or speed.

## Procedure
1. Define the market and customer segment being analyzed.
2. Identify direct, indirect, and do-nothing alternatives.
3. Compare customer outcomes, positioning, business models, and switching friction.
4. Gather win/loss and churn evidence where available.
5. Identify underserved needs and structural trends.
6. Separate durable advantages from easily copied features.
7. State implications for positioning, strategy, and experiments.
8. Track only signals that can change a decision.

## Decision points
Respond to a competitor when evidence shows material customer or strategic impact. Ignore parity pressure when it conflicts with positioning or lacks demand evidence.

## Common failure patterns
Feature checklists, treating market-size estimates as demand, copying announcements, relying on sales anecdotes alone, and ignoring substitutes.

## Verification
Claims have evidence and dates; alternatives include non-software options; implications connect to explicit product decisions.

## Expected output
A concise market map, competitive hypotheses, evidence, strategic implications, and monitoring signals.

## Stop conditions
Stop when required information is confidential or unlawfully obtained, or when market definition is too ambiguous for meaningful comparison.
