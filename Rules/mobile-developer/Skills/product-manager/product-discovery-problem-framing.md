# Product Discovery and Problem Framing

## Purpose
Turn ambiguous product opportunities into evidence-based problem statements and testable outcomes before committing delivery capacity.

## When to use
Use for new initiatives, weakly defined requests, roadmap candidates, or features with uncertain customer value. Do not use discovery to delay a well-understood mandatory change.

## Inputs
Business goals, customer evidence, analytics, support themes, stakeholder requests, constraints, and existing product behavior.

## Preconditions
Identify the decision owner and the decision discovery must enable. Separate facts from assumptions.

## Context to inspect
Review user journeys, prior experiments, product metrics, market context, operational constraints, and existing alternatives.

## Core knowledge
Strong discovery reduces uncertainty about problem, user, value, usability, feasibility, and viability. Outputs should be falsifiable rather than feature-shaped.

## Procedure
1. Define the business or user outcome at stake.
2. Identify affected user segments and jobs.
3. Gather quantitative and qualitative evidence.
4. Map symptoms to plausible root problems.
5. State assumptions and confidence levels.
6. Write a problem statement without prescribing a solution.
7. Define measurable evidence that would confirm or reject it.
8. Identify the cheapest research or experiment that reduces the largest uncertainty.
9. Record findings and update the opportunity decision.

## Decision points
Choose interviews for motivations and context, analytics for behavioral scale, prototypes for usability, and experiments for causal value hypotheses. Combine methods when one source cannot resolve uncertainty.

## Common failure patterns
Starting with a requested feature, interviewing only internal stakeholders, treating anecdotes as prevalence, collecting research without a decision, and confusing customer preference with demonstrated behavior.

## Verification
Evidence traces to the problem statement; assumptions are explicit; target users and desired outcomes are measurable; next decisions are clear.

## Expected output
A validated or rejected problem statement, evidence summary, uncertainty register, and recommended next experiment or delivery decision.

## Stop conditions
Stop and escalate when evidence cannot be obtained ethically, the initiative is purely mandatory, or decision authority is unresolved.
