# Product Economics and Business Cases

## Purpose
Evaluate whether product investments create sustainable economic value while acknowledging uncertainty and strategic option value.

## When to use
Use for major investments, pricing changes, market expansion, build-versus-buy, infrastructure-heavy features, and portfolio trade-offs.

## Inputs
Revenue mechanisms, costs, adoption assumptions, retention, margin, implementation effort, ongoing operations, risks, and strategic benefits.

## Context to inspect
Inspect unit economics, acquisition and servicing costs, gross margin, contractual effects, capacity, opportunity cost, and sensitivity to key assumptions.

## Core knowledge
A business case is a decision model, not a promise. Separate known data from assumptions and test which variables materially change the decision.

## Procedure
1. Define the investment decision and alternatives including doing nothing.
2. Identify value mechanisms and affected segments.
3. Estimate incremental revenue, savings, risk reduction, or strategic option value.
4. Estimate build, migration, support, infrastructure, and ongoing costs.
5. Model timing and adoption assumptions.
6. Calculate relevant economic indicators appropriate to the organization.
7. Run sensitivity scenarios on uncertain drivers.
8. Identify non-financial constraints and strategic effects.
9. State confidence and evidence gaps.
10. Reconcile actual outcomes after investment.

## Decision points
Use lightweight economics for reversible small bets; require deeper modeling for large, irreversible, or margin-sensitive investments.

## Common failure patterns
Counting gross revenue as value, excluding ongoing costs, optimistic adoption without evidence, false precision, and ignoring the do-nothing alternative.

## Verification
Formulas and assumptions are reviewable; scenarios expose key sensitivities; economics reconcile with finance definitions.

## Expected output
A transparent business case with alternatives, assumptions, scenarios, risks, and recommendation.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when financial definitions, cost ownership, or required finance approvals are unresolved.
