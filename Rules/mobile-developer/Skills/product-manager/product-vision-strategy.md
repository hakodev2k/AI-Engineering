# Product Vision and Strategy

## Purpose
Create a coherent product direction that links customer value, business advantage, constraints, and investment choices.

## When to use
Use when defining or refreshing product direction, entering a market, aligning multiple teams, or resolving competing roadmap themes.

## Inputs
Company strategy, customer problems, market evidence, product performance, capabilities, constraints, and competitive alternatives.

## Context to inspect
Inspect current positioning, customer segments, economics, technical assets, distribution, dependencies, and strategic commitments.

## Core knowledge
Strategy is a set of choices about where to play, how to win, what capabilities matter, and what not to pursue. A vision describes a durable desired future; it is not a feature roadmap.

## Procedure
1. Translate company goals into product-level strategic context.
2. Define target customers and valuable problems.
3. Identify differentiated strengths and constraints.
4. Describe the future customer state in a concise vision.
5. Choose strategic pillars and explicit non-goals.
6. Define leading and lagging measures for each pillar.
7. Test coherence against economics, technology, operations, and market evidence.
8. Communicate choices and implications to stakeholders.
9. Revisit when assumptions materially change.

## Decision points
Prefer focus when resources or differentiation are limited. Broaden scope only when adjacent segments share capabilities, distribution, and economics.

## Common failure patterns
Vision as slogans, strategy as a backlog, copying competitors, serving every segment, omitting trade-offs, and changing direction from isolated requests.

## Verification
Teams can explain target users, problems, differentiation, priorities, non-goals, and success measures consistently.

## Expected output
Product vision, strategic choices, pillars, non-goals, assumptions, and measurable outcomes.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when company strategy conflicts with product economics, critical market evidence is missing, or executive choices are required.
