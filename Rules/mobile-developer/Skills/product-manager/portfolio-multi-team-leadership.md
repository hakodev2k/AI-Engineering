# Portfolio and Multi-Team Product Leadership

## Purpose
Manage a product area spanning multiple teams by allocating investment across outcomes, platforms, lifecycle stages, and strategic options.

## When to use
Use when a Senior Product Manager owns several squads, a product suite, shared capabilities, or a portfolio with competing investments.

## Inputs
Product strategy, team missions, portfolio metrics, roadmaps, capacity, economics, dependencies, risks, and lifecycle data.

## Context to inspect
Inspect duplicated work, shared platforms, dependency bottlenecks, customer journey fragmentation, team cognitive load, and strategic coverage gaps.

## Core knowledge
Portfolio management optimizes the system rather than maximizing each team's local backlog. Team missions should be stable enough for ownership but revisable when strategy changes.

## Procedure
1. Map portfolio products, teams, customers, and strategic outcomes.
2. Identify shared capabilities and cross-team dependencies.
3. Evaluate investment by outcome, lifecycle, economics, risk, and option value.
4. Define or refine team missions and decision boundaries.
5. Allocate capacity across growth, core quality, platform, risk, and lifecycle work.
6. Remove duplicated initiatives and conflicting incentives.
7. Sequence dependencies to minimize coordination overhead.
8. Establish portfolio-level metrics and review cadence.
9. Rebalance when evidence, capacity, or strategy changes.
10. Communicate what will not be funded and why.

## Decision points
Create shared platforms when repeated demand and stable abstractions justify central ownership. Keep teams independent when coordination cost exceeds reuse benefit.

## Common failure patterns
Portfolio as combined backlogs, equal funding regardless of opportunity, too many dependencies, unstable team missions, and local metrics that harm end-to-end outcomes.

## Verification
Investments map to strategy, ownership is clear, dependency load is visible, and portfolio metrics reveal trade-offs across teams.

## Expected output
Portfolio investment model, team missions, dependency strategy, measures, and explicit allocation decisions.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when organizational ownership or budget authority prevents coherent allocation across the product area.
