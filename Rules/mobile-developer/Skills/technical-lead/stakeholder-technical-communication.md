# Stakeholder Technical Communication

## Purpose
Communicate technical decisions, risks, progress, and constraints in language appropriate to engineers, product partners, and leadership.

## When to use
Use for design alignment, status updates, incidents, trade-off discussions, estimates, and risk escalation.

## Inputs
Audience, decision, evidence, risks, options, requested action, timeline.

## Context to inspect
Inspect what the audience needs to decide, their technical depth, prior commitments, and consequences of misunderstanding.

## Core knowledge
Effective technical communication separates facts, assumptions, options, and recommendations. Detail should serve a decision rather than demonstrate expertise.

## Procedure
1. Identify the audience and required decision/action.
2. Lead with outcome, impact, or risk.
3. Separate confirmed evidence from assumptions.
4. Explain only the technical detail needed to understand consequences.
5. Present options with meaningful trade-offs.
6. State a recommendation and rationale when responsible for one.
7. Quantify uncertainty instead of hiding it.
8. Record decisions and owners.
9. Confirm shared understanding for high-impact matters.
10. Update stakeholders when material evidence changes.

## Decision points
Use written records for durable decisions; synchronous discussion for ambiguity or conflict; dashboards for continuously changing operational state.

## Common failure patterns
Jargon-heavy updates, burying risks, false certainty, reporting activity instead of outcomes, and escalating without a requested decision.

## Verification
Recipients can state impact, decision, owner, risk, and next action accurately.

## Expected output
Decision-oriented communication matched to audience and backed by evidence.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when communication reveals conflicting authority, commitments, or risk tolerance that the technical lead cannot resolve.
