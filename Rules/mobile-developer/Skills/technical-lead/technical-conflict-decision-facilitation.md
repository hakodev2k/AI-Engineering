# Technical Conflict and Decision Facilitation

## Purpose
Resolve technical disagreement through explicit criteria, evidence, and accountable decision-making while preserving healthy dissent.

## When to use
Use when design discussions stall, senior engineers disagree, or local preferences block progress.

## Inputs
Competing proposals, requirements, constraints, evidence, decision ownership, timeline.

## Context to inspect
Inspect whether disagreement is about goals, facts, risk tolerance, values, ownership, or implementation detail.

## Core knowledge
Consensus is useful but not always required. Good facilitation separates positions from underlying concerns and makes decision rules explicit before debating solutions.

## Procedure
1. Restate the shared outcome.
2. Identify the exact decision and owner.
3. Ask each proposal to state assumptions and strongest trade-offs.
4. Agree evaluation criteria.
5. Gather missing evidence or run bounded experiments.
6. Distinguish reversible from irreversible choices.
7. Decide using evidence and assigned authority when consensus does not emerge.
8. Record rationale and dissenting risks.
9. Define revisit triggers.
10. Ensure the team commits to execution after the decision.

## Decision points
Seek consensus for high-collaboration choices when time permits; use accountable decision ownership when delay costs exceed the value of further debate.

## Common failure patterns
Authority disguised as consensus, endless debate, personality-driven arguments, moving criteria after results, and revisiting settled decisions without new evidence.

## Verification
Participants can explain the decision criteria and next action even if their preferred option was not chosen.

## Expected output
A timely documented decision with rationale, owner, risks, and revisit conditions.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when conflict involves organizational authority, conduct concerns, or business priorities outside technical ownership.
