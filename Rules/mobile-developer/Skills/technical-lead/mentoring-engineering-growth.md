# Mentoring and Engineering Growth

## Purpose
Increase team capability through targeted coaching, delegation, feedback, and progressively broader ownership.

## When to use
Use during project work, reviews, design discussions, onboarding, and recurring skill gaps.

## Inputs
Observed work, role expectations, goals, project opportunities, feedback history.

## Context to inspect
Inspect demonstrated strengths, recurring gaps, task complexity, psychological safety, and available stretch opportunities.

## Core knowledge
Mentoring should build independent judgment rather than dependence on the lead. Feedback is strongest when specific, timely, behavior-based, and paired with practice opportunities.

## Procedure
1. Identify a concrete capability to strengthen.
2. Establish current evidence and desired behavior.
3. Explain principles and reasoning, not only answers.
4. Delegate a bounded task with clear success criteria.
5. Allow the engineer to propose decisions first.
6. Review outcomes and reasoning.
7. Give specific reinforcing and corrective feedback.
8. Increase scope as competence grows.
9. Encourage teaching or documentation to consolidate learning.
10. Reassess progress from repeated evidence.

## Decision points
Coach when the person can safely discover the answer; direct when urgency or risk demands immediate guidance.

## Common failure patterns
Taking work back too quickly, vague feedback, mentoring only top performers, and confusing personal style with engineering quality.

## Verification
The engineer handles similar decisions with less intervention and can explain trade-offs independently.

## Expected output
Improved capability, explicit growth goals, and increased autonomous ownership.

## Stop conditions
Escalate formal performance concerns through appropriate management channels rather than turning mentoring into unofficial discipline.
