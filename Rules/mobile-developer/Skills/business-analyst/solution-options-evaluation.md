# Solution Options Evaluation

## Purpose
Compare solution approaches against business outcomes, constraints, risk, cost, change impact, and operational fit without defaulting to the first proposed option.

## When to use
Use when stakeholders are choosing between process, policy, build, buy, integration, automation, or phased-delivery alternatives.

## Inputs
Business objectives, requirements, constraints, candidate options, cost assumptions, risks, architecture input, and operational context.

## Preconditions
Evaluation criteria and decision authority are identifiable.

## Context to inspect
Business value, delivery time, dependencies, user impact, operational burden, compliance, data implications, reversibility, and strategic alignment.

## Core knowledge
The BA should make trade-offs visible rather than pretending one option is universally best. Criteria must reflect business priorities and not be reverse-engineered to favor a preferred solution.

## Procedure
1. Restate the problem and required outcomes.
2. Identify viable alternatives, including process or policy changes where relevant.
3. Define weighted evaluation criteria with decision owners.
4. Gather evidence for each option.
5. Compare benefits, costs, risks, dependencies, and change impacts.
6. Highlight assumptions and uncertainty.
7. Identify minimum viable or phased variants.
8. Perform sensitivity checks on material assumptions.
9. Present trade-offs and recommendation neutrally.
10. Record the decision and rationale.

## Decision points
Prefer an option with lower complexity when value is similar; prefer reversible experiments when uncertainty is high.

## Common failure patterns
Comparing only implementation cost, hiding uncertainty, excluding the do-nothing option, and treating stakeholder preference as evidence.

## Verification
Confirm evaluation criteria were agreed before scoring, evidence is traceable, and decision owners understand material trade-offs.

## Expected output
An options assessment with criteria, evidence, assumptions, risks, recommendation, and recorded decision.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when required cost, risk, or feasibility evidence is unavailable for a material decision.
