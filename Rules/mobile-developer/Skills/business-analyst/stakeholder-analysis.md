# Stakeholder Analysis

## Purpose
Identify who influences, decides, executes, funds, supports, or is affected by a change, and plan engagement accordingly.

## When to use
Use at initiative start, during scope changes, when decisions stall, or when adoption risk is high.

## Inputs
Organization context, project charter, process maps, system ownership, governance model, and known stakeholder concerns.

## Preconditions
A defined initiative or change area exists.

## Context to inspect
Decision rights, influence, impact, expertise, incentives, availability, dependencies, and potential resistance.

## Core knowledge
Stakeholder analysis is dynamic. Senior BAs distinguish formal authority from practical influence and avoid treating all stakeholders as equal participants.

## Procedure
1. Identify stakeholder groups and named decision owners.
2. Classify their role in decisions, delivery, operation, and adoption.
3. Assess influence, impact, interest, and risk.
4. Identify information each stakeholder must provide or approve.
5. Record likely conflicts and incentive mismatches.
6. Define engagement cadence and communication style.
7. Confirm escalation paths for unresolved decisions.
8. Reassess after scope or organizational changes.

## Decision points
Engage high-impact/high-influence stakeholders closely; keep low-impact groups informed without overloading governance.

## Common failure patterns
Focusing only on sponsors, ignoring front-line users, assuming job title equals influence, and failing to identify actual approvers.

## Verification
Confirm critical decisions have owners, impacted groups are represented, and no major operational stakeholder is missing.

## Expected output
A stakeholder map with roles, influence, impact, engagement needs, decision rights, and escalation paths.

## Stop conditions
Escalate when authority conflicts cannot be resolved or required stakeholder access is blocked.
