# Business Outcomes and KPI Definition

## Purpose
Define measurable outcomes and indicators that show whether a change solved the intended business problem after delivery.

## When to use
Use during discovery, option evaluation, benefit definition, process improvement, product changes, and post-implementation review.

## Inputs
Business objectives, current performance data, stakeholder goals, process metrics, customer outcomes, constraints, and available measurement sources.

## Preconditions
The business problem and intended change are understood well enough to define success.

## Context to inspect
Baseline performance, data quality, metric ownership, reporting cadence, leading and lagging indicators, operational incentives, and possible unintended effects.

## Core knowledge
A KPI must support a decision and connect to an outcome. Senior BAs distinguish activity metrics from outcome metrics, define baselines and targets, and watch for measures that encourage harmful behavior.

## Procedure
1. Restate the business objective in outcome language.
2. Identify behaviors or results that would demonstrate success.
3. Establish current baseline where evidence exists.
4. Define a small set of meaningful leading and lagging indicators.
5. Specify calculation, source, frequency, segmentation, and owner.
6. Set target or threshold with rationale.
7. Identify counter-metrics for important unintended consequences.
8. Confirm data can be collected reliably.
9. Link requirements and process changes to expected metric movement.
10. Define when and how benefits will be reviewed after release.

## Decision points
Prefer outcome measures over delivery or activity counts. Use proxy metrics only when direct measurement is impractical and the proxy relationship is understood.

## Common failure patterns
Choosing vanity metrics, setting targets without baselines, measuring what is easy rather than what matters, and ignoring metric gaming or adverse side effects.

## Verification
Confirm every KPI has a definition, owner, data source, baseline or rationale, review cadence, and clear relationship to the business objective.

## Expected output
A business outcome and KPI specification with definitions, baselines, targets, ownership, data sources, and review plan.

## Stop conditions
Stop when success cannot be measured with available evidence and stakeholders cannot agree on a defensible proxy or qualitative acceptance method.
