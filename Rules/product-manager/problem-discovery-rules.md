# Problem Discovery Rules
## Purpose
Prevent solution-first planning and validate that important user problems are real.
## Scope
Problem framing, discovery, opportunity assessment, and hypothesis formation.
## MUST
- State the user, context, problem, current workaround, frequency, severity, and business relevance before proposing a major solution.
- Separate observed evidence from interpretation and assumptions.
- Bound uncertainty before large commitments through research, data, prototypes, or equivalent evidence.
## MUST NOT
- Use one anecdote as proof of broad demand.
- Define the problem solely as absence of a preferred feature.
## SHOULD
- Compare alternative problem explanations and underserved segments.
## Exceptions
Urgent regulatory, contractual, or safety work may proceed with reduced discovery when rationale and risk are recorded.
## Verification
Review discovery artifacts, evidence sources, assumptions, rejected hypotheses, and links between problem evidence and proposed investment.
