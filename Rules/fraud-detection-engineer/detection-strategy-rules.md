# Detection Strategy Rules

## Purpose
Define evidence-driven fraud detection strategy that balances loss prevention, customer impact, and operational capacity.

## Scope
Applies to fraud controls, detection programs, and material strategy changes.

## MUST
- Detection objectives MUST map to explicit fraud-loss, abuse, customer-friction, or compliance outcomes.
- Material controls MUST define target population, decision point, expected benefit, failure modes, and measurable success criteria.
- Strategy changes MUST be evaluated against both fraud capture and legitimate-user impact.
- High-risk changes MUST have an owner, rollout plan, monitoring plan, and rollback criteria.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT optimize detection rate while ignoring false positives or downstream operational cost.
- MUST NOT deploy a material strategy solely on intuition or model score distributions.

## SHOULD
- Strategies SHOULD prefer reversible, progressively deployable controls when equivalent protection is possible.
- Detection portfolios SHOULD combine preventive, detective, and investigative controls where appropriate.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented rationale, evidence, risk, alternative considered, validation plan, and accountable approval.

## Verification
Review strategy documents, experiment results, loss metrics, false-positive metrics, rollout records, and post-launch monitoring.
