# Roadmap Prioritization Rules

## Purpose
Ensure AI roadmap choices reflect validated value, risk, dependencies, and evidence rather than novelty or stakeholder volume.

## Scope
Applies to roadmap sequencing, portfolio trade-offs, opportunity scoring, and commitment decisions.

## MUST
- Prioritization MUST consider expected user value, strategic fit, delivery cost, operational burden, technical uncertainty, safety risk, and reversibility.
- High-uncertainty initiatives MUST include an evidence-gathering milestone before large irreversible investment.
- Dependencies that can block value realization MUST be identified before committing target dates.
- Material roadmap changes MUST document what was deprioritized and why.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT rank initiatives solely by executive request, competitor parity, or estimated revenue without considering risk and evidence quality.
- MUST NOT present speculative dates as committed delivery dates.
- MUST NOT hide foundational work required for safe and reliable AI behavior.

## SHOULD
- Prefer sequencing that retires the largest product or technical uncertainties early.
- Roadmaps SHOULD reserve capacity for evaluation, safety, reliability, and post-launch learning.

## Exceptions
Urgent exceptions require documented rationale, explicit trade-offs, decision owner, and downstream risk acceptance.

## Verification
Inspect prioritization criteria, initiative evidence, dependency maps, decision records, and roadmap change history.
