# Transparency and User Trust Rules

## Purpose
Ensure users understand when AI is involved, what it can do, and where its outputs require judgment.

## Scope
Applies to product copy, disclosures, confidence communication, provenance, generated content, and user expectations.

## MUST
- Users MUST be informed when AI-generated or AI-mediated behavior materially affects their experience or decisions.
- Product claims MUST accurately reflect demonstrated capability, known limitations, and the degree of automation.
- High-impact outputs MUST provide appropriate context, provenance, uncertainty, or review cues when users need them to act safely.
- Correction, feedback, or appeal mechanisms MUST be available when errors can materially affect users.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT imply human authorship, review, certainty, or expertise when it did not occur.
- MUST NOT use anthropomorphic framing to obscure system limitations or accountability.
- MUST NOT hide material automation behind ambiguous UX when disclosure is necessary for informed use.

## SHOULD
- Explanations SHOULD focus on actionable limitations rather than generic disclaimers.
- Trust messaging SHOULD be validated with user research rather than assumed.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented user impact, legal or policy considerations, and approval from the accountable product authority.

## Verification
Review user-facing copy, onboarding, disclosures, provenance UI, feedback paths, user research, and support documentation against actual system behavior.
