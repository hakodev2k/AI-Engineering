# Problem Framing Rules

## Purpose
Ensure AI product work starts from a validated user or business problem rather than from a model capability seeking a use case.

## Scope
Applies to discovery, opportunity definition, PRDs, roadmap proposals, and major feature changes.

## MUST
- The problem statement MUST identify the affected user, context, current pain, desired outcome, and evidence that the problem exists.
- Proposed AI functionality MUST explain why probabilistic automation or generation is preferable to deterministic alternatives.
- Success criteria MUST be defined before solution commitment and MUST include user-value measures, not only model metrics.
- Material assumptions MUST be recorded and assigned a validation method.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT treat model novelty, benchmark performance, or competitor activity as sufficient evidence of customer value.
- MUST NOT define success solely as launch completion or feature adoption.
- MUST NOT conceal unresolved problem ambiguity behind implementation detail.

## SHOULD
- Problem framing SHOULD distinguish frequency, severity, willingness to change behavior, and economic impact.
- Alternatives SHOULD include non-AI workflow, policy, UX, or process changes.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented urgency, missing evidence, decision owner, bounded risk, and a dated validation plan.

## Verification
Review the discovery record, problem statement, evidence sources, assumptions, alternatives, and success criteria. A reviewer should be able to trace each proposed capability to a validated need.
