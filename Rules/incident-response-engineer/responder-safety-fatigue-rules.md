# Responder Safety and Fatigue Rules

## Purpose
Protect response quality and people during prolonged or high-stress incidents.

## Scope
Staffing, rotations, decision load, escalation, and sustained response.

## MUST
- Add or rotate responders when fatigue, workload, or duration materially threatens safe judgment.
- Ensure critical actions receive an independent check when cognitive load is high and time permits.
- Escalate staffing gaps that leave essential command, operations, or expertise uncovered.
- Preserve reasonable breaks and handoffs during prolonged response.

## MUST NOT
- Treat exhaustion as evidence of commitment or require one responder to remain the sole knowledge holder.
- Allow avoidable multitasking to compromise high-risk production actions.

## SHOULD
- Separate incident command from deep debugging on complex incidents and maintain backup role coverage.

## Exceptions
Short-lived emergencies may temporarily concentrate roles, but workload and fatigue MUST be reassessed as soon as stabilization begins.

## Verification
Review role assignments, shift duration, handoffs, action-review records, and whether fatigue-related risks were identified and addressed.
