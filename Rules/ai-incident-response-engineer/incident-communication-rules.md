# Incident Communication Rules

## Purpose
Communicate AI incident status accurately without overstating uncertain conclusions or exposing sensitive information.

## Scope
Applies to internal updates, stakeholder communication, customer-facing status, and executive reporting.

## MUST
- Communications MUST distinguish confirmed facts, current impact, hypotheses, mitigations, unknowns, and next steps.
- Material status changes MUST be communicated at a cadence appropriate to severity.
- Public or customer claims about safety, security, privacy, or root cause MUST be reviewed by authorized stakeholders when required.
- Technical terminology MUST be translated into impact and risk appropriate to the audience without changing factual meaning.
- Corrections MUST be issued when prior material statements become inaccurate.
- Sensitive evidence MUST be shared only through approved channels and to appropriate recipients.

## MUST NOT
- Responders MUST NOT present model-generated summaries as verified incident facts without checking source evidence.
- Communications MUST NOT speculate about attribution, breach scope, or regulatory obligations as established fact.
- Incident updates MUST NOT disclose credentials, protected data, exploit details, or unnecessary harmful content.

## SHOULD
- Use stable timestamps, severity, impact scope, and next-update expectations.
- Maintain a single reviewed source for externally communicated incident facts.

## Exceptions
Urgent warnings may precede complete analysis when immediate user action is necessary; uncertainty must be explicit.

## Verification
Review incident updates against timeline evidence, approval records, correction history, and information-classification requirements.
