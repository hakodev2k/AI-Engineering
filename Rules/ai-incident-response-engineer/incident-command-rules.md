# Incident Command Rules

## Purpose
Provide clear authority, coordination, and decision ownership during AI incidents.

## Scope
Applies to incident command, technical leads, operations, communications, and specialist responders.

## MUST
- High-severity incidents MUST have a clearly identified incident commander or equivalent authority.
- Roles for technical investigation, operations, communications, and specialist review MUST be assigned when needed.
- Major decisions MUST identify decision owner, evidence, time, and resulting action.
- The commander MUST maintain a current incident objective and prioritize containment, impact assessment, recovery, and communication.
- Handoffs MUST transfer current state, unresolved risks, active hypotheses, pending actions, and authority.
- Safety, security, privacy, or legal specialists MUST be engaged when incident characteristics require their authority.

## MUST NOT
- Decision authority MUST NOT be ambiguous for destructive or high-risk actions.
- Responders MUST NOT bypass incident coordination with conflicting production changes.
- Status meetings MUST NOT replace active evidence collection or remediation work.

## SHOULD
- Separate command coordination from deep technical investigation during complex incidents.
- Use a shared timeline and action tracker as the operational source of truth.

## Exceptions
Small low-severity incidents may combine roles, but decision ownership and escalation path must remain explicit.

## Verification
Inspect incident timeline, role assignments, handoff records, decisions, and escalation history.
