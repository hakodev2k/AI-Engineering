# Analysis and Hypothesis Rules

## Purpose
Make forensic reasoning evidence-led, falsifiable, and resistant to confirmation bias.

## Scope
Applies to investigative hypotheses, artifact interpretation, correlation, and causal conclusions.

## MUST
- Material hypotheses MUST identify supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, and plausible alternatives.
- Analysts MUST distinguish fact, interpretation, inference, and unknown.
- Negative evidence MUST be used only when collection and detection coverage are sufficient.
- High-impact conclusions MUST be reproducible from cited evidence.
- New evidence that contradicts a working theory MUST trigger reassessment.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT select only artifacts that support an initial theory.
- MUST NOT treat correlation as causation without additional evidence.
- MUST NOT use analyst confidence as a substitute for evidence.
- MUST NOT hide unresolved contradictions.

## SHOULD
- Form competing hypotheses for ambiguous events.
- Seek disconfirming evidence before finalizing consequential conclusions.

## Exceptions
Time-critical triage may use provisional hypotheses if explicitly labeled and separated from final findings.

## Verification
Peer-review the evidence matrix, reproduce key queries, inspect contradictory artifacts, and test whether conclusions survive reasonable alternative explanations.
