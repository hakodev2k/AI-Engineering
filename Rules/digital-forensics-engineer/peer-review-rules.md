# Peer Review Rules

## Purpose
Reduce analyst error and ensure consequential forensic conclusions survive independent scrutiny.

## Scope
Applies to high-impact findings, novel methods, attribution, destructive-action recommendations, and final reports.

## MUST
- Material conclusions MUST receive review proportional to impact and uncertainty.
- Reviewers MUST have access to cited evidence, methods, tool versions, and limitations.
- Critical calculations, timeline transformations, and artifact interpretations MUST be reproducible.
- Reviewer disagreements affecting conclusions MUST be resolved or documented.
- The original analyst MUST preserve evidence of substantive corrections.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT treat stylistic proofreading as technical peer review.
- MUST NOT suppress dissent to create apparent certainty.
- MUST NOT approve a finding that cannot be traced to evidence.

## SHOULD
- Use an independent analyst for attribution, novel artifacts, and legally consequential conclusions.
- Blind reviewers to unnecessary narrative assumptions when practical.

## Exceptions
Time-critical incident triage may precede full review, but provisional status and required follow-up MUST be explicit.

## Verification
Inspect review records, reproduced queries, correction history, unresolved comments, and reviewer evidence references.
