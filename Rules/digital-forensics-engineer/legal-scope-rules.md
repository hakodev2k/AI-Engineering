# Legal Scope and Authority Rules

## Purpose
Keep forensic activity within explicit authority and approved investigative scope.

## Scope
Applies to collection, search, access, decryption, preservation, disclosure, and destructive or intrusive actions.

## MUST
- Authority and scope MUST be established before accessing non-public evidence.
- Collection criteria MUST identify relevant systems, accounts, time ranges, and data classes when known.
- Scope ambiguity that could expose materially unrelated private data MUST be escalated before expansion.
- New evidence sources discovered outside scope MUST be preserved only as authorized and otherwise left unexamined.
- Legal holds, retention requirements, and disclosure restrictions MUST be honored.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT expand investigative access merely because credentials or technical access exist.
- MUST NOT bypass access controls without explicit authority.
- MUST NOT destroy, alter, disclose, or export evidence contrary to applicable authorization.

## SHOULD
- Use the least intrusive collection method that meets evidentiary objectives.
- Record scope decisions and approvals with case records.

## Exceptions
Emergency preservation without full examination may be justified to prevent imminent loss where policy permits; document authority, necessity, boundaries, and follow-up approval.

## Verification
Review authorization records, collection filters, access logs, search terms, export scope, retention controls, and approval evidence.
