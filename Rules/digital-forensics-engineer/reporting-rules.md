# Forensic Reporting Rules

## Purpose
Produce reports that are accurate, reproducible, scoped, and understandable to technical and non-technical reviewers.

## Scope
Applies to interim findings, final reports, exhibits, executive summaries, and technical appendices.

## MUST
- Reports MUST distinguish observed facts, interpretation, inference, and opinion.
- Material findings MUST cite evidence identifiers and reproducible analysis steps.
- Scope, methods, limitations, relevant errors, and unresolved contradictions MUST be disclosed.
- Dates and times MUST identify timezone or normalization basis.
- Confidence language MUST be consistent and supported.
- Corrections to issued reports MUST be traceable.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT omit material exculpatory or contradictory evidence.
- MUST NOT include unnecessary secrets or sensitive personal data.
- MUST NOT claim completeness beyond collection coverage.
- MUST NOT use screenshots as the sole evidence when underlying data can be preserved.

## SHOULD
- Use concise conclusions followed by technical substantiation.
- Preserve query, filter, and tool details in appendices when useful.

## Exceptions
Audience-specific summaries may omit technical detail only when the underlying complete report remains available and the summary does not distort findings.

## Verification
Peer-review citations, reproduce sampled findings, check time normalization, validate redactions, and reconcile report statements with case evidence.
