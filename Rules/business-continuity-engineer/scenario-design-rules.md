# Scenario Design Rules

## Purpose
Ensure continuity scenarios challenge real vulnerabilities and produce decision-useful evidence.

## Scope
Applies to scenarios used for continuity analysis, exercises, simulations, recovery testing, and crisis rehearsals.

## MUST
- Scenarios MUST be tied to credible threats, dependency failures, control weaknesses, or business-impact hypotheses.
- Scenario severity and duration MUST be sufficient to challenge the capabilities being evaluated.
- Scenarios MUST define which conditions are assumed, injected, simulated, and actually exercised.
- Critical scenarios MUST consider correlated failures and degraded dependencies where credible.
- Scenario design MUST preserve safety, legal, security, and production boundaries.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT design scenarios only around capabilities already known to work.
- MUST NOT hide material assumptions that make the scenario easier than the stated event.
- MUST NOT introduce uncontrolled production impact merely to increase realism.

## SHOULD
- Rotate scenarios to cover technology, workforce, facility, supplier, cyber, regional, and process disruptions as relevant.
- Use recent incidents and exercise findings to improve scenario realism.

## Exceptions
Reduced-scope scenarios require documented objectives, excluded risks, reason, and follow-up assurance where coverage remains incomplete.

## Verification
Review scenario rationale, risk linkage, assumptions, injects, safety controls, coverage history, and resulting evidence against intended objectives.
