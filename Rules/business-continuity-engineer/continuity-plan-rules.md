# Continuity Plan Rules

## Purpose
Ensure continuity plans are executable under disruption rather than merely descriptive.

## Scope
Applies to plans for maintaining or restoring critical business capabilities during significant disruption.

## MUST
- Plans MUST define activation criteria, authority, roles, priorities, dependencies, recovery sequence, communication paths, and exit criteria.
- Critical procedures MUST be executable with the information and access expected to remain available during the disruption scenario.
- Plans MUST identify alternate methods when normal systems, locations, communications, or personnel are unavailable.
- Plan owners MUST maintain current contact and escalation information through an approved process.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT depend solely on resources that may become unavailable in the same scenario the plan addresses.
- MUST NOT describe responsibilities without assigning accountable roles or decision authority.

## SHOULD
- Keep operational instructions concise enough for use under pressure.
- Separate stable policy from frequently changing contact and operational data where this improves maintainability.

## Exceptions
Any missing continuity element requires a documented gap, interim control, owner, approval, and remediation date.

## Verification
Conduct walkthroughs and scenario exercises; verify plan access, role assignments, contact accuracy, dependencies, procedures, and recorded remediation of exercise findings.
