# Continuity Metrics Rules

## Purpose
Measure continuity readiness with evidence that supports risk and investment decisions.

## Scope
Applies to readiness indicators, recovery performance, plan coverage, exercise outcomes, remediation, and executive reporting.

## MUST
- Continuity metrics MUST have defined calculation, scope, source, owner, reporting cadence, and interpretation.
- Metrics MUST distinguish documented capability from tested or demonstrated capability.
- Recovery performance MUST compare actual or exercised outcomes with approved objectives where measurement is feasible.
- Material overdue findings and unresolved recovery gaps MUST remain visible until verified closure or approved risk acceptance.
- Trend conclusions MUST be supported by comparable data and disclosed scope changes.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT represent plan existence or exercise participation alone as proof of recoverability.
- MUST NOT manipulate thresholds, denominators, exclusions, or reporting periods to conceal deteriorating readiness.
- MUST NOT treat an aggregate green status as sufficient when a critical capability has a material unresolved gap.

## SHOULD
- Combine leading indicators such as plan freshness and test coverage with outcome indicators such as achieved recovery time and failed objectives.
- Use metrics to prioritize remediation rather than maximize superficial compliance scores.

## Exceptions
Metric limitations require documented data-quality constraints, interpretation caveats, owner, and improvement plan where material.

## Verification
Inspect metric definitions, source data, calculations, dashboards, sampling, trend history, exercise evidence, and reconciliation to open risks and findings.
