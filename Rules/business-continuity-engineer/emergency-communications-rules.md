# Emergency Communications Rules

## Purpose
Ensure disruption communications are timely, accurate, authorized, and resilient to channel failure.

## Scope
Applies to internal, executive, workforce, customer, supplier, regulator, and public communications during continuity events.

## MUST
- Communication plans MUST define audiences, authorized senders, approval paths, channels, alternates, and update cadence by scenario severity.
- Material statements MUST distinguish confirmed facts from estimates, assumptions, and unresolved questions.
- Sensitive, personal, security, legal, and commercially restricted information MUST be handled according to applicable controls.
- Critical contact lists and alternate channels MUST be maintained and periodically tested.
- Corrections MUST be issued promptly when previously communicated material facts become inaccurate.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT speculate publicly or present unverified recovery estimates as commitments.
- MUST NOT disclose confidential incident details merely to increase message completeness.
- MUST NOT rely on a single communication platform for critical notifications.

## SHOULD
- Use preapproved templates for common scenarios while adapting facts to the event.
- Coordinate messages across business, technical, legal, security, and communications owners when relevant.

## Exceptions
Urgent communications may follow emergency approval paths, but the sender, rationale, evidence, and subsequent review MUST be recorded.

## Verification
Review communication matrices, channel tests, templates, approval records, event timelines, correction handling, and exercise outcomes.
