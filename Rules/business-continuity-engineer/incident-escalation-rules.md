# Incident Escalation Rules

## Purpose
Ensure disruptions reach the right authority before business impact exceeds tolerated limits.

## Scope
Applies to escalation from operational incidents into continuity, crisis, supplier, executive, legal, or regulatory response paths.

## MUST
- Escalation criteria MUST use observable impact, duration, scope, safety, security, legal, and recovery-risk indicators.
- Escalation paths MUST identify primary and alternate contacts, required information, and decision authority.
- Incidents approaching recovery or impact thresholds MUST be escalated before those thresholds are breached when reasonably foreseeable.
- Escalations MUST preserve a timestamped record of trigger, evidence, recipient, and resulting decision.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT delay escalation solely to protect performance metrics, reputation, or ownership boundaries.
- MUST NOT depend on a single individual being reachable for a critical escalation path.

## SHOULD
- Automate threshold notification when reliable telemetry exists.
- Periodically test escalation routes outside active incidents.

## Exceptions
Alternative escalation paths require documented authority and must preserve equivalent accountability and traceability.

## Verification
Review incident timelines, escalation matrices, contact tests, alert rules, exercise evidence, and cases where thresholds were approached or exceeded.
