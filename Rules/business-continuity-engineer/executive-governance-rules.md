# Executive Governance Rules

## Purpose
Ensure continuity risk, authority, investment, and residual exposure are governed by accountable decision-makers.

## Scope
Applies to executive oversight, continuity policy, risk acceptance, investment priorities, crisis authority, and readiness reporting.

## MUST
- Governance MUST define accountable business and technical owners for critical capabilities and continuity risks.
- Material gaps against approved recovery objectives MUST be escalated with impact, evidence, options, cost, and recommended treatment.
- Risk acceptance for significant continuity exposure MUST be explicit, time-bounded, owned by an authority able to accept the business impact, and periodically reviewed.
- Executives MUST receive reporting that distinguishes demonstrated capability, planned capability, assumptions, and unresolved gaps.
- Decisions to materially reduce resilience, defer critical remediation, or accept unrecoverable scenarios MUST be documented with rationale and approval.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT allow a technical team to silently accept business-level continuity risk outside its authority.
- MUST NOT report unresolved material gaps as closed without verification evidence.
- MUST NOT use continuity governance solely as a documentation exercise disconnected from investment and operational decisions.

## SHOULD
- Review continuity posture alongside strategic, operational, security, supplier, and financial risk.
- Prefer remediation that reduces systemic and correlated risk across multiple critical capabilities.

## Exceptions
Emergency governance may use delegated authority defined in advance; decisions MUST be recorded and reviewed by normal governance after stabilization.

## Verification
Review governance charters, ownership matrices, executive reports, risk acceptances, investment decisions, remediation evidence, and crisis authority records.
