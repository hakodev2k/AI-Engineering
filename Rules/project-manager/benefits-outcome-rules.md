# Benefits and Outcome Rules
## Purpose
Keep project decisions tied to intended business outcomes rather than delivery activity alone.
## Scope
Benefits, outcome measures, value assumptions, adoption, and post-delivery ownership.
## MUST
- Define intended outcomes, measurable indicators, baseline or comparison basis, benefit owner, and expected realization timing.
- Revisit the business rationale when cost, scope, risk, or expected benefit materially changes.
- Distinguish project outputs from realized outcomes.
- Transfer benefit measurement ownership when realization continues after project closure.
## MUST NOT
- Claim project success solely from on-time or on-budget delivery when outcome criteria were defined.
- Continue materially changed work without reassessing whether expected value still justifies it.
## SHOULD
- Use leading adoption or behavior signals where final benefits take time to emerge.
## Exceptions
Compliance or mandatory projects may prioritize obligation fulfillment, but required outcomes and acceptance still need definition.
## Verification
Review outcome definitions, baselines, measures, owner, benefit forecast, adoption evidence, and post-project tracking.
