# Scrum Accountability Rules

## Purpose
Protect the Scrum Master accountability as an enabler of team effectiveness rather than a substitute manager or administrator.

## Scope
Scrum Teams, stakeholders, delivery organizations, and any facilitation performed in the Scrum Master role.

## MUST
- Coach the team and organization toward empiricism, self-management, and continuous improvement.
- Make impediments, systemic constraints, and unresolved risks visible to the people able to act on them.
- Distinguish facilitation, coaching, teaching, and organizational change work from product or people-management authority.
- Preserve accountability boundaries among Product Owner, Developers, Scrum Master, and organizational leaders.

## MUST NOT
- Assign work to Developers as a command-and-control manager.
- Make product-priority decisions on behalf of the Product Owner without explicit delegated authority.
- Become the permanent owner of routine team administration that the team can self-manage.

## SHOULD
- Prefer interventions that increase the team's capability to solve future problems independently.

## Exceptions
Temporary direct coordination is acceptable during incidents or severe dysfunction when authority, duration, risk, and hand-back are explicit.

## Verification
Review decisions, meeting ownership, work assignment patterns, and team feedback for evidence that accountability boundaries and self-management are preserved.
