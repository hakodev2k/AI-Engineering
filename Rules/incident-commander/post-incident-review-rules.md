# Post-Incident Review Rules

## Purpose
Convert incident evidence into durable reliability improvements without blame or hindsight distortion.

## Scope
Applies to incidents selected for formal or lightweight review after stabilization.

## MUST
- Reconstruct the timeline from contemporaneous evidence rather than memory alone.
- Separate triggering events, contributing conditions, detection gaps, response gaps, and recovery factors.
- Identify systemic controls that can prevent recurrence or reduce impact, not only individual mistakes.
- Assign follow-up actions to named owners with measurable completion criteria.
- Prioritize actions by expected risk reduction, not by ease of implementation alone.
- Review whether incident command, escalation, observability, communication, and change controls functioned as intended.

## MUST NOT
- Use hindsight to claim responders should have known facts unavailable at decision time.
- Close the review with vague actions such as "be more careful" or "monitor better".
- Suppress material contributing factors because they cross team or organizational boundaries.

## SHOULD
- Include evidence showing which mitigations worked, failed, or created secondary effects.
- Track recurring patterns across incidents and escalate systemic risk.

## Exceptions
Minor incidents may use a lightweight review when learning value and risk are low, but material unresolved risks still require tracked actions.

## Verification
Inspect the review for evidence-backed chronology, contributing factors, concrete action items, owners, verification criteria, and linkage to recurring incident themes.
