# Responder Fatigue Rules

## Purpose
Reduce decision errors and unsafe execution caused by prolonged incident response.

## Scope
Applies to sustained, high-severity, overnight, or cognitively demanding incidents.

## MUST
- Monitor duration and responder workload during prolonged incidents.
- Rotate commanders and critical technical owners before fatigue materially degrades judgment.
- Ensure handoffs preserve context before rotations.
- Escalate staffing shortages when safe continuity cannot be maintained.
- Treat repeated execution mistakes, confusion, or slowing decision quality as signals to add relief.

## MUST NOT
- Depend indefinitely on a single exhausted expert when qualified relief can be arranged.
- Shame responders for requesting rotation or rest.
- Allow fatigue to justify bypassing critical safety controls.

## SHOULD
- Maintain backup responders for critical operational domains.
- Use deputies and scribes to reduce cognitive load on the commander.

## Exceptions
Short emergency windows may require temporary workload concentration, but relief planning must begin immediately for extended response.

## Verification
Review incident duration, role rotations, staffing escalations, handoff quality, and whether fatigue-related errors occurred during the response.
