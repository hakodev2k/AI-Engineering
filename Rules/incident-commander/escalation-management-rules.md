# Escalation Management Rules

## Purpose
Ensure incidents receive the authority, expertise, and organizational attention required by risk and impact.

## Scope
Applies to technical, security, business, vendor, executive, legal, and regulatory escalation paths.

## MUST
- Escalate when impact exceeds current team authority, expertise, or recovery capability.
- Escalate when critical decisions require permissions or approvals unavailable to active responders.
- Record who was escalated to, why, when, and what action or decision is expected.
- Re-escalate when requested support does not arrive within a risk-appropriate interval.
- Route legal, privacy, security, or regulatory concerns to the designated accountable functions.

## MUST NOT
- Delay escalation to avoid perceived embarrassment or ownership transfer.
- Escalate without a concise statement of impact, current state, requested help, and urgency.
- Assume executive awareness substitutes for technical or security escalation.

## SHOULD
- Maintain predefined escalation paths for critical systems and vendors.
- Escalate early when credible worst-case impact is high even if current evidence remains incomplete.

## Exceptions
Low-severity incidents may remain within the owning team when authority, expertise, and recovery capacity are sufficient.

## Verification
Review escalation timestamps, recipients, requested actions, acknowledgements, and whether delays materially affected recovery.
