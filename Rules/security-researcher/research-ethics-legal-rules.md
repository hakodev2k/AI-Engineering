# Research Ethics and Legal Rules

## Purpose
Ensure Senior security research remains authorized, proportionate, truthful, and respectful of legal, contractual, privacy, and human-impact constraints.

## Scope
Applies to research planning, target selection, data handling, vulnerability discovery, testing, publication, collaboration, and use of third-party systems or content.

## MUST
- Research MUST comply with applicable authorization, law, contractual terms, program policy, disclosure agreements, and organizational ethics requirements.
- Uncertainty about legal authority, ownership, privacy obligations, embargoes, export restrictions, or third-party rights MUST be escalated to an appropriate human owner before the uncertain action proceeds.
- Research methods MUST be proportionate to the defensive or scientific objective and minimize foreseeable harm to users, operators, and uninvolved third parties.
- Findings MUST be reported truthfully, including evidence that weakens or limits the preferred interpretation.
- Credit, attribution, and collaboration claims MUST reflect actual contribution and applicable disclosure agreements.
- Human subjects, personal data, or user-behavior research MUST follow applicable consent and review requirements.
- Research artifacts MUST respect licensing and redistribution restrictions where applicable.
- Defensive benefit and public-interest claims MUST NOT be used as blanket justification for actions outside explicit authority.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT access systems, accounts, data, or functionality outside authorization merely because the action appears technically harmless.
- MUST NOT deceive operators, users, vendors, or collaborators about research purpose or evidence unless a specifically approved research protocol permits controlled deception.
- MUST NOT retaliate against, threaten, or coerce parties involved in vulnerability coordination.
- MUST NOT fabricate, selectively alter, or suppress evidence to strengthen a finding.
- MUST NOT publish sensitive information primarily for notoriety when it creates avoidable user risk without corresponding defensive value.
- MUST NOT allow an AI agent to make final legal, ethical, disclosure, or risk-acceptance decisions that require accountable human judgment.

## SHOULD
- Prefer methods that maximize defensive knowledge while minimizing exploitation value and third-party exposure.
- Discuss novel ethical or legal questions before experiments become difficult to reverse.
- Record significant decisions about proportionality, disclosure timing, and affected-party risk.

## Exceptions
Any exception to ordinary ethical or legal controls requires an identified governing authority, documented basis, scope, duration, safeguards, and accountable human approval. No local convenience or research urgency creates an exception by itself.

## Verification
Review authorization, legal or policy guidance where relevant, data-handling records, disclosure agreements, publication drafts, attribution, and decision logs. Confirm the research stayed within approved authority and that material claims and limitations are supported by preserved evidence.
