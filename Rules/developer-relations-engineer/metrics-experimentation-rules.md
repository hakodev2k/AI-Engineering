# Metrics and Experimentation Rules

## Purpose
Make Developer Relations decisions measurable without optimizing for vanity metrics or misleading attribution.

## Scope
Applies to program KPIs, campaigns, content performance, activation funnels, experiments, and impact reporting.

## MUST
- Each material program MUST define metrics that correspond to its intended developer outcome.
- Metric definitions, time windows, segmentation, and attribution assumptions MUST be documented.
- Experiments MUST define a hypothesis, success criterion, observation period, and known confounders before interpreting results.
- Reported performance changes MUST distinguish correlation from demonstrated causation.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT claim program impact from impressions alone when the objective is adoption, retention, activation, or developer success.
- MUST NOT selectively omit materially negative results from decision reviews.
- MUST NOT alter metric definitions after results are known without documenting the change.

## SHOULD
- Metrics SHOULD combine quantitative signals with qualitative developer evidence.
- Long-running programs SHOULD monitor unintended effects such as support burden or low-quality activation.

## Exceptions
Early exploratory work may use directional metrics if their limitations are explicit.

## Verification
Inspect metric definitions, dashboards, experiment plans, source data, attribution logic, and decision records.
