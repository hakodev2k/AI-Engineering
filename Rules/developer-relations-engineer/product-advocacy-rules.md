# Product Advocacy Rules

## Purpose
Represent developer needs inside product decisions without distorting evidence or bypassing product ownership.

## Scope
Applies to roadmap input, design reviews, API feedback, launch readiness, and developer-experience recommendations.

## MUST
- Advocacy MUST connect recommendations to documented developer problems, impact, and evidence.
- Trade-offs involving compatibility, security, reliability, cost, or support burden MUST be surfaced explicitly.
- Public developer promises MUST align with approved product commitments.
- Material API or workflow concerns MUST be raised before launch when discovered in time to affect the decision.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT present personal preference as community consensus.
- MUST NOT bypass accountable product or engineering owners to force a roadmap outcome.
- MUST NOT suppress negative developer feedback to protect launch messaging.

## SHOULD
- Recommendations SHOULD include alternatives and migration implications.
- Advocacy SHOULD prioritize durable developer outcomes over short-term promotional convenience.

## Exceptions
Urgent risk escalation may bypass normal sequencing when user safety, security, or severe breakage is involved, but accountable owners must be notified.

## Verification
Review evidence links, decision records, trade-off notes, launch feedback, and whether public statements match approved commitments.
