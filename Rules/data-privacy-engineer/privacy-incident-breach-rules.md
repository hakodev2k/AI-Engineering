# Privacy Incident and Breach Rules

## Purpose
Ensure suspected privacy incidents are contained, investigated, and evidenced without delaying escalation.

## Scope
Applies to unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, corruption, misrouting, overcollection, retention failures, and privacy-control bypasses.

## MUST
- Suspected incidents MUST preserve relevant evidence while minimizing further exposure.
- Incident handling MUST identify affected data categories, subjects, systems, time window, access paths, and containment status.
- Material incidents MUST be escalated according to organizational security, privacy, legal, and regulatory procedures.
- Containment actions MUST consider whether they create additional data loss or destroy forensic evidence.
- Corrective actions MUST address the bounded root cause or documented contributing conditions.

## MUST NOT
- Teams MUST NOT delay escalation until complete certainty is available when material exposure is plausible.
- Incident evidence MUST NOT be copied into insecure collaboration channels.
- Logs or records MUST NOT be altered to make the event appear less severe.

## SHOULD
- Investigation SHOULD use logs, access records, configuration history, traces, and data-flow evidence rather than assumptions.
- Post-incident improvements SHOULD include preventive and detective controls.

## Exceptions
Any deviation from standard incident handling requires documented necessity and accountable incident/privacy leadership approval.

## Verification
Review incident timelines, evidence inventories, containment records, access logs, notifications, root-cause analysis, and remediation verification.
