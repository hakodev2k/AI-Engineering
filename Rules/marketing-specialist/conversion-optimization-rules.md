# Conversion Optimization Rules

## Purpose
Improve conversion outcomes without sacrificing measurement integrity, customer trust, or downstream quality.

## Scope
Applies to landing pages, forms, offers, checkout or signup flows, and conversion-focused journeys.

## MUST
- Optimization work MUST define the target conversion, baseline, guardrails, and downstream quality metric where relevant.
- Material changes MUST be measured against an appropriate comparison or experiment design.
- Form or journey changes MUST preserve required consent, disclosure, and accessibility behavior.
- Gains MUST be assessed for durability and downstream impact before broad rollout.

## MUST NOT
- MUST NOT use deceptive patterns, hidden costs, false scarcity, or misleading defaults.
- MUST NOT optimize conversion by degrading lead or customer quality without disclosure.
- MUST NOT claim improvement without comparable before/after evidence.

## SHOULD
- Optimization SHOULD prioritize high-friction, high-volume, or high-value stages supported by evidence.
- Tests SHOULD include guardrails for complaints, abandonment, and quality.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require rationale, customer-impact assessment, risk, owner, and approval.

## Verification
Review experiment plans, analytics, journey recordings or QA evidence, disclosures, and downstream quality metrics.
