# Prioritization and Discovery
Use evidence proportional to decision cost. For low-cost reversible changes, lightweight evidence can be enough. For expensive or irreversible choices, require stronger validation.

Priority review should consider: user/business impact, severity, strategic alignment, deadline, dependency unblock, opportunity cost, delivery effort, uncertainty, risk and reversibility. Scores such as RICE/WSJF may inform but never replace judgment.

Discovery loop: frame problem -> list assumptions -> identify riskiest assumption -> choose cheapest evidence -> learn -> update decision. Stop when evidence is sufficient for the next reversible decision, not when every uncertainty is eliminated.

Avoid vanity metrics. Prefer behavioral/product metrics connected to the intended outcome, with guardrails for quality, reliability, support load and unintended harm.
