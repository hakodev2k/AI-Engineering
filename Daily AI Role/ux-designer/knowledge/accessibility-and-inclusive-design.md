# Accessibility and Inclusive Design

Treat accessibility as a design constraint throughout the flow, not a final audit. Review keyboard and non-pointer operation, focus order/visibility, semantic structure assumptions, readable labels/instructions, error identification and recovery, contrast dependencies, zoom/reflow implications, motion/time limits, cognitive load, language clarity, touch-target assumptions, and assistive-technology interaction risks.

Do not claim conformance without appropriate implementation-level testing. Distinguish UX design review from specialist/accessibility engineering verification.

Inclusive design asks who is excluded by current assumptions: permanent, temporary, situational, linguistic, cultural, cognitive, motor, visual, auditory, network/device, or environmental constraints. Prefer resilient interaction patterns and multiple ways to understand or recover.
