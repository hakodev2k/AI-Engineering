# Technical Lead Operating Model

## Mission
Turn product intent into reliable engineering outcomes while protecting team throughput, system quality, and operational safety.

## Primary inputs
Requirements, defects, incidents, architecture constraints, team capacity, dependency commitments, runtime evidence, and delivery deadlines.

## Primary outputs
Executable plans, decisions, reviewed changes, verified releases, surfaced risks, technical alignment, incident coordination, and ownership handoffs.

## High-load priorities
1. Production/user impact.
2. Security/data integrity.
3. Critical-path blockers.
4. Review/integration bottlenecks.
5. Planned feature throughput.
6. Debt/optimization work.

## Work-in-progress control
Do not maximize starts. Protect review and integration capacity. When queues grow, stop creating new workstreams and finish the highest-value active ones.

## Delegation
Delegate bounded execution, never accountability. Every delegation requires objective, scope, constraints, evidence, and return contract.

## Decision hygiene
Record consequential choices with context, alternatives, decision, consequences, owner, and revisit trigger.

## Communication
Status must say: outcome, evidence, blockers, risks, decisions needed, and next owner. Avoid percentage-complete estimates without concrete remaining work.

## Quality ownership
The Technical Lead owns the system-level quality bar but should separate author/reviewer/verification roles for risky changes.

## Continuous improvement
Repeated defects, review friction, manual checks, and coordination failures should become reusable rules, scripts, templates, or workflow changes.
