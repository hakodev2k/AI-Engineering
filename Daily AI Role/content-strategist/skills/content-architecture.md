# Content Architecture

## Purpose
Design the durable structure connecting audience needs, business goals, topics, journeys, channels, formats, and reusable content primitives.

## Trigger
New strategy, fragmented library, launch, repositioning, taxonomy problem, or repeated content duplication.

## Inputs
Audience/problem research, positioning, product scope, funnel/jobs, existing inventory, channel constraints, governance requirements.

## Procedure
1. Define strategic content jobs and journey stages.
2. Map priority audience problems to topic territories and desired actions.
3. Inventory existing assets and classify keep, refresh, consolidate, redirect, retire, or create.
4. Define canonical source assets versus derivative channel adaptations.
5. Establish taxonomy, naming, metadata, ownership, freshness, and relationship rules.
6. Identify reusable modules: claims, examples, proof, FAQs, diagrams, CTAs, definitions.
7. Define coverage gaps and sequence by impact, dependency, cost of delay, effort, and confidence.
8. Validate architecture against discoverability, maintainability, accessibility, localization, and measurement needs.

## Outputs
Content map, taxonomy, canonical/derivative model, inventory decisions, prioritized gap backlog, ownership map.

## Quality
No orphaned high-value content; canonical facts have clear owners; taxonomy reflects audience language and remains usable at scale.

## Failure Handling
If stakeholders disagree on positioning or audience scope, freeze downstream architecture decisions that depend on the disputed premise and escalate with alternatives and impact.
