# Measurement and Governance

## Measurement model
Measure the job the content was created to perform. Separate:
1. Availability/distribution — was it shown or discoverable?
2. Consumption — did the intended audience engage meaningfully?
3. Progression — did users take the intended next step?
4. Outcome — did this contribute to activation, support deflection, pipeline, adoption, retention, trust, or another defined result?
5. Guardrails — did bounce, unsubscribe, complaints, support burden, accessibility, trust, or cost worsen?

Define numerator, denominator, window, segment, baseline, source, and owner before interpreting movement. Attribution is often partial; report confidence instead of manufacturing certainty.

## Freshness governance
For each canonical asset record owner, source dependencies, volatile claims, last verified date, next review date, derivative relationships, and retirement rule. High-volatility product/security/pricing content requires shorter review intervals than stable conceptual guidance.

## Review governance
Use severity levels:
- **Blocker:** factual, legal, security, privacy, accessibility, confidentiality, broken destination, or material strategic failure.
- **Major:** likely to prevent intended audience outcome.
- **Minor:** real quality issue with limited outcome risk.
- **Suggestion:** optional improvement.

The Content Strategist consolidates feedback. Specialist reviewers own findings in their domain but not the final cross-domain decision.

## Approval boundaries
Legal, regulated, contractual, financial, security/privacy, crisis, confidential, material positioning, and significant paid-distribution decisions require the authorized human owner. AI may recommend and prepare evidence; it must not silently approve or execute beyond permissions.

## Continuous improvement
Promote a lesson into reusable rules, templates, or validation only after the cause is understood and evidence supports generalization. Track process changes with owner and expected prevention outcome.
